# Prior art for the Open Music Theory curriculum

This is a catalog of what other people have built for each topic in [open_music_theory_benchmark.md](open_music_theory_benchmark.md): instruments, product features, web experiments, explainer videos, and exercises on piano and guitar.
The aim is to find microworlds, things that embody one dimension of music concretely, so entries are chosen for how narrowly they isolate a topic and not for being canonical.

The topics are grouped by theme, not by the benchmark's tiers.
Each heading gives the tier the benchmark put the topic in (1 is explicit in Perfecto's UI, 4 is not represented).
Two headings are not Open Music Theory chapters and are here because they are the nearest neighbours of the app: the lineage of chord instruments, and tuning.

Each entry says what the thing is, then which dimension it isolates and how the hand or eye meets it.
Within a topic the order is instruments, product features, web experiments, videos, then exercises and techniques.

## How far to trust the links

The entries were gathered by web research on 2026-10-08.
Every link was either returned by a search or fetched and found to exist; most were not read in full, so descriptions and years often come from general knowledge and not from the linked page.
An entry marked "(link not verified)" is something known to exist for which no link could be confirmed, and it carries no link.
The search allowance ran out partway through, which fell hardest on YouTube: many channels worth citing (12tone, 8-bit Music Theory, Nahre Sol, Rick Beato and others) have few or no videos here, and some are named with no title.
A few video links go to an episode listing and not to YouTube, and say so.

## Where little was found

- **Chromatic harmony as technique** (augmented sixths, common-tone chords, the omnibus, elision): lectures and textbook pages only, nothing played.
- **Fragile, absent and emergent tonics:** scholarship and arguments about single songs; no instrument or app represents an uncertain tonic.
- **Tonicization:** one real microworld (Mapping Tonal Harmony Pro's tonic regions per degree).
- **Classical and "puff" schemas in pop:** nothing beyond Open Music Theory's own chapters.
- **Classical form and 20th-century rhythm:** listening guides and sequencers that happen to fit; nothing purpose-built.
- **Chord-scale theory:** nothing found lets a player choose a mode per chord as the chord grid's rows do.
- **Chord-following "no wrong notes" surfaces:** only the Omnichord and the autoharp follow the chord; everything else locks to a key.
- **Octave numbers and the blues scale:** thin, mostly naming conventions and items in scale lists.

## Pitch, scales and modes

### Half steps, accidentals, pitch names (tier 1)

Prior art here is mostly physical: instruments where one mechanical move is exactly one half step, or where an accidental is a separate control from the note it alters.
The explainers tend to fixate on the one irregularity of the piano, the missing black keys between B and C and between E and F.

- **Guitar fretboard** (instrument feature, centuries old). [Fingerboard, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingerboard)
  Each fret shortens the string by one equal-tempered semitone.
  It isolates the half step as a unit of distance: one fret is one step, with no black and white distinction to hide the count.
- **Capo** (guitar accessory). [Capo, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capo)
  A clamp across all strings at one fret.
  It makes "up N half steps" a single physical placement, so transposition is counted in frets by the hand.
- **Chromatic harmonica slide** (instrument, Hohner and others, 1920s on). [Chromatic harmonica, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_harmonica)
  A diatonic layout with a spring-loaded button that raises whatever note is being played by a semitone.
  It isolates the accidental as a modifier: the mouth chooses the letter and the thumb adds the sharp.
- **Pedal harp pedals** (instrument, Érard double action, 1810). [Pedal harp, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedal_harp)
  Seven pedals, one per letter name, each with three positions for flat, natural and sharp.
  It is the purest embodiment of the accidental: a letter name and its alteration are separate controls, and the feet set the alteration for every octave at once.
- **Pitch Properties of the Pedal Harp, with an Interactive Guide** (web explainer in a journal, Mark Gotham, Music Theory Online 22.4, 2016). [MTO article](https://www.mtosmt.org/ojs/index.php/mto/article/view/155/128)
  A paper with an interactive pedal diagram showing which pitch collections each pedal setting gives.
  It lets the reader flip each letter between flat, natural and sharp and see what scale results.
- **Dodeka keyboard** (hardware keyboard, Jacques-Daniel Rochat). [Dodeka keyboard](https://dodekamusic.com/products/dodeka-keyboard/)
  A single row of identical keys, one per chromatic note, with C, E and A flat marked as landmarks.
  It removes the diatonic bias of the piano so that every adjacent key is one half step and a whole step is always two keys.
- **Boomwhackers chromatic set** (classroom instrument, 1995 on). [Boomwhackers](https://boomwhackers.com/)
  Plastic tubes cut to length, each labelled with its note name and given a fixed colour.
  It makes a pitch name a physical object you hold, and the sharps and flats are literally an add-on set.
- **Steps and Accidentals** (web lesson, musictheory.net). [Lesson](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/20)
  A click-through animated lesson on half steps, whole steps and the five accidentals, shown on a keyboard.
  It isolates the definition itself: one step at a time, one key lights and the name changes.
- **Keyboard Note Identification** (web exercise, musictheory.net). [Exercise](https://www.musictheory.net/exercises/keyboard)
  A drill that highlights one piano key and asks for its name.
  It trains only the mapping from key position to pitch name, including which enharmonic names are accepted.
- **Chromatic Scale: Visualize Intervals and Triads** (web tool, muted.io). [muted.io chromatic scale](https://muted.io/chromatic-scale/)
  A circular view of the twelve notes that lights intervals and triads from any chosen note.
  It shows the twelve half steps as equal slices of a circle instead of as unequal keys.
- **Why is there no B# or E# note on the piano?** (video, David Bennett Music Theory). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lN54Y73LlLo)
  An explainer on why the keyboard has two places with no black key.
  It isolates the mismatch between seven letter names and twelve half steps.
- **Why is there no B sharp?** (video, Conceptual Academy). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fShQWXTbp6o)
  A basics video covering pitch, notes, sharps, flats, overtones and scales.
  It approaches the same gap from the physics side.
- **Learn music theory in half an hour.** (video, Andrew Huang). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rgaTLrZGlk0)
  A fast survey that starts from the twelve notes and counts half steps to build everything else.
  It treats the half step as the only primitive and derives scales and chords by counting.
- **One-finger-per-fret chromatic warm-up, and the piano chromatic fingering with the third finger on black keys** (guitar and piano exercises). (link not verified)
  Standard technical drills that walk every half step in order.
  They put the chromatic scale into the hand as an even, unaccented sequence.

### Octave numbers / ASPN (tier 1)

There is little that teaches octave numbering as such.
Prior art is mostly naming conventions and hardware controls that move by octaves, plus one well-known inconsistency about which octave middle C is in.

- **Organ stop footages** (instrument convention, centuries old). [Eight-foot pitch, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-foot_pitch)
  Organ stops and synth oscillators are labelled 16', 8', 4' and 2' after pipe lengths.
  It isolates the octave as a halving of length, and the player selects a register by pulling a stop instead of moving the hands.
- **Register key and octave key** (woodwind mechanism). [Register key, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Register_key)
  A thumb key that makes the same fingering sound in a higher register.
  It separates "which note" (the fingers) from "which register" (the thumb), the same split as an octave control beside a set of keys.
- **Monochord** (demonstration instrument, antiquity). [Monochord, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monochord)
  One string with a movable bridge.
  Stopping the string at its midpoint gives the octave, which is the same fact as the guitar's double dot at the twelfth fret.
- **Scientific pitch notation** (naming convention). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_pitch_notation)
  The C4 system in which the number changes at every C.
  It is the direct reference for an OCTAVE control that shows a number.
- **Helmholtz pitch notation** (older naming convention, 1860s). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmholtz_pitch_notation)
  Octaves marked by letter case and primes, as in c' for middle C.
  It is a rival answer to the same question and shows that the numbering is a convention, not a fact.
- **The C3 versus C4 middle C dispute** (product inconsistency across MIDI software and hardware). [webmidi issue 42, "Wrong octave system-wide"](https://github.com/djipco/webmidi/issues/42)
  MIDI fixes middle C as note 60 but manufacturers label it C3, C4 or C5.
  It is a small real-world case study in how an octave number on screen can disagree with another device.
- **Piano key frequencies table** (reference). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_key_frequencies)
  Every one of the 88 keys with its key number, scientific name and frequency.
  It lines up three numberings of the same pitches side by side.
- **Octave shift buttons on short MIDI keyboards, and octave shapes on guitar** (product feature and guitar technique). (link not verified)
  Two-octave controllers reach the full range through plus and minus octave buttons, and guitarists learn the two fixed shapes that find the same note an octave up.
  Both treat the octave as a jump that leaves the note name alone.

### Pitch and pitch class; integer notation (tier 2)

Prior art splits into colour systems that give each pitch class a hue, circular diagrams that fold the octaves onto a clock face, and tools that treat a set of pitch classes as a number.
The Shepard tone is the one true auditory microworld: a sound with pitch class and no octave.

- **Boomwhackers Chroma-Notes colours** (classroom instrument). [Boomwhacker, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boomwhacker)
  Each note name has one colour, and notation software such as Finale has a notehead colour scheme matched to it.
  It makes pitch class a colour and octave a tube length, so the two are seen as separate properties.
- **Clavier à lumières** (colour organ, Alexander Scriabin, 1910). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clavier_%C3%A0_lumi%C3%A8res)
  A keyboard written into the score of Prometheus that projects a colour for each note.
  It maps pitch class to colour for an audience, with the colours ordered by fifths.
- **A Study of Scales** (web reference, Ian Ring). [ianring.com](https://ianring.com/musictheory/scales/)
  Every possible set of the twelve pitch classes, each given a number from 0 to 4095 by treating the notes as bits.
  It is integer notation taken to its end: the major scale is 2741, and each scale gets a page with a clock diagram and its properties.
- **Chromatone** (web site). [Study of scales page](https://chromatone.center/theory/scales/study/)
  A colour-per-pitch-class theory site that walks through the same binary scale numbering.
  It joins the colour mapping and the integer mapping in one place.
- **PC Set Calculator** (web tool, Jay Tomlin). [Help page](https://www.jaytomlin.com/music/settheory/help.html)
  A calculator that takes pitch classes as integers and returns normal form, prime form and interval vector.
  It is the working tool of integer notation, clicked on a clock face.
- **Shepard tone demo** (web experiment, Michael Bach). [michaelbach.de](https://michaelbach.de/ot/aud-ShepardTone/)
  An endlessly rising scale built from tones that sound in all octaves at once, with controls for the number of voices.
  It isolates pitch class by removing octave from the sound itself.
- **Shepard tone** (web toy, Optical Toys). [optical.toys](https://optical.toys/shepard-tone/)
  A one-click version of the same illusion with an explanation.
  It is the shortest route to hearing that pitch height and pitch class are separable.
- **Tritone paradox demo** (web experiment, University of British Columbia). [cs.ubc.ca](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/imager/contributions/flinn/Illusions/TT/tt.html)
  Two Shepard tones half an octave apart, which different listeners hear as rising or falling.
  It shows that with octave removed, direction around the pitch-class circle is ambiguous at six half steps.
- **Theory aQWERTYon pitch wheels** (web app, NYU MusEDLab, 2019). [Ethan Hein's write-up](https://ethanhein.substack.com/p/the-aqwertyon-pitch-wheels-and-the-future-of-music-theory-visualization)
  A computer-keyboard instrument that lights the notes being played on a chromatic circle in real time.
  It shows every note played as a pitch class on a clock while the hands play in several octaves.
- **Chromatic circle and pitch constellation** (diagram conventions). [Chromatic circle, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_circle)
  The twelve pitch classes on a clock face, with a scale or chord drawn as a polygon.
  It is the standard picture that most of the tools above animate.
- **ChordGeometries** (desktop software, Dmitri Tymoczko). [Download page](https://dmitri.mycpanel.princeton.edu/ChordGeometries.html)
  A program that plots chords as points in the geometric spaces of voice leading.
  It extends the pitch-class circle to chords, so a chord is a location and a progression is a path.
- **Twelve-tone matrix calculator** (app feature, Tenuto by musictheory.net). [Tenuto](https://www.musictheory.net/buy/tenuto)
  One of six calculators in the app, beside ones for accidentals, intervals, scales and chords.
  It is integer notation as a grid exercise.

### Enharmonic equivalence and spelling (tier 2)

The instruments here are the historical ones that refused equivalence and gave D sharp and E flat separate keys.
The videos argue about whether two names are "really" the same note, which is the question a spelling function has to answer.

- **Split sharps** (keyboard feature, 15th to 18th century). [Split sharp, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split_sharp)
  Black keys divided front and back, most often for E flat and D sharp and for G sharp and A flat.
  It makes the spelling a choice of the finger: the two names are two keys with two pitches.
- **Archicembalo** (harpsichord, Nicola Vicentino, 1555). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archicembalo)
  A two-manual instrument with many more than twelve keys to the octave.
  It is the extreme case of a keyboard that keeps every enharmonic spelling distinct.
- **Enharmonic keyboards and the generalized keyboard** (keyboard family, including Bosanquet's of the 1870s). [Enharmonic keyboard, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enharmonic_keyboard)
  Keyboards with more than twelve keys per octave where differently spelled notes differ in pitch.
  They show spelling as geometry: a sharp lies in one direction and a flat in the other.
- **Lumatone** (hardware controller, 2019 on). [lumatone.io](https://www.lumatone.io/)
  280 lit hexagonal keys that can be mapped to tunings beyond twelve notes.
  In a mapping such as 31 notes to the octave, C sharp and D flat become different keys with different colours.
- **Pedal harp enharmonics** (instrument technique). [Pedal harp, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedal_harp)
  Harpists set two adjacent strings to the same pitch, or respell G flat as F sharp so that it can sound against G natural.
  It is spelling with physical consequences: the name decides which string plays.
- **Is Cb the same note as B?** (video, Adam Neely). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SZftrA-aCa4)
  An argument about when two names for one key are and are not the same note.
  It isolates the question of spelling from the question of pitch.
- **Why the Notes E# B# Cb and Fb Really Do Exist** (video, Music Matters). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FDzw1jByVDA)
  A lesson on why scales need the "white key" sharps and flats.
  It teaches the rule that each letter appears once in a scale, which is the rule a spelling function implements.
- **Enharmonic Equivalents** (video, Music Matters). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5aDP7u78EIU)
  A plain definition with keyboard examples.
  It covers the equivalence side where the previous video covers the spelling side.
- **Enharmonic Equivalents: What You Need to Know** (video, MusicTheoryAcademy). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hLJ4YLFT_g8)
  A short reference video on the pairs of names.
  It is the list-of-pairs treatment.
- **Why It's Impossible to Tune a Piano** (video, minutephysics). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Hqm0dYKUx4)
  An explanation of why stacked pure intervals do not close into octaves.
  It shows why G sharp and A flat were once different pitches and what equal temperament gave up to merge them.
- **"Each letter once" scale spelling, and the respell commands in notation programs** (theory rule and product feature). (link not verified)
  The classroom rule for spelling a scale, and the menu command that flips a note between its names.
  Both treat spelling as a function of key, applied after the pitch is known.

### The piano keyboard, and alternative/isomorphic key layouts (tier 3)

This is the richest field.
Almost every alternative layout exists to make one thing true that the piano does not: the same shape plays the same interval, chord or scale in every key.

- **Jankó keyboard** (piano keyboard, Paul von Jankó, 1882). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jank%C3%B3_keyboard)
  Six rows of keys in two interleaved whole-tone rows, each note playable in three places.
  It isolates transposition: one fingering serves all twelve keys, and the hand spans more.
- **Pianino with Jankó and normal keyboard, pivoting** (museum instrument, 1908 to 1910). [Historisches Museum Basel](https://www.hmb.ch/en/museums/eguides/a/e/pianino-with-janko-and-normal-keyboard-pivoting-1908-1910)
  An upright with both keyboards on a pivot.
  It is the comparison built as one object.
- **Chromatone** (Jankó-style synthesizer keyboard, Japan). [chromatone.jp](https://chromatone.jp/)
  A current commercial instrument on the whole-tone row layout.
  It is the Jankó idea still on sale.
- **Wicki-Hayden layout** (button layout, Kaspar Wicki 1896, Brian Hayden 1986). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicki%E2%80%93Hayden_note_layout)
  Hexagonal buttons with whole steps along a row and fourths and fifths between rows, first made for bandoneon and concertina.
  A major scale is one compact cluster, and its shape is the same in every key.
- **Striso board** (hardware controller, Piers Titus van der Torren). [striso.org](https://www.striso.org/)
  A small expressive instrument with a Wicki-Hayden-inspired layout tilted so that pitch rises in one clear direction.
  It adds a visible pitch-height axis to an isomorphic grid.
- **Harmonic table and the AXiS-49** (hardware controller, C-Thru Music, 2008). [Sound On Sound review](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/c-thru-music-axis-49)
  98 hexagonal keys where straight up is a fifth and the two diagonals are major and minor thirds.
  A triad is a triangle under three fingers, pointing one way for major and the other for minor.
- **MIDIHEX** (DIY harmonic table controller). [PJRC project page](https://www.pjrc.com/midihex-harmonic-table-midi-device/)
  A home-built revival of the AXiS layout.
  It shows the following the layout kept after the company closed.
- **Lumatone** (hardware controller, 2019 on). [lumatone.io](https://www.lumatone.io/)
  280 hexagonal keys with a colour per key and remappable layouts.
  It is the layout as a variable: the same surface becomes Wicki-Hayden, harmonic table or a microtonal map.
- **LinnStrument fourths layout** (hardware controller, Roger Linn Design, 2014). [Why the fourths string layout](https://rogerlinndesign.com/support/support-linnstrument-fourths-layout)
  Rows of semitones tuned a fourth apart like an eight-string bass, with the naturals lit and every C a different colour.
  It keeps the guitar's regularity without the guitar's one odd string, and uses lights to restore the piano's landmarks.
- **Harpejji** (electric tapped string instrument, Tim Meeks, Marcodi, 2007). [What is a harpejji?](https://www.marcodi.com/blogs/news/what-is-a-harpejji)
  Strings a whole tone apart with semitone frets, played flat with both hands, and marked in a black and white pattern.
  It is an isomorphic layout disguised as a piano.
- **Exquis** (hardware controller, Intuitive Instruments, 2023). [Intuitive Instruments](https://dualo.com/en/welcome/)
  61 lit hexagonal keys with thirds stacked upward and semitones across, descended from the Dualo layout.
  Chords are vertical shapes and the chosen scale is a lit path through the grid.
- **Dodeka keyboard** (hardware keyboard). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodeka_keyboard)
  One row of twelve equal keys per octave, paired with its own four-line chromatic notation.
  It is the one-dimensional isomorphic layout.
- **Chromatic button accordion, B-system and C-system** (instrument, 19th century on). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_button_accordion)
  Three to five rows of buttons where each diagonal is a minor third and patterns repeat across the board.
  It is the most widely played isomorphic keyboard, with a living argument over which mirror image is better.
- **Chapman Stick** (tapped string instrument, Emmett Chapman, 1974). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapman_Stick)
  A fretboard tapped with both hands, melody strings in fourths and bass strings in fifths.
  It puts two interval grids under two hands.
- **Push 64-pad note layout** (product feature, Ableton). [Using Push 2, Live manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/using-push-2/)
  An 8 by 8 grid where each row is a fourth above the one below.
  It brought the fourths grid to a mass audience, with a chromatic mode and a folded in-key mode on the same pads.
- **Musix Pro** (iOS app, Shiverware). [Layouts page](https://shiverware.com/musixpro/layouts.html)
  A hexagonal or rectangular touch keyboard with Wicki-Hayden, Jankó, harmonic table and LinnStrument presets and a layout builder.
  It lets one pair of hands compare the layouts directly.
- **GeoShred** (iOS app, moForte and Wizdom Music). [moForte](https://www.moforte.com/geoShredApp/)
  A touch surface of rows tuned like strings with a physically modelled guitar behind it.
  It is the fourths grid as an expressive touch instrument.
- **RelayerForAll** (software, Dynamic Tonality). [dynamictonality.com](https://dynamictonality.com/relayer.htm)
  A utility for mapping isomorphic layouts onto controllers.
  It treats layout as a mapping separate from the hardware.
- **Hexagonal Keyboard Layout Survey** (blog post, Jeff Kaufman). [jefftk.com](https://jefftk.com/p/hexagonal-keyboard-layout-survey)
  A comparison of the hexagonal layouts by which intervals lie on which axes.
  It is a compact map of the design space.
- **Why are there Black and White Keys on the Piano?** (video, LivingPianosVideos). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hW9vS3i9yfg)
  A piano dealer's explanation of the layout.
  It argues for the irregular pattern as what makes the keyboard navigable.
- **Intro to Piano Keyboard Layout: Black Key Groups and White Key Names** (video, Beth Tomlin Piano). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pbsvz1hFCxI)
  The first-lesson method of finding notes from the groups of two and three black keys.
  It is the landmark technique that every lit isomorphic grid has to replace.
- **All-fourths and major-thirds guitar tunings** (guitar technique). [All fourths tuning, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_fourths_tuning)
  Retuning the guitar so every pair of strings is the same interval apart.
  It is the guitarist's own route to an isomorphic layout, at the cost of the familiar chord shapes.

### Intervals (tier 2)

Intervals are embodied either as a direction on a grid, as a ratio of string lengths, or as a remembered tune.
The ear-training tools are many and nearly identical; the grids and the mnemonics are the distinctive part.

- **Harmonic table layout** (button layout). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_table_note_layout)
  A hexagonal layout whose three axes are the fifth, the major third and the minor third.
  It makes an interval a direction, so the hand learns a major third as "up and to the right".
- **Tonnetz** (lattice diagram, Euler 1739, later Riemann). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonnetz)
  A lattice of notes joined by fifths and thirds, with triads as triangles.
  It is the same idea as a picture, and the source of the hexagonal controllers.
- **TonnetzViz** (web experiment, Ondřej Cífka). [cifkao.github.io](https://cifkao.github.io/tonnetz-viz/)
  A browser Tonnetz that lights up from live MIDI or the computer keyboard.
  It lets a pianist see the interval geometry of what the hands already know.
- **Monochord** (demonstration instrument). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monochord)
  A single string divided by a movable bridge.
  It isolates the interval as a ratio of lengths: half for the octave, two thirds for the fifth.
- **Specific Intervals** (web lesson, musictheory.net). [Lesson](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/31)
  The animated lesson on counting half steps to name an interval's quality.
  It teaches interval size as a count on the keyboard.
- **Interval Calculator** (web tool, musictheory.net). [Calculator](https://www.musictheory.net/calculators/interval)
  Pick a note and an interval and see the result on a staff and keyboard.
  It is the smallest possible interval tool, one note in and one note out.
- **Interval Ear Training** (web exercise, musictheory.net). [Exercise](https://www.musictheory.net/exercises/ear-interval)
  Hear two notes and name the interval.
  It isolates the sound of each interval from any key.
- **Interval Ear Training** (web exercise, teoria.com). [Exercise](https://www.teoria.com/en/exercises/ie.php)
  The same drill with options for melodic or harmonic intervals and direction.
  It adds the distinction between an interval heard in sequence and one heard together.
- **Music Interval Ear Training** (web tool, muted.io). [muted.io intervals](https://muted.io/intervals/)
  An interval trainer played on an on-screen piano.
  It ties the heard interval back to a distance between two keys.
- **Interval Song Chart Generator** (web tool, EarMaster). [EarMaster](https://www.earmaster.com/products/free-tools/interval-song-chart-generator.html)
  Builds a personal chart of well-known songs that begin with each interval, up and down.
  It is the reference-song mnemonic turned into a tool.
- **Interval recognition by reference songs** (ear-training technique). [Interval recognition, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interval_recognition)
  The practice of remembering each interval by the opening of a familiar tune.
  It attaches each interval to a melody the learner can already sing.
- **Chrome Music Lab: Harmonics and Strings** (web experiments, Google). (link not verified)
  Two toys that show string lengths and the harmonic series as simple ratios.
  They give the monochord's lesson as something to pluck on a screen.
- **Why It's Impossible to Tune a Piano** (video, minutephysics). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Hqm0dYKUx4)
  An explanation of intervals as frequency ratios and why they cannot all be pure at once.
  It isolates the gap between the interval as a ratio and the interval as a number of half steps.
- **Music And Measure Theory** (video, 3Blue1Brown). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cyW5z-M2yzw)
  A mathematical look at which frequency ratios sound harmonious.
  It treats consonance as a property of numbers.
- **Scales in thirds, sixths and octaves** (piano exercise, Hanon, The Virtuoso Pianist, part III). [IMSLP](https://imslp.org/wiki/The_Virtuoso_Pianist_(Hanon,_Charles-Louis))
  Technical exercises that run a scale as a chain of one fixed interval.
  They put one interval size into the hand shape until it is automatic.

### Circle of fifths (tier 2)

The circle shows up as an instrument layout more often than people expect: accordion basses, steelpans and string tunings all put fifths side by side.
On the web it is the most cloned music theory widget there is, so the interesting examples are the ones that do something with it.

- **Stradella bass system** (accordion left-hand layout, 19th century). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stradella_bass_system)
  Columns of bass and chord buttons ordered by fifths, with major, minor, seventh and diminished chords in rows.
  It puts I, IV and V in three adjacent columns, so the hand feels key relationships as distance.
- **Fourths-and-fifths tenor steelpan** (instrument layout, Trinidad). [Hansen and Rossing, The Caribbean steelpan and some offsprings](https://www.hangblog.org/pdf/hansen_rossing_the-caribbean-steelpan-and-some-offsprings.pdf)
  The lead pan's notes are laid around the rim so each neighbour is a fifth away, with octaves on the inner rings.
  It is a literal playable circle of fifths, chosen partly because neighbouring notes then ring in sympathy.
- **Cycle of fifths steel pan** (patent, 2004). [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20040035283A1/en)
  A patent on a pan laid out strictly in the cycle of fifths.
  It documents the layout as a deliberate design.
- **Clavier à lumières** (colour organ, Scriabin, 1910). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clavier_%C3%A0_lumi%C3%A8res)
  Scriabin's note colours follow the circle of fifths, so the spectrum runs around it.
  It makes distance in fifths a distance in hue.
- **Camelot Wheel** (DJ key notation, Mark Davis, Mixed In Key, 2006). [Mixed In Key](https://mixedinkey.com/camelot-wheel/)
  The circle of fifths renumbered as a clock, 1 to 12, with A for minor and B for major.
  It strips the note names off entirely and leaves only adjacency: mix to the same number or one step either way.
- **The Chord Wheel** (cardboard tool, Jim Fleser, Hal Leonard). [Hal Leonard](https://www.halleonard.com/product/695579/the-chord-wheel)
  A printed circle of fifths with a rotating transparent overlay that outlines the chords of a key.
  It is the circle as a slide rule: turn the overlay and the key changes.
- **Interactive Circle of Fifths Chart** (web tool, muted.io). [muted.io](https://muted.io/circle-of-fifths/)
  A clickable circle that shows each key's notes and chords and toggles between the major and minor circles.
  It joins the circle to the scale and chords of the chosen key.
- **Interactive Circle of Fifths** (web tool, Rand Scullard). [randscullard.com](https://randscullard.com/CircleOfFifths/)
  A rotatable circle with key signatures and diatonic chords.
  It is one of the older and most linked versions, built around turning the wheel.
- **Circle of fifths chart** (web tool, ToneGym, 2022). [ToneGym](https://tonegym.co/tool/item?id=circle-of-fifths)
  An interactive chart for modulations, progressions and key signatures.
  It leans toward using the circle to move between keys.
- **Circle of fifths** (web tool, Musicca). [Musicca](https://www.musicca.com/circle-of-fifths)
  A plain interactive reference.
  It is the classroom version.
- **Chrome Music Lab: Arpeggios** (web experiment, Google). (link not verified)
  A wheel of major and minor chords that plays an arpeggio pattern on the chosen one.
  It turns the circle into a chord selector that is played, not read.
- **Interview: Jacob Collier (Part 1)** (video, June Lee). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DnBr070vcNE)
  A long theory interview with notation and diagrams added on screen.
  It is the best-known source for stacking fifths upward for brightness and downward for darkness.
- **Pythagorean Tuning: The Circle of Fifths, Note Ratios, The Pythagorean Comma and The Wolf Interval** (video, Serenity Music Tuition). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Icp5RitME0A)
  The circle built from pure fifths, and why it fails to close.
  It shows the circle as something tuned, not just drawn.
- **Circle Of Fifths On Guitar** (video, Chris Sherland Guitar). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fiAay-Ld_IM)
  The circle walked as seven-fret moves on the neck.
  It translates the diagram into a hand movement.
- **The Circle Of Fifths Is MUCH BETTER Like This!** (video, StichMethod Guitar). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PEcWZUnOUEY)
  An argument for a redrawn circle for guitarists.
  It is a sample of the many attempts to improve the diagram.
- **The Circle of Fifths: How to REALLY Use It (guitar and piano)** (video, Mike George). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SL_-gtCD2as)
  A practical tour of what the circle is for.
  It is a representative of the "how to use it" genre.
- **"Father Charles Goes Down And Ends Battle", and strings tuned in fifths** (mnemonic and instrument fact). (link not verified)
  The sentence for the order of sharps, reversed for flats, and the violin family's open strings.
  The mnemonic is the circle as words, and the open strings are four steps of it under the bow.

### Major scales and scale degrees (tier 1)

Diatonic instruments are the prior art: instruments that can only play one major scale, so the scale is the instrument.
On screens the equivalent is any surface that numbers or folds the notes by degree.

- **Tin whistle** (instrument). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin_whistle)
  Six holes that, lifted one at a time, give one major scale.
  It maps scale degree to fingers lifted: each finger is the next degree.
- **Richter-tuned diatonic harmonica** (instrument, 19th century). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richter-tuned_harmonica)
  Ten holes in one key, with blow notes giving the tonic chord and draw notes the rest of the scale.
  It ties scale degrees to breath direction, and a player owns one instrument per key.
- **Appalachian dulcimer** (instrument). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_dulcimer)
  A fretboard with diatonic frets only, so there are gaps where the chromatic notes would be.
  It is a fretted instrument with the wrong notes removed, and the uneven fret spacing shows the whole and half steps.
- **Diatonic button accordion** (instrument). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diatonic_button_accordion)
  Each row of buttons is one major scale, with different notes on push and pull.
  It makes a key a row.
- **Push "In Key" mode** (product feature, Ableton). [Using Push 2, Live manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/using-push-2/)
  The pad grid is folded so that only notes of the key remain, with the root pads in a different colour.
  Moving right is always the next scale degree, whatever the key.
- **Hookpad** (web app, Hooktheory). [Hookpad](https://www.hooktheory.com/hookpad)
  A songwriting editor where melody is entered as scale degrees 1 to 7 and every degree has a fixed colour.
  It makes the degree, not the pitch, the thing you type and see, so a song looks the same in every key.
- **Music Theory For Beginners: Begin With 7 Notes** (web article, Hooktheory). [Hooktheory blog](https://www.hooktheory.com/blog/music-theory-beginners/)
  The argument for learning in relative notation.
  It states the design idea behind the tool above.
- **Nashville Number System** (session notation, 1950s). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Number_System)
  Chord charts written as scale-degree numbers so the band can change key on the spot.
  It is degrees as the working language, the Arabic-numeral cousin of Roman numerals.
- **The Nashville Number System Demystified** (web article, Premier Guitar). [Premier Guitar](https://www.premierguitar.com/pro-advice/the-nashville-number-system-demystified)
  A player's guide to reading and writing the charts.
  It shows the system in use.
- **The Major Scale and Scale Degrees** (web lessons, musictheory.net). [The Major Scale](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/21)
  Two animated lessons: building the scale from whole and half steps, then naming its degrees.
  They isolate the pattern first and the numbering second.
- **Play with notes and scales** (web experiment, Ableton Learning Music, 2017). [Learning Music](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/notes-and-scales/play-with-notes-and-scales.html)
  A small piano roll in the browser with Tonic and Scale settings that reshape the same pattern.
  It lets a learner hear one melody move through different tonics and scales.
- **Chrome Music Lab: Song Maker** (web experiment, Google, 2018). [Song Maker](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/Song-Maker/)
  A grid sequencer whose rows are the notes of a chosen scale, coloured by degree.
  It offers only scale notes, so the grid is the major scale and nothing else.
- **Keys and scales in Live 12** (product feature, Ableton, 2024). [Ableton FAQ](https://help.ableton.com/hc/articles/11425083250972)
  A clip-level scale setting that highlights and folds the piano roll and that MIDI devices follow.
  It makes the scale a property of the material that every tool respects.
- **Whole-whole-half-whole-whole-whole-half, and the two tetrachords** (theory formula). [Tetrachord, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrachord)
  The step pattern of the major scale, often taught as two identical four-note halves a whole step apart.
  It is the scale as a recipe that can be started on any note.
- **Hanon no. 39, scales in all keys** (piano exercise, 1873). [The Virtuoso Pianist, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virtuoso_Pianist_in_60_Exercises)
  All major and minor scales with standard fingering.
  It is the scale as a daily physical routine, key by key.
- **CAGED and three-notes-per-string scale patterns** (guitar systems). (link not verified)
  Two ways of dividing the major scale across the neck, five shapes tied to open chords or seven even patterns.
  They turn one scale into a set of hand positions that move as a block to change key.

### Scale-degree names and solfège (tier 2)

Solfège has the oldest embodiments in this list: a hand that stands for the scale.
The modern ones are ear-training apps that drill the feel of each degree against a tonic.

- **Curwen hand signs** (gesture system, Sarah Glover and John Curwen, 19th century). [Curwen Hand Signs, LibreTexts](https://human.libretexts.org/Courses/Sierra_College/Equipping_the_Musical_Ear/01%3A_Pitch/1.04%3A_Curwen_Hand_Signs)
  One hand shape per solfège syllable, made at a height that follows the pitch.
  It gives every scale degree a posture whose shape hints at its tendency, such as the pointing finger of ti.
- **Kodály method** (teaching method, mid 20th century). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kod%C3%A1ly_method)
  A school curriculum built on movable do, hand signs and a set order of introducing degrees, starting from sol and mi.
  It adds a sequence: the degrees are learned a few at a time, pentatonic first.
- **Tonic sol-fa** (notation system, John Curwen). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonic_sol-fa)
  Music written as the letters d r m f s l t with no staff.
  It is notation that records only the scale degree.
- **Guidonian hand** (medieval mnemonic). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guidonian_hand)
  The joints of the left hand each assigned a note of the gamut, pointed to while teaching.
  It is the original hand interface for solfège.
- **Shape notes and Sacred Harp singing** (notation and singing tradition, United States, 1801 on). [Sacred Harp, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacred_Harp)
  Noteheads drawn as triangle, oval, rectangle and diamond for fa, sol, la and mi, with singers singing the syllables through before the words.
  It puts the scale degree into the shape of the note itself.
- **fasola.org** (web site of the Sacred Harp community). [fasola.org](https://fasola.org/)
  The tradition's own hub for singings and resources.
  It shows that the system is still in weekly use.
- **Functional Ear Trainer** (app, after Alain Benbassat's method). [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1088761926)
  Plays a cadence to set the key, then one note, and asks which degree it was.
  It isolates the function of a note in a key, as opposed to its distance from another note.
- **Hookpad degree colours** (product feature, Hooktheory). [Hookpad](https://www.hooktheory.com/hookpad)
  Degree 1 is always red, 2 always orange, and so on, across the editor, the books and the song database.
  It is colour used as solfège.
- **Scale Degrees** (web lesson, musictheory.net). [Lesson](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/23)
  The lesson that gives the names tonic, supertonic, mediant and the rest.
  It is the function-name vocabulary on its own.
- **Chrome Music Lab solfège task cards** (classroom resource, TES). [TES](https://www.tes.com/teaching-resource/pentatonic-chrome-music-lab-task-cards-for-sol-mi-la-do-re-12881729)
  Teacher-made cards that have pupils enter sol, mi, la, do and re patterns in Song Maker.
  They show a general grid tool being bent to movable do.
- **"Do-Re-Mi"** (song, Rodgers and Hammerstein, The Sound of Music, 1959). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do-Re-Mi)
  A song in which each syllable is introduced on its own scale degree.
  It is the most widely known solfège lesson there is.
- **Solfege! Fixed vs Movable Do** (video, MyMusicExpert). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BCq9wbD79jg)
  A comparison of the two systems.
  It isolates the question of whether a syllable names a pitch or a degree.
- **The Great Solfège Debate: Moveable Do vs Fixed Do** (video, Tune Indigo Learning). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mSn1VA_o8L8)
  The same question, argued.
  It is a second take on the pitch-or-degree split.
- **Musical Moment 2: What Is Movable Do?** (video, Jesse Miller). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lNclrJPalZs)
  A short definition.
  It is the minimal explainer.

### Minor scales (natural, harmonic, melodic) (tier 1)

Prior art treats the three minors as one scale with two adjustable notes.
Instruments fixed in a minor scale exist, the handpan above all.

- **Handpan in D Kurd** (instrument tuning). [Handpan Scale: How to Choose the Right One?, Instruments du Monde](https://instruments-du-monde.com/en/blogs/handpan/handpan-scale)
  The most common handpan tuning: a low D in the centre and the notes of D natural minor around the rim.
  It is an instrument that is a natural minor scale, with the notes ordered by alternating sides instead of in a line.
- **Hang** (instrument, PANArt, 2000). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hang_(instrument))
  The original of the handpan family, each one built in a single fixed scale.
  It established the idea of buying a scale as an object.
- **Circuit Tracks scales** (product feature, Novation). [Novation user guide](https://userguides.novationmusic.com/hc/en-gb/articles/26207128061458)
  A groovebox whose pads are remapped to one of sixteen scales, including natural, harmonic and melodic minor.
  The pads always hold one octave of the scale per row, so changing scale changes what the same pads play.
- **Scales and modes reference** (web tool, muted.io). [muted.io scales](https://muted.io/scales/)
  Interactive pages for each scale on piano and guitar.
  It lets the three minors be compared key by key on a keyboard.
- **musictheory.net lessons** (web lessons). [Lessons index](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons)
  The index includes a lesson on the three minor scales after the major scale.
  It presents harmonic and melodic minor as alterations of natural minor, one raised note at a time.
- **What is the Harmonic Minor scale?** (video, David Bennett Music Theory). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-amnre-0I3w)
  The scale explained through songs that use it.
  It isolates the raised seventh and the sound of the augmented second it creates.
- **Exploring Harmonic Minor** (video series, Signals Music Studio). [Series page](https://signalsmusic.studio/lessons/series/exploring-harmonic-minor)
  A series on the scale, its chords and its modes.
  It follows one scale all the way out to what can be written with it.
- **Minor scales: everything you need to know in 5 minutes** (video, Piano Fluency). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vxHfmMvHdF8)
  The three forms at the piano.
  It is the short at-the-keyboard version.
- **Understanding natural, harmonic, and melodic minor scales** (video, Piano Fluency). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aY4wpZyf3lE)
  A longer treatment from the same channel.
  It covers why each form exists.
- **Minor Scales: Natural, harmonic and melodic explained** (video, MusicTheoryAcademy). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a34qYxvRtJU)
  A notation-led explanation.
  It is the exam-syllabus version.
- **Explained: Barry Harris' 6th Diminished Scale, Major and Minor** (video, Roni Ben-Hur, Mike's Master Classes). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YdMA8Ll2RI4)
  The eight-note scales Barry Harris taught, a sixth chord interleaved with a diminished seventh, in major and minor forms.
  It is a different answer to "what is the minor scale", built for harmonising every note.
- **Barry Harris' 6th Diminished: The Basics** (video, Open Studio). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_n0vuuKdUyM)
  Adam Maness introduces the same method at the piano.
  It is the most approachable entry to it.
- **Jazz minor scale** (theory convention). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_minor_scale)
  Melodic minor played the same way up and down.
  It is the form the modes of melodic minor are taken from.
- **Melodic minor up, natural minor down** (piano exam practice, also in Hanon no. 39). [The Virtuoso Pianist, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virtuoso_Pianist_in_60_Exercises)
  The classical way of practising the scale with different notes in each direction.
  It makes the hand feel the sixth and seventh degrees as movable.

### Diatonic modes, including modes of melodic and harmonic minor (tier 1)

Two framings compete: modes as the same notes started elsewhere, and modes as one tonic made brighter or darker.
Folk instruments embody the first; the popular videos of the last ten years argue for the second.

- **Dulcimer modal tunings** (instrument practice). [A Tuning for Every Palette, FolkWorks](https://folkworks.org/a-tuning-for-every-palette/)
  Because the frets are diatonic, players retune the melody string to change mode: DAA for Ionian, DAD for Mixolydian, and so on.
  It makes the mode a tuning choice made before playing, and the same fret pattern then gives a different scale.
- **Harmonica positions** (instrument practice). [Harmonica positions and modes, Learn the Harmonica](https://www.learntheharmonica.com/post/harmonica-positions-modes-difference)
  Playing one diatonic harmonica from a different home note: first position is Ionian, second (cross harp) Mixolydian, third Dorian.
  It is "same notes, new tonic" as a practical habit, and the reason blues players carry a harp a fourth above the song's key.
- **Great Highland bagpipe chanter** (instrument). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Highland_bagpipe)
  Nine notes over a constant drone, with a flattened seventh.
  It is an instrument fixed in one mode against a drone, which is the clearest way to hear a mode at all.
- **Exquis scale lighting** (product feature, Intuitive Instruments). [Intuitive Instruments](https://dualo.com/en/welcome/)
  Several hundred scales that light the keys to show which notes belong.
  It shows a mode as a lit path on an isomorphic grid, so each mode has its own shape.
- **aQWERTYon pitch wheel colours** (web app, NYU MusEDLab). [Ethan Hein's write-up](https://ethanhein.substack.com/p/the-aqwertyon-pitch-wheels-and-the-future-of-music-theory-visualization)
  A scale drawn on the chromatic circle with bright notes in green, dark notes in blue and neutral ones in purple.
  It puts the brightness of a mode on screen as a ratio of colours.
- **Modes from light to dark around the chromatic circle** (web article, Ethan Hein). [Substack](https://ethanhein.substack.com/p/modes-from-light-to-dark-around-the-chromatic-circle)
  The seven modes drawn as clock diagrams in order from Lydian to Locrian, one note flattened at each step.
  It shows the brightness ordering as a sequence of single-note changes.
- **Dorian mode reference page** (web tool, muted.io). [muted.io Dorian](https://muted.io/dorian-mode/)
  One of a set of per-mode pages with piano, guitar and chords.
  It is the per-mode lookup.
- **A Study of Scales** (web reference, Ian Ring). [ianring.com](https://ianring.com/musictheory/scales/)
  Each scale page lists its modes as rotations of the same bit pattern.
  It treats a mode as a rotation, which covers the modes of melodic and harmonic minor with no special cases.
- **Lydian Chromatic Concept of Tonal Organization** (book, George Russell, 1953). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydian_Chromatic_Concept_of_Tonal_Organization)
  The theory that takes Lydian, the scale made by stacking fifths, as the parent scale.
  It is the origin of the brightness idea in jazz teaching.
- **The Modes Of The Major Scale** (video series, Signals Music Studio). [Series page](https://signalsmusic.studio/lessons/series/understanding-the-modes)
  One lesson per mode, each written into a short piece.
  It isolates the characteristic note and chord of each mode.
- **The Modes Ranked by Brightness** (video, David Bennett Music Theory). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jNY_ZCUBmcA)
  Scales and tonalities compared from brightest to darkest.
  It is the brightness ordering as a single video.
- **Songs that use the Dorian mode** (video, David Bennett Music Theory). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n_Zzztd5ZCk)
  One of a series that identifies each mode in well-known songs.
  It attaches each mode to tunes the viewer already knows.
- **Modes and Scales** (video playlist, David Bennett Music Theory). [YouTube playlist](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLlx2eo2tD6KoGvA_q0DFrYeLuwklZEy0O)
  The whole series, covering the minor scales and each mode of the major scale.
  It is a per-mode catalogue by example.
- **I made an album in a day using ALL the modes** (video, David Bennett Music Theory). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UO9Ua3lLhWM)
  One short piece written in each mode.
  It shows the modes as moods by composing in them.
- **Tutorial Level 2: Modes** (video, 8-bit Music Theory). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yS83fcFhG0E)
  An introduction to modes through video game music.
  It uses game soundtracks as the listening examples.
- **The DORIAN Mode Feels MYSTERIOUS (among other things)** (video, 8-bit Music Theory). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SbRD3tPipNw)
  One of the channel's single-mode studies.
  It isolates one mode and the feelings composers get from it.
- **Interview: Jacob Collier (Part 1)** (video, June Lee). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DnBr070vcNE)
  The interview that spread the brightness and darkness account of modes.
  It ties mode brightness to direction around the circle of fifths.
- **But what EXACTLY does super-ultra-hyper-mega-meta mean?** (video, Andy Chamberlain Music). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLunr5WL0kc)

### Pentatonic and blues scales (tier 2)

Pentatonic instruments are everywhere in early music education and in instruments sold to non-musicians.
The blues scale has less dedicated prior art; it mostly appears as one more entry in a scale list or as a guitar box.

- **Orff instruments with removable bars** (classroom instruments, Carl Orff, 1920s on). [Orff Schulwerk, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orff_Schulwerk)
  Xylophones and metallophones whose bars lift off, so the teacher removes F and B to leave a pentatonic scale.
  It makes the pentatonic scale by subtraction, and the child can see the gaps.
- **Pentatonic xylophones for young children** (instrument category). [Thomann guide](https://www.thomann.co.uk/onlineexpert_page_young_children_s_instruments_percussion_instruments.html)
  Fixed five-note instruments, commonly C D F G A, with note names engraved on the bars.
  The guide sells them on the point that there are no wrong notes to hit.
- **Steel tongue drum** (instrument, 2000s). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_tongue_drum)
  A steel shell with cut tongues, most often tuned to a pentatonic scale.
  It is a handful of notes arranged around a circle so that the two hands alternate up the scale.
- **Native American flute** (instrument). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_flute)
  A flute whose basic fingering gives a minor pentatonic scale.
  It builds one pentatonic scale into the finger holes.
- **Slendro** (gamelan tuning). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slendro)
  A five-note tuning with roughly equal steps.
  It is a reminder that "pentatonic" is a family, and that the black-key version is one member.
- **Wind chimes** (instrument). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_chime)
  Tubes commonly tuned to a pentatonic set so that any random order is pleasant.
  It is the no-wrong-notes property demonstrated by the wind.
- **Orba lead voice** (product feature, Artiphon, 2020). [Engadget review](https://www.engadget.com/artiphon-orba-review-music-fidget-toy-mpe-midi-synthesizer-looper-130020266.html)
  A handheld instrument with eight pads, whose lead part is mostly limited to a pentatonic scale in the chosen key.
  It gives a solo voice eight safe notes under the thumbs.

### "No wrong notes" scale-locked playing surfaces (tier 1)

There are two kinds: instruments built with only the right notes, and general surfaces that hide or remap the wrong ones.
Almost all lock to a key or scale; very few follow the chord of the moment, which is what the solo strip does.

- **Autoharp** (instrument, 1880s). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoharp)
  A zither with chord bars that damp every string not in the chosen chord.
  It locks a strum to the current chord by muting, so any stroke across the strings is right.
- **Omnichord** (electronic instrument, Suzuki, 1981). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnichord)
  Chord buttons under one hand and a touch strip under the other that plays only notes of the held chord.
  It is the closest ancestor of a chord-following strip: the notes under the finger change when the chord button changes.
- **Omnichord OM-108** (current model, Suzuki). [Suzuki](https://suzukimusic-global.com/products/om-108/)
  The reissue of the instrument.
  It shows the idea is still in production.
- **Handpan and steel tongue drum** (instruments). [Steel tongue drum, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_tongue_drum)
  Instruments built in one scale with a handful of notes.
  The lock is physical: there are no other notes to hit.
- **Kaossilator** (hardware synth, Korg, 2007). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaossilator)
  A touch pad whose horizontal axis is pitch, quantised to a chosen key and one of dozens of scales, with a settable note range.
  Korg's own pitch is that solos can be played without a single wrong note, by sliding a finger.
- **Tenori-on** (hardware instrument, Toshio Iwai and Yamaha, 2007). [Yamaha design page](https://www.yamaha.com/en/about/design/synapses/id_005/)
  A 16 by 16 grid of lit buttons played as a sequencer and instrument.
  The grid's rows are scale notes, so drawing patterns stays in key.
- **Orba** (hardware instrument, Artiphon, 2020). [Artiphon](https://artiphon.com/)
  Eight pads, each assigned a note of a chosen key in major or minor.
  It is a scale lock with no screen: key and scale are set in the app and the pads only ever hold scale notes.
- **Push "In Key" versus "Chromatic"** (product feature, Ableton). [Using Push 2, Live manual](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/using-push-2/)
  In Key folds the grid to scale notes only, and Chromatic keeps all notes with the scale ones lit.
  It offers both answers to the design question: remove the wrong notes, or keep them and dim them.
- **Launchpad Pro Scale mode** (product feature, Novation, 2016). [Scale mode guide (PDF)](https://fael-downloads-prod.focusrite.com/customer/dev/s3fs-public/novation/downloads/15481/lp-pro-scale-mode.pdf)
  Note mode remapped to a chosen scale and root, with a setting for how many notes each row overlaps the next.
  It adds a control for the layout of the locked notes, not just which notes they are.
- **Circuit Tracks scales** (product feature, Novation). [Novation user guide](https://userguides.novationmusic.com/hc/en-gb/articles/26207128061458)
  Two rows of eight pads that hold one octave each of the chosen scale.
  It applies the lock to recorded patterns too, which follow a scale change.
- **GarageBand Scale button** (product feature, Apple, iOS). [GarageBand for iPhone user guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/garageband-iphone/chs39282b52/ios)
  Choosing a scale turns the keyboard or fretboard into note bars, one per scale note.
  It redraws the instrument so that the wrong notes are not there to touch.
- **Komplete Kontrol Easy mode** (product feature, Native Instruments). [NI community thread](https://community.native-instruments.com/discussion/17533/maschine-mk3-a49-how-to-get-white-keys-only-scales)
  Any scale is mapped onto the white keys alone, and the black keys do nothing.
  It reuses the piano's own C major shape as the lock for every scale.
- **Scale awareness in Live 12** (product feature, Ableton, 2024). [Ableton FAQ](https://help.ableton.com/hc/articles/11425083250972)
  Scale-aware devices move any out-of-scale note to the nearest scale pitch, the lower one on a tie.
  It is the lock applied after the fact, as correction instead of prevention.
- **Scaler** (plugin, Scaler Music). [scalerplugin.com](https://www.scalerplugin.com/)
  A chord and scale plugin that can bind chords and scale notes to keys.
  It is the plugin form of the idea for people with an ordinary keyboard.
- **ThumbJam** (iOS app, Sonosaurus, 2009). [thumbjam.com](https://thumbjam.com/)
  A touch instrument of horizontal note bars in a chosen key and scale, with a large scale library.
  It is the long-standing iOS instance of a scale-locked strip played with the thumbs.
- **Bebot** (iOS app, Normalware, 2008). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bebot)
  A touch synth with a singing robot, whose surface can snap to a chosen scale.
  It is a continuous surface with optional scale snapping.
- **muted.io piano scale lock** (web tool). [muted.io piano](https://muted.io/piano/)
  A browser piano that can highlight a scale or lock to it so that only its notes sound.
  It is the remove-or-dim choice as a toggle on a drawn keyboard.
- **aQWERTYon** (web app, NYU MusEDLab). [MusEDLab announcement](https://wp.nyu.edu/musedlab/?p=235)
  The computer keyboard mapped to a scale so there are no wrong notes, with each column of keys forming a chord.
  It turns the typing keyboard into a scale-locked instrument where columns are chords and rows are steps.
- **Song Maker** (web experiment, Chrome Music Lab). [Song Maker](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/Song-Maker/)
  A grid that offers only the notes of its scale.
  It is the lock in a sequencer instead of a played surface.
- **Chrome Music Lab: Kandinsky** (web experiment, Google). (link not verified)
  Drawings turned into sound, with shapes mapped onto a fixed set of pleasant notes.
  It hides pitch choice altogether, the far end of the no-wrong-notes idea.
- **Bobby McFerrin Demonstrates the Power of the Pentatonic Scale** (video, World Science Festival). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ne6tB2KiZuk)
  The audience as a pentatonic instrument played by where McFerrin stands.
  It is a human scale-locked strip, and evidence that listeners predict the next cell.
- **Black-keys improvisation over a drone** (piano teaching technique). (link not verified)
  A first improvisation exercise in which the pupil plays any black keys while the teacher holds a bass.
  It is the oldest version of the lock, done by telling the hand where to stay.

## Chords and chord instruments

### Chord instruments as a lineage (not an OMT chapter)

The lineage splits into two families: a button per chord (root and quality fused into one control), and a root control crossed with a quality control.
A newer third family fixes a key first and gives one button per scale degree, with a second control for colour, which is the family Perfecto belongs to.

- **Autoharp** (acoustic instrument, Charles F. Zimmermann patent, 1882). [Wikipedia: Autoharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoharp)
  A zither with spring-loaded bars whose felt pads mute every string that is not in the bar's chord.
  One bar is one whole chord (root and quality fused), pressed by the left hand while the right strums all the strings, so the layout is a short list of chords for a few related keys.
- **Autoharp bar history** (research blog, Loopholes). [Bar Exam](https://loopholes.blog/2022/04/bar-exam/)
  A long-running organology blog on how the chord bar and its damping were invented and contested between Zimmermann and Gütter.
  It isolates the mechanism itself: a chord as a subtraction from all strings, not an addition of notes.
- **Stradella bass accordion** (acoustic instrument, 19th century). [Wikipedia: Stradella bass system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stradella_bass_system)
  The accordion's left-hand buttonboard of up to 120 buttons.
  Columns are roots in circle-of-fifths order, and each column runs counter-bass, root bass, major, minor, dominant seventh, diminished seventh, so the hand moves sideways to change root and in and out to change quality.
- **Stradella left hand, as taught** (wikibook). [Accordion/Left hand](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Accordion/Left_hand)
  A tutorial on finding and combining the bass and chord rows.
  It shows how extensions are reached with no extension buttons: two chord buttons pressed together, or a bass note under another root's chord.
- **Hammond S-6 Chord Organ** (electronic organ, Hammond, 1950). [SMEM: Hammond S-6](https://www.smemmusic.ch/index.php/fr/node/1380)
  A home organ with a 37-key melody keyboard and 96 chord buttons for the left hand.
  The buttons are 12 roots in fifths crossed with 8 types (major, minor, 7th, diminished, augmented, 6th, minor 7th, 9th), and two pedals give the root and fifth of whichever chord is held.
- **Magnus electric chord organ** (reed organ, Magnus Organ Corporation, 1958 on). [Wikipedia: Chord organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chord_organ)
  A cheap fan-blown reed organ sold by the million with numbered songbooks.
  It has six major and six minor chord buttons beside a short keyboard, so the whole chord vocabulary is twelve fused root-and-quality buttons.
- **Optigan** (optical-disc organ, Mattel, 1971). [Wikipedia: Optigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optigan)
  A home organ that plays recorded loops of real bands from a clear optical disc.
  Its chord pad is three rows of buttons, major, minor and diminished, and each button plays a recorded band vamping on that chord, so a chord button is a whole accompaniment.
- **Hohner Harmonetta** (mouth-blown free reed, Hohner, 1950s). [Wikipedia: Harmonetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonetta)
  A harmonica-like instrument with 32 hexagonal buttons laid out on the harmonic table.
  Each button opens one pitch class, and because fifths and thirds are neighbours, three adjacent buttons under one fingertip are a triad.
- **Suzuki Omnichord OM-27** (electronic instrument, Suzuki, 1981). [Wikipedia: Omnichord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnichord)
  The first Omnichord: 27 chord buttons, a strumplate, preset rhythms and one harp sound.
  The buttons are three rows, major, minor and seventh, over nine roots, and the left hand holds a chord while the right strokes the plate.
- **Suzuki Omnichord OM-36 and OM-84** (Suzuki, 1984). [Perfect Circuit: Omnichord history](https://www.perfectcircuit.com/signal/suzuki-omnichord-history)
  The second generation, with all twelve roots and an improved strumplate.
  The OM-84 keeps three rows of buttons but reaches 84 chords by pressing two or three rows in one column together, and adds the Chord Computer that stores a sequence of chords.
- **Suzuki Omnichord OM-100 and OM-200M** (Suzuki, 1989). [MusicRadar: Blast from the past](https://www.musicradar.com/news/blast-from-past-suzuki-omnichord)
  The third generation, with an angled strumplate and a strap for standing.
  The chord buttons are unchanged, and the OM-200M sends the chords and strums out over MIDI.
- **Suzuki Omnichord OM-150, OM-250M and OM-300** (Suzuki, to 1995). [Wikipedia: Omnichord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnichord)
  The last analogue-era models, with refreshed sounds.
  They isolate nothing new, which is the point: the three-row button field stayed fixed for fifteen years.
- **Suzuki QChord QC-1** (digital instrument, Suzuki, 1999). [Wikipedia: QChord](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/QChord)
  The Omnichord's digital successor, with General MIDI sounds, MIDI in and out and song cartridges.
  The same major, minor and seventh rows sit beside a strumplate, with other qualities by button combinations.
- **Suzuki Omnichord OM-108** (reissue, Suzuki, 2024). [Perfect Circuit: OM-108](https://www.perfectcircuit.com/suzuki-omnichord-om-108.html)
  The reissue, with 38 chord buttons and a four-octave strumplate.
  It has 12 roots and 9 chord types, 108 chords, the two new types being sus4 and add9, still reached by combinations of the three rows.
- **OM-108, what changed** (press piece, New Atlas, 2024). [Omnichord returns](https://newatlas.com/music/suzuki-omnichord-om108)
  A summary of the reissue's voices and rhythms.
  It notes that drum pads can be put on the strumplate, so the strumming surface becomes a second instrument.
- **Casio "Casio Chord" and Yamaha "Single Finger"** (auto-accompaniment fingering modes, 1980s on). [Fingering styles guide](https://www.epianos.co.uk/tyros-5-fingering-styles-guide/)
  The left-hand zone of a portable keyboard that turns one or a few keys into a full backing chord.
  On Yamaha, the root key alone is major, plus the nearest black key to its left is minor, plus the nearest white key is a seventh, and both make a minor seventh; Casio's rule counts keys instead (one for major, two for minor, three for seventh, four for minor seventh).
- **Fingered against single finger** (patent, Yamaha, 1999). [US5942710](https://patents.google.com/patent/US5942710)
  A patent on chord designation that sets out the two methods and their limits.
  It states the trade plainly: single finger reaches only major, minor, seventh and minor seventh, and fingered needs the player to know the chord.
- **Yamaha PSS-390 PortaSound** (portable keyboard, Yamaha, late 1980s). [SMEM: PSS-390](https://www.smemmusic.ch/index.php/de/node/2562)
  A small FM keyboard with single-finger and fingered auto chords and auto bass.
  It is a typical case of the chord zone as a split of the ordinary keyboard instead of separate buttons.
- **ISLA Instruments KordBot** (MIDI controller, 2016 to 2018). [Sound On Sound review](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/isla-instruments-kordbot)
  A chord controller with 32 chord-type buttons, a two-octave lit keyboard, 12 chord-memory pads and a ribbon.
  A type button latches the quality and any key then plays that quality on its root, with inversions per type, and a Song Builder mode that lights the chords of a key.
- **Irijule TheoryBoard** (MIDI controller, 2017 to 2021). [Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/news/theoryboard-wants-to-be-your-springboard-to-advanced-music-theory/)
  A 96-pad controller set to one of several hundred scales.
  The left 48 pads are the scale's chords, each chord repeated in six or more voicings up its column, and the right pads are the scale's single notes.
- **Conductive Labs NDLR** (MIDI sequencer, 2017). [Matrixsynth](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2017/09/the-conductive-labs-ndlr.html?m=0)
  A four-part arpeggiator box (pad, drone, two motifs) driven by one chord.
  A ring of seven buttons picks the scale degree, with the quality following from the key and mode, and the same buttons shifted pick the chord type (triad, 7th, 9th, suspensions).
- **Roland J-6 Chord Synthesizer** (AIRA Compact, Roland, 2022). [Roland J-6](https://www.roland.com/global/products/j-6/)
  A pocket Juno-voiced synth with a chord sequencer.
  One of 100 chord sets is called up and each key of its one-octave keyboard then plays a different chord of that set, so the keyboard is a palette, not twelve roots.
- **InstaChord** (handheld instrument, InstaChord Inc., Japan, 2020). [instachord.com](https://instachord.com/)
  A guitar-shaped electronic instrument from Japan sold on being playable by people who gave up guitar.
  The left hand presses numbered scale-degree buttons and the right hand strums pads, with small modifier buttons for sevenths and other colours.
- **Kantan Play core ("Kanpure")** (pocket instrument, InstaChord with M5Stack, 2025). [Switch Science: KANTAN Play](https://www.switch-science.com/pages/kantanplay-en)
  An open-source, game-console-sized instrument built on an M5Stack core.
  Buttons are numbered scale degrees in the chosen key, modifier buttons give minor, major and seventh variants, and presets play up to six instrument parts from one finger.
- **HiChord** (pocket synth, Pocket Audio, 2024). [hichord.shop](https://hichord.shop/)
  A pocket chord synth with seven chord buttons, a joystick and a small display.
  The seven buttons are the degrees of the chosen key, and the joystick's nine positions change the chord's quality (major or minor swap, suspended, sevenths, ninths), the closest relative of Perfecto's keys-plus-joystick.
- **Telepathic Instruments Orchid** (chord synth, Kevin Parker's company, 2024). [Orchid ORC-1](https://telepathicinstruments.com/products/orchid-orc-1)
  A chord-generating synth with a one-octave keyboard.
  The right hand's key is the root, the left hand's upper row of buttons is the chord type and the lower row adds modifiers that combine, two voicing dials roll the chord through inversions a note at a time, and a separate bass engine plays the root.
- **AlphaTheta Chordcat** (groovebox, 2024). [DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/tech/alphatheta-announces-new-portable-groovebox-chordcat)

  In Chord Cruiser mode it proposes chords that fit what came before and the player assigns the ones they like to keys, so the layout is built by choosing, not given.
- **minichord** (open-source pocket synth, Benjamin Poilvé, 2024). [PJRC: minichord](https://www.pjrc.com/minichord-electronic-harp/)
  A Teensy-based pocket Omnichord sold through Seeed.
  It has 21 chord buttons in seven columns of three, one column per note of C major, plus a sharp button, and a twelve-segment touch strip to strum.
- **LiberLive C1** (stringless guitar, 2024). [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/08/the-liberlive-c1-stringless-instrument-isnt-a-guitar-and-thats-fine)

  Together they cover the plugin layouts: a chord per key, a chord per degree, and a chord from dice.
- **Ripchord** (free plugin, Trackbout). (link not verified)
  A free chord-memory plugin that maps one key to one stored chord and imports chord presets.
  It isolates the mapping alone, with no theory attached.
- **Hookpad** (web app, Hooktheory, 2012 on). [Hookpad](https://www.hooktheory.com/hookpad)
  A song sketchpad where chords are entered by scale degree and coloured by function.
  A palette shows every chord of the key, and borrowed chords, secondary dominants, extensions and inversions are added to a placed chord.

Listed but not found as chord instruments: Chompi (a sampler, with no chord mode I could confirm), Joué and Dato (nothing confirmed), and Polyend.
Known but not confirmed by a link here: the Suzuki Tronichord (the Omnichord's predecessor), Yamaha QY10-style chord tracks entered as root plus type, Native Instruments' Smart Play chord mode, Artiphon's Orba chord mode, the Dualo du-touch, and the LAVA Genie.

### Triads (tier 1)

Prior art for the bare triad is mostly geometric: surfaces where a triad is one shape under the hand, and one-knob devices that list the triad types.

- **Stradella major and minor rows** (accordion). [Wikipedia: Stradella bass system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stradella_bass_system)
  The third and fourth rows of the left hand, a major triad and a minor triad for each root.
  One button is one triad, and the neighbouring columns are the triads a fifth away, so I, IV and V are three adjacent buttons.
- **Harmonic table layout** (button layout, used by the Harmonetta and the C-Thru Axis). [Wikipedia: Harmonic table note layout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmonic_table_note_layout)
  A hexagonal note layout with fifths vertically and thirds on the diagonals.
  A major triad is an upward triangle and a minor triad a downward one, the same in every key, so quality is a shape.
- **Mutable Instruments Plaits, chords model** (Eurorack module, 2018). [Plaits manual](https://pichenettes.github.io/mutable-instruments-documentation/modules/plaits/manual/)
  One of the module's synthesis models plays a four-note chord from one pitch.
  One knob chooses the chord type and another its inversion, and the root comes out of a separate jack.
- **Elektron Model:Cycles chord machine** (groovebox machine, 2020). [Perfect Circuit](https://www.perfectcircuit.com/signal/elektron-model-cycles)
  One of the six FM machines, which sounds a whole chord per note.
  It behaves like a synth stuck in chord memory: a knob picks the chord shape and every sequencer note is that shape moved in parallel.
- **Chrome Music Lab: Chords** (web experiment, Google, 2016). [Chords](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/Chords/)
  A piano keyboard with a major and minor switch.
  Clicking any key plays the triad on it, so the only two things to learn are root and quality.
- **Ableton Learning Music: chords chapter** (web lesson, Ableton, 2017). [Make some chords](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/chords/chords.html)
  A browser lesson with a small note grid to build chords in.
  It goes from stacking any notes to major and minor triads one page at a time, each page with one playable grid.
- **musictheory.net: Introduction to Chords** (web lesson). [Lesson 40](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/40)
  A step-through lesson that builds the four triad types on the staff and keyboard.
  It isolates the triad as two stacked thirds and changes one third at a time.
- **TonnetzViz** (web app, Ondřej Cífka). [TonnetzViz](https://cifkao.github.io/tonnetz-viz/)
  A Tonnetz that lights up in real time from MIDI or the computer keyboard.
  Triads appear as lit triangles, so the eye learns the major and minor shapes by playing them.
- **"The Other Side Of Triads"** (video, 12tone, 2017). [12tone episode list](https://thetvdb.com/series/12tone/allseasons/official)
  A 12tone video on triads, listed among the channel's 2017 uploads.
  The YouTube link is not verified; the title is confirmed from the episode list.
- **"Chords on Bass Lesson #2: Close Position Triads" and "#3: Open Position Triads"** (videos, Adam Neely, 2011). [Adam Neely episode list](https://thetvdb.com/series/adam-neely/allseasons/official)
  Two early lessons on playing triads on bass guitar.
  They isolate spacing: the same three notes close together and then spread, on an instrument where chords are awkward (YouTube links not verified).
- **CAGED chord shapes** (guitar technique). (link not verified)
  Five open-chord shapes (C, A, G, E, D) that link up along the neck to cover any major triad.
  It makes a triad a movable hand shape with the root's position inside the shape learned by sight.
- **Triads on three-string sets** (guitar exercise). (link not verified)
  Playing each triad in its three inversions on strings 1-2-3, then 2-3-4, and so on.
  It isolates the triad from the six-string chord, and is the usual route to triads as melody.

### Seventh chords (tier 1)

Sevenths show up as the first thing a chord instrument adds beyond triads, usually as one more row or one more button.

- **Stradella seventh and diminished rows** (accordion). [Accordion Life: Push those buttons](https://accordionlife.com/post/push-those-buttons)
  The fifth and sixth rows of the left hand.
  Modern instruments leave the fifth out of both, so a "seventh" button sounds root, third and seventh, and can be put over another bass note to make a different chord.
- **Hammond S-6 chord types** (chord organ, 1950). [SMEM: Hammond S-6](https://www.smemmusic.ch/index.php/fr/node/1380)
  Eight chord types per root.
  Seventh, minor seventh, major sixth and diminished each have a row of their own, a 1950 view of which four-note chords a home player needs.
- **Omnichord seventh row and combinations** (Suzuki, 1981 on). [Wikipedia: Omnichord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnichord)
  The third button row is the dominant seventh.
  From the OM-84 on, major seventh and minor seventh are made by pressing the seventh button with the major or the minor one, so a seventh chord is literally a triad plus a seventh.
- **Yamaha Single Finger sevenths** (keyboard feature). [Fingering styles guide](https://www.epianos.co.uk/tyros-5-fingering-styles-guide/)
  The rule for sevenths in the single-finger chord zone.
  A white key to the left of the root adds the seventh and a black key makes it minor, so the two modifiers are two fingers.
- **musictheory.net: Seventh Chords and More Seventh Chords** (web lessons). [Lesson 45](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/45)
  Two lessons that build five and then three more seventh chord types.
  Each type is shown as a triad plus a seventh interval, changed one at a time.
- **musictheory.net: Diatonic Seventh Chords** (web lesson). [Lesson 46](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/46)
  The seventh chord on each degree of major and minor scales.
  It isolates which quality falls on which degree, the table Perfecto's keys compute.
- **"The 7 Levels of Jazz Harmony"** (video, Adam Neely, 2019). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lz3WR-F_pnM)
  One pop tune reharmonised seven times, from plain triads upward.
  The early levels isolate what adding the seventh alone does to the same melody.
- **"One Exercise - All The Chords"** (video, Aimee Nolte). (link not verified)
  A piano exercise that voices major, minor, dominant and half-diminished sevenths in every key.
  It isolates the four common seventh qualities as one hand pattern changed a note at a time.
- **Diatonic sevenths up the scale** (piano and guitar exercise). (link not verified)
  Playing Imaj7, ii7, iii7, IVmaj7, V7, vi7, viiø7 up and down in every key.
  It is the standard drill for hearing quality as a result of degree.

### Roman numerals / chords by scale degree (tier 1)

This is the family Perfecto is in: fix the key, then give one control per degree.
The prior art divides into instruments that play by degree and tools that analyse or notate by degree.

- **Conductive Labs NDLR degree buttons** (hardware, 2017). [Matrixsynth](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2017/09/the-conductive-labs-ndlr.html?m=0)
  Seven buttons in a ring, labelled I to VII.
  The chord's quality is chosen for the player from the degree, key and mode, which is exactly the Roman numeral idea.
- **HiChord's seven buttons** (hardware, 2024). [hichord.shop](https://hichord.shop/)
  Seven chord buttons for the seven degrees of the chosen key.
  Changing key is one setting and the buttons keep their meaning.
- **Kantan Play and KANTAN Music notation** (hardware and notation, InstaChord, Japan). [Switch Science: KANTAN Play](https://www.switch-science.com/pages/kantanplay-en)
  An instrument with degree buttons and a matching number-based chord chart format.
  Songs are published as rows of numbers, so the chart and the buttons are the same thing.
- **Launchkey Chord Map** (controller feature). [Novation: Launchkey](https://novationmusic.com/articles/watch-how-creative-can-i-be-with-the-new-launchkey/)
  Eight pads holding eight chords of the scale.
  It isolates "chords of this key" as a bank that can be swapped for a more adventurous bank.
- **GarageBand chord strips** (iOS feature). [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/212214/garageband_ipad_01.html)
  Eight strips for the chords of the song's key.
  The strips are labelled with chord names, not numerals, but they move with the key.
- **Nashville Number System** (notation, 1950s). [Wikipedia: Nashville Number System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Number_System)
  Session players' chord charts written as the numbers 1 to 7.
  It isolates key-independence: one chart serves any key the singer calls.
- **Hookpad** (web app, Hooktheory). [Hookpad](https://www.hooktheory.com/hookpad)
  Chords are stored and shown by degree and coloured by degree.
  Changing the key moves everything, and the colours let function be seen without reading numerals.
- **Hooktheory Trends** (web tool). [Trends](https://www.hooktheory.com/trends)
  Pick a chord by degree and see which chords most often follow it across more than 75,000 analysed songs.
  It isolates the numeral as a statistic: what comes after IV.
- **Suggester and Tonaly** (iOS apps). [Tonaly](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/tonaly-write-practice-songs/id1242467844)
  Chord pickers that show a key's chords as numerals, Tonaly's on a circle of fifths.
  Tonaly can switch its labels between names, Roman numerals and Nashville numbers on the same circle.
- **Strum Machine charts** (web app). [Strum Machine](https://strummachine.com/)
  Backing tracks from chord charts, for bluegrass and old-time players.
  Its charts switch between letters and Nashville numbers, for players who think in numbers already.
- **musictheory.net: Roman Numeral Analysis** (web lessons). [Lesson 44](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/44)
  Lessons on labelling diatonic triads and sevenths with numerals.
  It isolates the casing rule (upper for major, lower for minor, a circle for diminished).
- **muted.io: Chords in Keys** (web tool). [Chords in Keys](https://muted.io/chords-in-keys/)
  An interactive table of the chords of every key.
  It isolates the table itself, playable.
- **"Building Blocks: Functional Harmony" and "The Job Of A Chord"** (videos, 12tone, 2017). [12tone episode list](https://thetvdb.com/series/12tone/allseasons/official)
  Two parts of 12tone's beginners' series, on what each degree's chord does.
  They isolate function from the numeral (YouTube links not verified).
- **"4 Chords"** (video, The Axis of Awesome, 2011). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oOlDewpCfZQ)
  A comedy medley of dozens of hits over I-V-vi-IV.
  It is the best-known demonstration that songs are the same once heard by degree.
- **Diatonic chords in twelve keys** (piano exercise). (link not verified)
  Playing I to vii° and common progressions such as ii-V-I by number in every key.
  It trains the hand to do what a degree button does.

- **KordBot display and chord memory** (hardware). [Sound On Sound review](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/isla-instruments-kordbot)

- **Scaler and Scaler Detector** (plugins). [scalermusic.com](https://scalermusic.com/)

- **ChordPro** (text format, 1992). [chordpro.org](https://www.chordpro.org/)

- **"Forte Numbers: A Name For Every Chord"** (video, 12tone, 2017). [12tone episode list](https://thetvdb.com/series/12tone/allseasons/official)

### Altered and extended chords (9, 11, 13, alt) (tier 1)

Instruments reach extensions in three ways: one more button that combines, one more finger, or a second axis.
Explainers nearly all present them as thirds stacked past the seventh.

- **Orchid's modifier row** (hardware). [Orchid ORC-1](https://telepathicinstruments.com/products/orchid-orc-1)
  A lower row of buttons that add notes to the chord type held on the upper row.
  Modifiers combine, so an extended chord is several fingers of the left hand, one per added note.
- **HiChord's joystick** (hardware). [hichord.shop](https://hichord.shop/)
  Nine joystick positions that recolour the chord under the button.
  Extension is a direction of the thumb instead of another button.
- **Omnichord OM-108's sus4 and add9** (hardware, 2024). [Perfect Circuit: OM-108](https://www.perfectcircuit.com/suzuki-omnichord-om-108.html)
  The two chord types the reissue added to the old seven.
  It shows which two colours a 2024 player was thought to miss most.
- **Hammond S-6's ninth row** (chord organ, 1950). [SMEM: Hammond S-6](https://www.smemmusic.ch/index.php/fr/node/1380)
  A row of ninth chords for all twelve roots.
  An extended chord as a single button, seventy years before the current wave.
- **Navichord's extra fingers** (iOS app). [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/navichord-chord-sequencer/id916452748)
  One finger plays a triad on the Tonnetz and a second or third adds the seventh or ninth.
  Extensions are the neighbouring triangles, so a ninth chord is visibly two triads sharing notes.
- **NDLR chord types** (hardware). [Matrixsynth](https://www.matrixsynth.com/2017/09/the-conductive-labs-ndlr.html?m=0)
  The degree buttons, shifted, choose triad, seventh, ninth or suspended.
  Extension is a mode applied to all degrees at once.
- **"Music Theory Minute #1.1: What's a Chord Extension?"** (video, 8-bit Music Theory, 2019). [8-bit Music Theory, 2019](https://thetvdb.com/series/8-bit-music-theory/seasons/official/2019)
  A one-minute explainer, first of a four-part set.
  The others are "#1.2: Voicing Extended Chords", "#1.3: Altered Chord Extensions" and "#1.4: Upper Structure Triads", one idea a minute (YouTube links not verified).
- **"Jacob Collier Explains Music in 5 Levels of Difficulty"** (video, WIRED, 2018). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eRkgK4jfi6M)
  Collier explains harmony to a child, a teen, a student, a professional and Herbie Hancock.
  The same tune is reharmonised at each level, so the extensions are heard arriving.
- **"Interview: Jacob Collier (Part 1)"** (video, June Lee, 2017). [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DnBr070vcNE)
  An annotated interview with transcribed examples on screen.
  It includes the "Super-Ultra-Hyper-Mega-Meta Lydian" idea, extensions stacked in fifths until the scale changes.
- **The Hendrix chord** (reference). [Wikipedia: Dominant seventh sharp ninth chord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant_seventh_sharp_ninth_chord)
  The 7♯9 chord and where it is used.
  One altered chord isolated as a single guitar grip with a single famous use.
- **The mu chord** (reference, Steely Dan). [Wikipedia: Mu chord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu_chord)
  Steely Dan's name for a major triad with an added second.
  One added note isolated and given a name by the band that used it (the page redirects to the band's article).
- **Upper-structure triads** (piano technique). [Wikipedia: Polychord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polychord)
  A plain triad in the right hand over a tritone or shell in the left.
  It makes an altered dominant into two easy shapes, and is how pianists reach ♯9, ♯11 and ♭13 without counting.

### Chord-scale theory (a mode per chord; thirds stacked through a mode) (tier 1)

Prior art is mostly tables and scale catalogues, plus controllers that derive a chord set from any scale.
I found few instruments that let the player choose the mode per chord, which is what Perfecto's grid rows do.

- **TheoryBoard's scale-derived chords** (hardware). [Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/news/theoryboard-wants-to-be-your-springboard-to-advanced-music-theory/)
  Choose one of several hundred scales and the chord pads become that scale's chords.
  Chords are computed from the scale, and borrowing from another mode is a control.
- **MPC Pad Perform, Chords** (feature). [CDM: MPC 1.8](https://cdm.link/mpc-1-8-update-expands-play-inside-look-developers/)
  Pads that stack scale thirds on each scale note by a chosen pattern.
  It is thirds stacked through a scale, stated as a setting (1-3-5, 1-3-5-7).
- **Suggester's scale types** (iOS app). [App Store: Suggester 2](https://apps.apple.com/app/suggester-2/id6448964737)
  Chords listed for major, the modes, jazz minor, whole tone and more.
  It isolates what chords a non-major scale produces.
- **Ian Ring's "A Study of Scales"** (web catalogue). [A Study of Scales](https://ianring.com/musictheory/scales/)
  Every possible scale, numbered, with its modes and properties.
  It is the odd, exhaustive version of a mode list.
- **Chord-scale system** (reference). [Wikipedia: Chord-scale system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chord-scale_system)
  The jazz teaching method of pairing each chord with a scale, and its critics.
  It notes that students learn about 21 scales, the same number as Perfecto's grid rows.
- **Lydian Chromatic Concept** (book, George Russell, 1953). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydian_Chromatic_Concept_of_Tonal_Organization)
  The book chord-scale theory starts from.
  It orders scales by distance from Lydian, a brightness order like the grid's.
- **"Chord Scales in Jazz Chord Construction - Part I" and "Part II"** (videos, Adam Neely, 2010). [Adam Neely episode list](https://thetvdb.com/series/adam-neely/allseasons/official)
  Two early lessons on building chords out of their scales.
  They take the direction Perfecto does, scale first and chord from it (YouTube links not verified).
- **"Building Blocks: The Modes"** (video, 12tone, 2017). [12tone episode list](https://thetvdb.com/series/12tone/allseasons/official)
  The modes chapter of 12tone's beginners' series.
  It isolates each mode's one characteristic note (YouTube link not verified).
- **"The Maestro's 6th Chords"** (podcast and video, Open Studio's You'll Hear It). [Episode page](https://youllhearit.transistor.fm/episodes/the-maestros-6th-chords)
  Adam Maness introduces Peter Martin to Barry Harris's sixth-diminished scale.
  It is a rival chord-scale theory: an eight-note scale whose stacked chords alternate a sixth chord and a diminished seventh.
- **Modes over a drone** (guitar and piano exercise). (link not verified)
  Playing each mode of one parent scale over a held root.
  It isolates a mode's colour from its fingering.

### Inversion (tier 2)

Inversion is usually a knob, a pair of up and down buttons, or a position along a strip.
On the oldest chord instruments it is not a choice at all.

- **Orchid's voicing dials** (hardware). [Orchid ORC-1](https://telepathicinstruments.com/products/orchid-orc-1)
  Two dials that move the chord up or down one note at a time.
  Turning rolls the bottom note to the top, a continuous cascade of inversions under one hand.
- **Qu-Bit Chord v2** (Eurorack module). [Schneidersladen](https://schneidersladen.de/en/qu-bit-electronix-chord-v2-black)
  A four-voice chord oscillator.
  Quality and inversion are separate knobs with separate voltage inputs, so inversion can be sequenced apart from the chord.
- **Instruō harmonàig** (Eurorack module). [Schneidersladen](https://schneidersladen.de/en/instruo-harmonaig)
  A diatonic quantiser with four voices for root, third, fifth and seventh.
  One control chooses root position to third inversion and another chooses closed, open or two uneven voicings.
- **Plaits chords model, the TIMBRE knob** (Eurorack module). [Plaits manual](https://pichenettes.github.io/mutable-instruments-documentation/modules/plaits/manual/)
  The knob beside chord type sets inversion and transposition.
  Sweeping it carries the same chord up through its inversions.
- **Stradella's fixed octave** (accordion). [Wikipedia: Stradella bass system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stradella_bass_system)
  All the chord buttons draw on one octave of reeds.
  Each chord comes out in whichever inversion fits that octave, so the player never chooses and chords connect smoothly.
- **Launchkey inversion pads** (controller feature). [Novation: Launchkey](https://novationmusic.com/articles/watch-how-creative-can-i-be-with-the-new-launchkey/)
  Two performance pads, Inversion Up and Inversion Down.
  Each press steps the held chord to its next inversion.
- **GarageBand chord strip segments** (iOS feature). [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/212214/garageband_ipad_01.html)
  A Smart Keyboard chord strip divided along its length.
  Touching higher on the strip plays a higher inversion, the nearest thing to Perfecto's slide used for voicing.
- **Cubase: "Inversion Therapy"** (technique article, Sound On Sound). [Inversion Therapy](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/inversion-therapy)
  A walkthrough of changing chord voicings and inversions with Cubase's chord tools.
  It isolates inversion as an edit made after the chord is chosen.
- **pianochord.org inversion finder** (web tool). [Chord inversions finder](https://www.pianochord.org/chord-inversions-finder.html)
  Pick a chord and see each inversion on a keyboard.
  It isolates the lookup.
- **musictheory.net: Triad Inversion and Seventh Chord Inversion** (web lessons). [Lesson 42](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/42)
  Step-through lessons that move the bottom note up an octave.
  One note moves per step.
- **Inversions up the keyboard** (piano exercise). (link not verified)
  Each triad block and broken through root, first, second and back, hands separate then together.
  It is the standard drill, and trains the fingering change that goes with each inversion.

### Voice leading and jazz voicings (drop 2, shell, rootless, Barry Harris, etc.) (tier 2)

Software treats this as an automatic "stay close" switch.
Players treat it as a set of named recipes drilled through a tune.

- **TheoryBoard voicing columns** (hardware). [Attack Magazine](https://www.attackmagazine.com/news/theoryboard-wants-to-be-your-springboard-to-advanced-music-theory/)
  Each chord repeated in six or more voicings up its column of pads.
  Voice leading is the player's path between rows.
- **Cubase Chord Pads: adaptive voicing** (DAW feature). [Steinberg help](https://steinberg.help/nuendo/v8/en/cubase_nuendo/topics/chord_pads/chord_pads_adaptive_voicing_c.html)
  Pads re-voice themselves so that pitches do not jump from the previous chord.
  One pad can be named the reference that the others stay near, a clear statement of what Perfecto's voice-leading flag does.
- **Cubase Chord Pads** (technique article, Sound On Sound). [Using Chord Pads](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/cubase-using-chord-pads?amp)
  A walkthrough of pads with voicing and tension controls and player types (piano, guitar).
  It isolates the idea that a voicing belongs to an instrument.
- **OneMotion Chord Player** (web app). [Chord Player](https://www.onemotion.com/chord-player/)
  A progression builder with voicing options.
  It isolates voicing as a per-chord setting heard at once.
- **ChordGeometries** (desktop software, Dmitri Tymoczko). [ChordGeometries](https://dmitri.mycpanel.princeton.edu/ChordGeometries.html)
  A viewer for chords as points in a three-dimensional space where distance is voice-leading work.
  It isolates voice leading as geometry: near chords need small finger movements.
- **Drop voicings** (reference). [Wikipedia: Voicing (music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voicing_(music))
  The article's section on drop-2 and drop-4 naming.
  It isolates the recipe: take a close chord and drop one voice an octave.
- **Block chords and locked hands** (reference). [Wikipedia: Block chord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_chord)
  The Shearing style of a chord under every melody note.
  It isolates voicing as something that follows a melody.
- **The So What chord** (reference). [Wikipedia: So What chord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/So_What_chord)
  Bill Evans's voicing of three fourths and a third.
  One voicing isolated and named after one record.
- **"Building Rootless Left Hand Voicings"** (podcast and video, Open Studio's You'll Hear It). [Episode page](https://youllhearit.transistor.fm/episodes/building-rootless-left-hand-voicings)

- **Notes on the method of Barry Harris** (web notes, Frédéric Galliano). [Notes](https://irfu.cea.fr/Pisp/frederic.galliano/Zique/barry_harris.html)

### Slash chords and bass notes (tier 2)

The bass note is nearly always a separate control: another row, a pedal, another finger, or a separate synth engine.

- **Stradella bass rows under chord rows** (accordion). [Accordion: Left hand](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Accordion/Left_hand)
  Two rows of single bass notes beside four rows of chords.
  Any bass button can go under any chord button, so C over E is two fingers, and the counter-bass row is there to make thirds in the bass easy.
- **Hammond S-6 pedals** (chord organ). [SMEM: Hammond S-6](https://www.smemmusic.ch/index.php/fr/node/1380)
  Two pedals that sound the root and the fifth of whichever chord button is held.
  It isolates the alternating bass as a foot choice.
- **Orchid's bass engine** (hardware). [Orchid ORC-1](https://telepathicinstruments.com/products/orchid-orc-1)
  A separate bass synth that follows the chord's root or is played solo.
  The bass is a voice of its own under the chord.
- **SoundPrism's bass column** (iOS app). [CDM](https://cdm.link/soundprism-colorful-playable-ipad-app-and-organizing-tones-and-harmony-visually/)
  A separate column at the edge of the tile grid for bass notes.
  The left thumb picks the bass while the right hand picks the chord.
- **Launchkey "Bass and Chord" and "Left and Right" pads** (controller feature). [Novation: Launchkey](https://novationmusic.com/articles/watch-how-creative-can-i-be-with-the-new-launchkey/)
  Performance pads that play the bass note first and then the chord, or split the chord into two hands.
  It isolates bass and chord in time.
- **Yamaha "On Bass" fingering** (keyboard feature). [Fingering styles guide](https://www.epianos.co.uk/tyros-5-fingering-styles-guide/)
  An accompaniment mode where the lowest key held becomes the bass.
  It is the arranger keyboard's way to play a slash chord into the backing band.
- **Hookpad inversions** (web app). [Hookpad](https://www.hooktheory.com/hookpad)
  A placed chord can be given an inversion, which sets its bass.
  It ties the slash to the degree, in the figured-bass way.
- **Slash chord** (reference). [Wikipedia: Slash chord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slash_chord)
  The notation and its uses, including the walkdown.
  It notes the simplifying use: F/D written so a beginner can play Dm7.
- **Guitar Lab: Slash Chord Formulas** (video course, TrueFire). [TrueFire](https://truefire.com/c963)
  A guitar course wholly on slash chords.
  It isolates the triad-over-bass shapes as a vocabulary.
- **Descending bass under a held chord** (piano and guitar exercise). (link not verified)
  C, C/B, C/A, C/G and the like, known as a walkdown or line cliché.

- **Triad over a bass note** (piano exercise). (link not verified)
  Left hand one note, right hand a plain triad from another root (D/C, B♭/C).
  It shows the slash chord as the cheapest way to an extended sound.

### Strumming / arpeggiating a held chord as a surface (tier 1)

The Omnichord's idea, hold a chord with one hand and stroke its notes with the other, has been rebuilt on touch strips, paddles and screens.
The surface always holds only the notes of the held chord.

- **Autoharp** (acoustic instrument). [Wikipedia: Autoharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoharp)
  The origin of the idea.
  The strum crosses every string and the bar decides which sound.
- **Omnichord strumplate** (Suzuki, 1981 on). [Wikipedia: Omnichord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnichord)
  A touch plate that plays the held chord's notes across four octaves.
  Stroked it is a harp, tapped it is single notes, and no place on it is wrong.
- **OM-108 strumplate voices** (Suzuki, 2024). [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/music/suzuki-omnichord-om108)
  Ten voices for the plate, apart from the chord's own sound.
  It isolates the strum as a second instrument over the chord.
- **minichord touch zone** (open-source hardware). [PJRC: minichord](https://www.pjrc.com/minichord-electronic-harp/)
  Twelve touch sections stacked upright.
  They are swept to strum or touched one at a time to pick.
- **LiberLive C1 paddles** (stringless guitar). [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/08/the-liberlive-c1-stringless-instrument-isnt-a-guitar-and-thats-fine)
  Two levers flicked up or down in place of strings.
  It reduces the strum to its gesture, direction and timing with no string to choose.
- **Orchid performance modes** (hardware). [MusicTech](https://musictech.com/news/gear/telepathic-instruments-orchid-global-release/)
  Five modes, Strum, Slop, Arpeggiator, Pattern and Harp, for how a held chord is sounded.
  Slop, a deliberately uneven spread, is the odd one.
- **Arturia KeyStep 37 strum** (controller feature, 2020). [CDM](https://cdm.link/keystep-37-adds-keys-encoders-chord-features-to-arturia-keyboard/)
  A chord mode with a strum function played from the touch strip.
  One key holds the chord and the strip strums it.
- **Artiphon Chorda and Instrument 1** (hardware). [artiphon.com](https://artiphon.com/)
  Smart instruments that are strummed, tapped or bowed.
  The same surface is fretted and strummed, so chord and strum are two hands on one strip.
- **Polychord strum keys** (iPad app). [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013-05-01-polychord-for-ipad-an-inspiring-way-to-create-music.html)
  Thirteen keys that change to the notes of whichever chord circle is held.
  It is close to Perfecto's solo strip: no key is outside the chord.
- **GarageBand Smart Guitar** (iOS feature). [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/212214/garageband_ipad_01.html)
  Six strings drawn across each chord strip.
  A swipe across a strip strums that chord and a tap on one string picks it.
- **Launchkey manual arp pads** (controller feature). [Novation: Launchkey](https://novationmusic.com/articles/watch-how-creative-can-i-be-with-the-new-launchkey/)
  Two pads, Manual Arp Up and Down.
  Each press plays the next note of the held chord, an arpeggio with the player as the clock.
- **Ableton Live 12 Chord device, Strum** (DAW feature, 2024). [Isotonik: MIDI effects in Live 12](https://isotonikstudios.com/one-chord-endless-directions-midi-effects-in-ableton-live-12/)
  The Chord MIDI effect gained strum time, tension and crescendo.
  It isolates the strum as three numbers.
- **Logic Pro Chord Trigger** (DAW feature). [Ask.Audio](https://ask.audio/articles/logic-pro-x-tutorial-playing-impossible-patterns-with-chord-trigger)
  A MIDI plugin that gives each key a stored chord.
  The article puts an arpeggiator after it, the usual software stand-in for a strumplate.
- **Chrome Music Lab: Arpeggios** (web experiment, Google, 2016). [Arpeggios](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/Arpeggios/)
  A wheel of major and minor chords in fifths with a choice of arpeggio pattern.
  Click a chord and it is arpeggiated, so the only choices are chord and pattern.
- **Strum Machine** (web app). [Strum Machine](https://strummachine.com/)
  Backing tracks stitched from recordings of real strummed guitar chords.
  It isolates the strum pattern as the thing a chart is played with.
- **Alberti bass** (piano technique). [Wikipedia: Alberti bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberti_bass)
  The low, high, middle, high broken-chord pattern.
  The oldest named arpeggiator pattern.
- **Travis picking and rolled chords** (guitar and piano techniques). (link not verified)
  A steady thumb alternating bass notes under fingerpicked chord tones, and a chord spread from the bottom up.
  Both are what the strum settings on the devices above imitate.

## Harmony in motion and counterpoint

### Named progressions and schemas (tier 2, classical and puff schemas tier 4)

Prior art here is mostly lists: a progression is a preset you pick, a chart you play along with, or a statistic about what songs do.
Very little lets the hand feel a schema as a shape, and nothing found isolates the classical or "puff" schemas in pop beyond Open Music Theory's own chapters.

- **Suzuki Omnichord** (hardware instrument, Suzuki, 1981 onward, reissued as the OM-108). [Wikipedia: Omnichord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnichord)
  A row of chord buttons under one hand and a touch strumplate under the other.
  It isolates the progression itself: the left hand does nothing but choose the next chord of a I-IV-V or doo-wop loop, and the strum can never sound a note outside it.
- **iReal Pro: the 50 built-in exercises** (product feature, Technimo). [iReal Pro: Advanced Practicing Techniques](https://www.irealpro.com/news/advanced-practicing-techniques)
  Chord charts for ii-V-I, blues and other stock progressions with a generated rhythm section.
  It isolates the named progression as a loop to play over, written once as a chart and heard in any key.
- **Hooktheory Trends** (web tool, Hooktheory). [hooktheory.com/trends](https://www.hooktheory.com/trends)
  Click a chord and see which chords follow it in the TheoryTab database, with a probability on each and the songs that do it.
  It isolates "what usually comes next": a named progression appears as a path of clicks through a tree of likelihoods.
- **Hooktheory Trends, the write-up** (blog post, Hooktheory). [The Trends tool](https://www.hooktheory.com/blog/trends-tool/)
  Explains that the next-chord sizes come from a Markov analysis of every song in the database.
  It isolates the idea that a schema is a statistical habit, not a rule.
- **Autochords** (iOS app). [App Store: Autochords](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/autochords/id788423364)
  Pick a mood and a key and it hands back a main progression with alternatives, shown as guitar, piano or ukulele shapes.
  It isolates the stock progression as a thing chosen by feel, with no theory on screen.
- **Chordbot: Song-O-Matic** (app feature, Contrasonic). [App Store: Chordbot](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/chordbot/id394754767)
  A chord-progression player with an automatic song generator that has pop, jazz and experimental profiles.
  It isolates genre as a set of likely progressions: the same generator gives different schemas per profile.
- **ChordMaps2** (iPad MIDI controller, Steve Mugglin). [App Store: ChordMaps2](https://apps.apple.com/mx/app/chordmaps2/id1098330720)
  Common progressions are played by touching chord locations on "musical maps" in major and minor keys.
  It isolates the route: a progression is a path traced with the finger across a fixed map.
- **Royal road progression** (named schema, J-pop). [Wikipedia: Royal road progression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_road_progression)
  The IVmaj7-V7-iii7-vi loop, named and catalogued as a schema of its own.
  It isolates a schema that starts away from the tonic, which the four-chord demonstrations in English mostly do not cover.
- **The Axis of Awesome, "4 Chords" (official music video)** (YouTube, The Axis of Awesome, 2011). [youtube.com/watch?v=oOlDewpCfZQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oOlDewpCfZQ)
  A medley of dozens of pop songs sung over one I-V-vi-IV loop.
  It isolates the progression by holding it still while everything else changes.
- **"Chord Progressions - The 'Doo-Wop Chords'"** (YouTube, channel not confirmed). [youtube.com/watch?v=UwiE_VWuKJQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UwiE_VWuKJQ)
  A walk through I-vi-IV-V from "Blue Moon" onward.
  It isolates one schema across decades of songs.
- **"Songs Using the 12-Bar Blues Chord Progression"** (YouTube, channel not confirmed). [youtube.com/watch?v=p3Rifu3maGo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3Rifu3maGo)
  A list video of songs on the twelve-bar form.
  It isolates the form as a container that many songs share.
- **ii-V-I in all twelve keys** (piano and guitar exercise; Jamey Aebersold's Volume 3 "The ii/V7/I Progression" is the standard play-along, link not verified).
  The cadence is played round the circle of fifths until the hands know it without reading.
  It isolates the schema as a hand shape that survives transposition.

### Harmonic function, phrase model, cadences, predominants, prolongation (tier 3)

Function is usually drawn as a flow chart (tonic to predominant to dominant) or trained by ear as "how far from home".
Few instruments expose it directly; the nearest are maps where position means function.

- **ChordMapMidi** (iOS app, Steve Mugglin with AudioKit). [audiokitpro.com/chordmapmidi](https://audiokitpro.com/chordmapmidi/)
  Mugglin's chord flow diagrams (from his chordmaps.com, link not verified) made into a playable MIDI surface.
  It isolates function as direction of flow: chords sit in boxes joined by arrows, and the finger follows the arrows home.
- **Chord maps explained** (article, LedgerNote). [ledgernote.com: Chord Maps](https://ledgernote.com/blog/interesting/chord-maps/)
  A write-up of the same coloured boxes and arrows for navigating a key.
  It isolates the phrase model as a diagram to read before playing.
- **Functional Ear Trainer** (iOS and Android app, after Alain Benbassat's method). [App Store: Functional Ear Trainer](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1088761926)
  A short cadence sets up a key, then one note sounds and you name its scale degree.
  It isolates function as a feeling relative to home: the cadence is the whole context and the answer is always a degree, never a pitch.
- **Harmonia: Roman numeral and function labelling** (app feature, Illiac Software, Heinrich Taube). [App Store: Harmonia](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1495723746)
  Scores you label with Roman numerals, graded at once by an analysis engine.
  It isolates analysis as an act: the eye reads a chord, the hand types its function, and the machine says yes or no.
- **Seth Monahan, "Basics of Classical Harmony and Counterpoint"** (YouTube lecture series, since 2016). [Yale faculty page that describes the series](https://music.yale.edu/faculty/seth-monahan/)
  A numbered run of lessons that builds from the phrase model to chromatic harmony.
  It isolates function as a syntax, taught one chord class per lesson on an annotated score.
- **Barry Harris's sixth-diminished scale** (piano and guitar technique). [barryharris.com: harmony tutorial](https://www.barryharris.com/music-harmony-tutorial)
  A major sixth chord and the diminished seventh a step below it are interleaved into one eight-note scale.
  It isolates tonic and dominant as two alternating hand shapes: walking the scale in block chords is tonic, dominant, tonic, dominant.
- **"Songs that use the Picardy Third"** (YouTube, David Bennett Piano). [youtube.com/watch?v=jGaNdKabvQ4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jGaNdKabvQ4)
  Examples of minor passages that cadence on the major tonic.
  It isolates a single cadence type by ear, song after song.

### Modal mixture and borrowed chords (tier 2)

Software treats borrowing as a second palette: choose a parallel mode and its chords appear beside the diatonic ones.
Videos treat it as a list of songs, with the minor iv as the standard first example.

- **Hookpad: "bor" borrowed chords** (product feature, Hooktheory). [Hooktheory forum: what is bI (bor)?](https://forum.hooktheory.com/t/what-is-bi-bor/5301)
  Any chord outside the seven native ones is entered by borrowing it from a named parallel scale.
  It isolates mixture as provenance: every chromatic chord carries the name of the mode it came from.
- **Hookpad: chord search** (product feature, Hooktheory). [Hooktheory forum: chord search documentation](https://forum.hooktheory.com/t/hookpad-chord-search-documentation/3307)
  Type a chord name and Hookpad finds it, picking the borrowed scale closest to the current one when it is not diatonic.
  It isolates the question "which mode would I have to borrow from to get this chord?".
- **Scaler: modal interchange requests and Modulation Pathways** (product feature and forum thread, Scaler Music). [Scaler forum: support for creating modal interchange](https://forum.scalermusic.com/t/feature-suggestions-support-for-creating-modal-interchange/732?page=2)
  Users asking for, and Scaler adding, a way to pull chords from parallel modes into a progression.
  It isolates borrowing as a browsing act across scales laid side by side.
- **"MODAL INTERCHANGE - Borrowed Chords and Parallel Modes Explained"** (YouTube, channel not confirmed). [youtube.com/watch?v=14gBV0Ni-DY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=14gBV0Ni-DY)
  An explainer of parallel modes as the source of borrowed chords.
  It isolates the parallel-mode table as the thing to memorise.
- **"Modal Interchange - Chord Progressions with Beautiful IVm ideas"** (YouTube, channel not confirmed). [youtube.com/watch?v=RTRo0omubRQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RTRo0omubRQ)
  A lesson built around the minor iv in a major key.
  It isolates one borrowed chord and the one voice (the flat sixth degree) that makes it.
- **David Bennett Piano, "Modal Interchange (Borrowed Chords) Simply Explained! (Lesson 6)"** (YouTube, jazz piano course; link not verified).
  Lesson 6 of his jazz piano course.
  It isolates mixture at the keyboard, as chords swapped under the hands.
- **Modal interchange for jazz players** (article with exercises, Learn Jazz Standards). [learnjazzstandards.com: modal interchange](https://www.learnjazzstandards.com/blog/modal-interchange/)
  A written lesson on where borrowed chords sit and how far they can go before the key is lost.
  It isolates the boundary between borrowing and modulating.

### Tonicization and secondary dominants (tier 3)

The prior art is thin on instruments and rich in notation tricks: the slash in V/V, a map with a small tonic region around each degree, or a jazz drill of dominants in a chain.

- **Mapping Tonal Harmony Pro** (iOS, Mac and Android app, mDecks Music). [App Store: Mapping Tonal Harmony Pro](https://apps.apple.com/app/id566767002)
  A map of a key in which each scale degree can be set as a tonic region with its own subdominant and dominant regions.
  It isolates tonicization as geography: a secondary dominant is a short trip to a neighbouring region, drawn as a path while the progression plays.
- **Mapping Tonal Harmony Pro, reviewed** (article, No Treble). [notreble.com review](https://www.notreble.com/?p=27563)
  A walk through the map and its play-along and reharmonisation uses.
  It isolates how the map reads to a working bassist.
- **Hookpad: secondary chords, including secondaries of borrowed chords** (product feature, Hooktheory). [Hooktheory forum: secondary subdominants](https://forum.hooktheory.com/t/secondary-subdominants/2097)
  A chord can be entered as V/x or IV/x of any target, and Hookpad 2 allows targets that are themselves borrowed.
  It isolates the "of": the hand picks an applied function and then the chord it points at.
- **David Bennett Piano, "Secondary dominants are the best thing since sliced bread! (Lesson 5)"** (YouTube, jazz piano course; link not verified).
  Lesson 5 of the same course.
  It isolates the applied dominant as one added chord before each diatonic target.
- **"TriTone Sub, Secondary Dominant, Backdoor Dominant, understand them all easily.."** (YouTube, channel not confirmed). [youtube.com/watch?v=Mx_IVhdknws](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Mx_IVhdknws)
  One visual matrix that places the three kinds of substitute dominant side by side.
  It isolates "ways to approach a target chord" as a single grid.
- **Extended dominants round the circle** (piano and guitar exercise; no single source).
  Dominant sevenths are played in a chain, each the V of the next, as in the bridge of rhythm changes.
  It isolates tonicization with no tonic: the hand feels every chord as leading somewhere and none as arrival.

### Modulation: pivot chord, chromatic, direct (tier 3)

Instruments mostly handle a key change as a setting, not a gesture.
The specialised prior art is route-finding software, catalogues of worked modulations, and close readings of single famous key changes.

- **Telepathic Instruments Orchid: Key Mode** (hardware chord synth, 2025). [Orchid manual](https://help.telepathicinstruments.com/en-US/articles/manual-268078)
  Key Mode locks a fixed chord to each key of the one-octave keyboard for the selected key.
  It isolates the key as a lock: changing key re-maps every chord at once, which makes a direct modulation easy and a pivot invisible.
- **Scaler 2: Modulation Pathways** (plugin feature, Scaler Music). [Plugin Boutique: Scaler 2](https://www.pluginboutique.com/products/6439-Scaler-2)
  Choose a destination scale and Scaler proposes routes to it of five kinds: progression, secondary scale, modal interchange, mediants and neo-Riemannian.
  It isolates modulation as a route between two keys, with the technique as the choice of road.
- **iReal Pro: automatic transposition each chorus** (product feature, Technimo). [iReal Pro: Advanced Practicing Techniques](https://www.irealpro.com/news/advanced-practicing-techniques)
  A practice setting shifts the chart or a looped section by a chosen interval every cycle, through all twelve keys.
  It isolates the direct modulation as a drill: the same bars arrive again, a step away, with no preparation.
- **Max Reger, "Modulation"** (book of worked examples, 1903; Dover reprint 2007). [Dover edition listing](https://imusic.se/books/9780486457321/max-reger-2007-modulation-bog)
  A short book of modulations from one key to each other key, each a few chords long with its analysis.
  It isolates the pivot: every example is the shortest road between two named keys.
- **The Truck Driver's Gear Change Hall of Shame** (website, gearchange.org; link not verified).
  A long-running catalogue of pop songs that lift the last chorus by a semitone or tone.
  It isolates one modulation type and collects it as a cliche.
- **"The most elegant key change in all of pop music"** (YouTube, Adam Neely). [youtube.com/watch?v=epqYft12nV4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=epqYft12nV4)
  An analysis of the G major to C-flat major change in Celine Dion's "All By Myself", through modal mixture and a common tone.
  It isolates one modulation and takes it apart voice by voice.
- **"Songs that Tritone key change"** (YouTube, David Bennett Piano). [youtube.com/watch?v=sDeSPliYMb0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sDeSPliYMb0)
  Examples of the widest possible key change.
  It isolates distance between keys as the thing heard.
- **"The most feared song in jazz, explained"** (YouTube, Vox Earworm with Adam Neely and Braxton Cook, 2018). [youtube.com/watch?v=62tIvfP9A2w](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=62tIvfP9A2w)
  "Giant Steps" explained on a circle of fifths as three keys a major third apart.
  It isolates rapid modulation as a triangle drawn on the circle.
- **Coltrane changes in all keys** (jazz exercise). [Wikipedia: Coltrane changes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coltrane_changes)
  The major-third cycle practised as a substitute for a plain ii-V-I.
  It isolates key change as a fingering problem at speed.
- **The capo** (guitar technique; no source).
  Moving a capo raises every open shape by the same interval.
  It isolates direct modulation as a physical slide of the whole instrument.

### Neapolitan, tritone substitution, chromatic mediants (tier 3)

Each of these is one chord with a chromatic root, and the prior art treats each as a colour to recognise: song lists, film-score recipes, and one jazz system that derives the substitutes from a diminished chord.

- **Scaler 2: the Mediants pathway** (plugin feature, Scaler Music). [Plugin Boutique: Scaler 2](https://www.pluginboutique.com/products/6439-Scaler-2)

  It isolates the third-related jump as a button to audition.
- **"Songs that use the Neapolitan Chord"** (YouTube, David Bennett Piano). [youtube.com/watch?v=cKpA16YjSKk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cKpA16YjSKk)
  A list of pop and classical uses of the flat-II chord.
  It isolates the chord by ear across styles.

### Neo-Riemannian theory and the Tonnetz (tier 3)

This is the richest subtopic: the Tonnetz is a ready-made playing surface, so it exists as hexagonal controllers, Eurorack modules, iOS apps, web lattices and geometric visualisers.
Most either lay out notes so that triads are triangles, or sequence the P, L and R moves directly.

- **Noise Engineering Tonnetz Sequent** (Eurorack module, 2015, discontinued). [Tonnetz Sequent](https://noiseengineering.us/products/tonnetz-sequent/)
  A triad generator whose gate inputs trigger the P, L and R transformations and which outputs root, third and fifth as three pitch voltages.
  It isolates the transformation itself: you do not sequence chords, you sequence moves, and a rhythm of triggers becomes a walk across the lattice.
- **Ornament and Crime: Harrington 1200 and Automatonnetz** (Eurorack firmware apps, open source). [ModularGrid: uO_C](https://modulargrid.net/e/after-later-audio-uo-c-1u)
  Harrington 1200 applies Tonnetz transformations to a triad on trigger inputs, and Automatonnetz joins those transforms to a "vector" sequencer over a grid of cells.
  It isolates the path: Automatonnetz stores a route across a small grid of transforms, so the chord depends on where the walk has been.
- **C-Thru Music AXiS-49 and AXiS-64** (hexagonal MIDI controllers, harmonic table layout after Peter Davies, 1983). [Sound On Sound: AXiS-49 review](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/c-thru-music-axis-49)
  From any button, up is a fifth, up-right a major third and up-left a minor third.
  It isolates the triad as a triangle one finger can cover, so major and minor are two orientations of one shape.
- **Striso board** (MPE instrument, Piers Titus van der Torren). [MusicTech: Striso Board](https://musictech.com/news/striso-board-mpe-instrument-kickstarter/)
  An isomorphic layout with pitch on one axis and the chain of fifths on the other, which keeps sharps and flats apart.
  It isolates fifths-distance as a direction of the hand, and stays correct under meantone tunings.
- **Intuitive Instruments Exquis** (MPE controller, 61 hexagonal keys). [SynthAnatomy: Exquis](https://synthanatomy.com/2022/10/exquis-new-expressive-mpe-and-cv-controller-with-hexagonal-layout.html)
  Semitones run across and thirds stack upward.
  It isolates the stacking of thirds as a vertical reach, with scales lit as paths.
- **Lumatone** (280-key isomorphic keyboard). [Lumatone: what makes an isomorphic keyboard special](https://help.lumatone.io/what-is-lumatone/what-makes-an-isomorphic-keyboard-special)
  A honeycomb of freely mappable keys on which any interval has one shape everywhere.
  It isolates transposition invariance, at a size that holds several octaves of a Tonnetz-like mapping.
- **Navichord** (iOS app). [App Store: Navichord](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/navichord-chord-sequencer/id916452748)
  A Tonnetz playing surface with one-finger triads, a chord sequencer and Roman numeral names.
  It isolates the triangle: a finger on the junction of three hexes plays the triad, and a neighbouring junction is a P, L or R move.
- **Musix Pro** (iOS app, Shiverware). [Musix Pro layouts](https://shiverware.com/musixpro/layouts.html)
  A hexagonal keyboard that can switch among the isomorphic layouts, harmonic table included.
  It isolates the layout as a variable, so the same chord can be compared as a shape in each.
- **Scaler: neo-Riemannian pathway** (plugin feature and user thread, Scaler Music). [Scaler forum: composing using neo-Riemannian theory](https://forum.scalermusic.com/t/composing-using-neo-reimannian-theory-sliding-harmony/8530)
  Scaler's modulation suggestions include a neo-Riemannian method, and users discuss composing with it.
  It isolates the moves as suggestions inside a chord-progression tool.
- **TonnetzViz** (web app, Ondrej Cifka). [github.com/cifkao/tonnetz-viz](https://github.com/cifkao/tonnetz-viz)
  A browser Tonnetz that lights up in real time from MIDI input or the computer keyboard.
  It isolates seeing: you play as usual and watch the shape your chord makes and where it moves.
- **Neo-Riemannian harmony visualiser** (web explainer, Oliver Steele). [viz.osteele.com/neo-riemannian](https://viz.osteele.com/neo-riemannian)
  A Tonnetz in which each P, L or R step flips a triangle, with the held and moving voices shown and the result played.
  It isolates voice-leading cost: every step shows which one note moved and by how many semitones.
- **The Tonnetz Environment (Web Hexachord)** (web platform, 2021). [A Tonnetz online interactive environment](https://europeanmusictheory.eu/a-tonnetz-online-interactive-environment/)
  A research and teaching site that plays pieces through different Tonnetze.
  It isolates the choice of lattice: the same music traces different shapes on different interval networks.
- **ChordGeometries** (Max/MSP program, Dmitri Tymoczko, 2006). [ChordGeometries 1.1](https://dmitri.mycpanel.princeton.edu/ChordGeometries.html)
  Chords entered on a MIDI keyboard become points in three-dimensional chord spaces, and voice leadings become paths between them.
  It isolates voice leading as distance in a space, including the Mobius strip of two-note chords.
- **ChordGeometries demo: Chopin through four-dimensional space** (Vimeo, Dmitri Tymoczko). [vimeo.com/20301089](https://vimeo.com/20301089)
  The E minor prelude shown as a path through the space of four-note chords.
  It isolates a whole piece as one continuous line.
- **Planet-4D** (visualisation model, Gilles Baroin with Emmanuel Amiot). [Music Theory Online: Old and New Isometries between Pc sets in the Planet-4D Model](https://www.mtosmt.org/issues/mto.15.21.3/mto.15.21.3.amiot-baroin.html)
  The twelve pitch classes placed on a four-dimensional hypersphere and animated.
  It isolates symmetry: transformations of chords are rotations of the model.
- **Neo-Riemannian theory in Sonic Pi** (code and forum thread). [in-thread.sonic-pi.net thread](https://in-thread.sonic-pi.net/t/neo-riemannian-theory-for-composition-analysis-and-math-education-in-sonic-pi/7329)
  P, L and R written as live-coded functions for composing and teaching.
  It isolates the moves as functions you type and compose.
- **"What The Heck Is Neo-Riemannian Analysis?"** (YouTube, 12tone). [youtube.com/watch?v=_VxN4rnOpho](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_VxN4rnOpho)
  A hand-drawn explainer of the three moves, with practice exercises on the channel's blog.
  It isolates the definitions, drawn one at a time.
- **Open Music Theory, "Neo-Riemannian Triadic Progressions"** (textbook chapter). [OMT chapter](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/neo-riemannian-triadic-progressions/)
  The chapter the benchmark was made against.
  It isolates the vocabulary on the page, with no playable surface.
- **PLR cycles at the piano** (exercise; no single source).
  Alternating two moves gives a closed loop of triads: P and L the hexatonic cycle of six, P and R the octatonic cycle of eight.
  It isolates parsimony under the hand: one finger moves by a step and the other two stay.

### Embellishing tones (tier 3)

Software mostly reduces this to "in the chord or not", shown by colour.
The named types (passing, neighbour, suspension) live in counterpoint teaching and in jazz rules for placing chord tones on the beat.

  Objects for three-note linear segments and verticalities used to find passing and neighbour tones in a score.

- **Avoiding objectionable parallels with non-chord tones** (open textbook section, Hutchinson). - **Barry Harris's half-step rules** (jazz technique). [barryharris.com: improvisation tutorial](https://barryharris.com/music-improvisation-tutorial)

- **Enclosures and approach notes** (bebop exercise; no single source).

  It isolates the suspension and its resolution as one finger's motion over a held shape.

  Fux's own first-species examples played with the score.
  It isolates note against note, the species with no dissonance at all.
- **"Magnificent Counterpoint in the Finale of Mozart's Jupiter Symphony"** (YouTube, Richard Atkinson). [youtube.com/watch?v=YTxYykhQZbI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YTxYykhQZbI)
  A colour-coded score that follows every theme, stretto and inversion in the movement.
  It isolates the subject: each theme keeps one colour wherever it appears.
- **"Five Simultaneous Themes: Mozart's Jupiter Symphony"** (YouTube, Richard Atkinson). [youtube.com/watch?v=zRBDUKmbvJA](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRBDUKmbvJA)
  The coda alone, where five themes sound together.
  It isolates invertible counterpoint as five colours stacked and re-stacked.
- **"Terrible Counterpoint in Mozart's 'A Musical Joke'"** (YouTube, Richard Atkinson). [youtube.com/watch?v=df3dr3cDOfo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=df3dr3cDOfo)
  Mozart's deliberate mistakes, pointed out one by one.
  It isolates the rules by showing them broken on purpose.
- **Music Animation Machine** (animated scores, Stephen Malinowski, since 1982). [Wikipedia: Stephen Malinowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Malinowski)
  Scrolling bar-graph scores of Bach fugues and much else, one colour per voice.
  It isolates the independence of voices for the eye, with no notation to read.
- **A cantus firmus a day** (paper or keyboard exercise, after Fux; no source).
  One given line and one added line, working through the species in order.
  It isolates one constraint at a time, which is what makes the method a set of microworlds already.

## Rhythm, form, texture and timbre

### Tempo (tier 1)

Tempo is nearly always a number on a dial, so the interesting prior art is the few things that make it something you hold, feel on the body, or hear several of at once.

- **Maelzel's metronome** (hardware, Johann Maelzel, patented 1815). [Wikipedia: Metronome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metronome)
  A clockwork pendulum with a sliding weight that ticks a set number of beats per minute.
  It isolates tempo as one number, and the hand sets it by sliding a weight along a marked rod while the eye watches the swing.
- **Technics SL-1200 pitch fader** (hardware feature, Technics, 1972 onward). [Wikipedia: Technics SL-1200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technics_SL-1200)
  A direct-drive turntable whose long fader changes the platter speed by a few percent.
  It makes tempo a continuous thing under one finger, and beatmatching two records is the whole skill built on it.
- **Soundbrenner Pulse** (wearable hardware, Soundbrenner, 2015 onward). [soundbrenner.com](https://www.soundbrenner.com/)
  A metronome worn like a watch that taps the beat on the skin instead of sounding it.
  It moves tempo from the ear to touch, set by tapping or turning the face.
- **Ableton Link** (product feature and open protocol, Ableton, 2016). [ableton.com/link](https://www.ableton.com/en/link/)
  A network protocol that keeps the tempo and beat of several apps and devices together with no leader.
  It isolates tempo as a shared value any participant can nudge, and everyone follows.
- **Ableton Learning Music, "Tempo and genre"** (web explainer, Ableton, 2017). [Tempo and genre](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/make-beats/tempo-and-genre.html)
  One page where the same drum pattern is played at different speeds and named as different genres.
  It isolates tempo by holding the pattern fixed and letting a single slider change what style the beat seems to be.
- **Poème symphonique for 100 metronomes** (composition, György Ligeti, 1962). [Wikipedia: Poème symphonique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po%C3%A8me_Symphonique)
  A hundred wound metronomes set to different tempos are started together and left to run down.
  It is tempo with everything else removed, heard as a cloud that thins into single pulses.
- **Studies for Player Piano** (compositions for a machine, Conlon Nancarrow, 1948 to 1992). [Wikipedia: Studies for Player Piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studies_for_Player_Piano_(Nancarrow))
  Hand-punched piano rolls in which voices play the same line at tempos in ratios such as 3:4 or even irrational ones.
  It treats tempo as something a single voice owns, so several tempos sound at once and can be seen as distances on the roll.
- **Beatmatching by ear** (technique, DJ practice). [Wikipedia: Beatmatching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatmatching)
  The DJ exercise of bringing one record's tempo to another's using the pitch fader and a nudge of the platter.
  It trains tempo as a difference you hear as drift and correct with the hand.
- **Rubato practice** (technique, piano and voice). [Wikipedia: Tempo rubato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tempo_rubato)
  The player stretches and recovers time within a phrase while the underlying pulse is kept in mind.
  It isolates tempo as something bent locally, which a fixed BPM control cannot show.

### Simple and compound meter, time signatures, odd meters (tier 1)

Prior art splits between grids whose length or grouping can be changed and catalogues of songs that teach an odd meter by example.

- **Teenage Engineering OP-Z** (hardware sequencer, Teenage Engineering, 2018). [OP-Z](https://teenage.engineering/products/op-z)
  A sixteen-track sequencer in which every track has its own length, from 1 to 144 steps, and its own speed.
  It isolates bar length as a per-track setting, so an odd meter is made by shortening a row of buttons.
- **Korg Volca "active step"** (hardware feature, Korg, 2013 onward). [Korg volca beats](https://www.korg.com/us/products/dj/volca_beats/)
  A mode on the Volca boxes in which steps of the sixteen are switched out of the loop while it plays.
  It makes meter a matter of which steps exist, so a bar of 7 or 5 is two button presses away from 4/4.
- **Korg Electribe "last step"** (hardware feature, Korg). [Korg electribe](https://www.korg.com/us/products/dj/electribe/)
  A per-part setting for where the pattern turns round.
  It isolates the bar's length from the pattern's content, and different parts can turn round in different places.
- **Chrome Music Lab: Rhythm** (web experiment, Google Creative Lab, 2016). [Rhythm](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/Rhythm/)
  Cartoon animals play a three-row grid, and each screen is a different meter (3, 4, 5 and 6 beats).
  It isolates the number of beats in the bar, and the hand meets it as the same grid at a different width.
- **Groove Pizza** (web app, NYU Music Experience Design Lab, 2016). [musedlab.org/groovepizza](https://musedlab.org/groovepizza)
  A circular drum sequencer where the number of slices in the bar can be changed and hits are joined into polygons.
  It shows meter as how a circle is cut, and a rhythm's evenness as the shape drawn inside it.
- **musictheory.net, "Simple and Compound Meter"** (interactive lesson, Ricci Adams). [Lesson 15](https://www.musictheory.net/lessons/15)
  A stepped animation that divides the same bar by twos and by threes.
  It isolates the simple and compound distinction as how a beat is split, one click at a time.
- **Rhythm Doctor** (game, 7th Beat Games, early access 2021). [Wikipedia: Rhythm Doctor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_Doctor)
  A one-button rhythm game about pressing on the seventh beat, with later levels in irregular time signatures and polyrhythms.
  It isolates counting to the bar's end, with one thumb and almost no visual help.
- **"Songs that use 5/4 time"** (video, David Bennett Piano, YouTube). [youtube.com/watch?v=KQ76-WiFTlo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KQ76-WiFTlo)
  A run of well-known songs in 5/4, each counted aloud over the recording.
  It isolates one meter and teaches it by recognition instead of by notation.
- **"Songs that use 11/8 time" and "Songs that use 13/8 time"** (videos, David Bennett Piano, YouTube). [11/8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wS9t4Ftkz7Q), [13/8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C9NJeteDIys)
  The same format for meters long enough that they must be heard as groups of twos and threes.
  They isolate how a long odd bar is grouped, shown as counts on screen.
- **Yogev Gabay** (YouTube channel, drummer). [youtube.com/@YogevGabay](https://www.youtube.com/@YogevGabay)
  A drummer's channel that takes apart odd-meter and polymetric passages from progressive metal and jazz, with the counts drawn over the playing.
  It isolates the grouping inside an odd bar as something the limbs play; no single video title was verified.
- **Dave Brubeck Quartet, "Time Out"** (album, 1959). [Wikipedia: Time Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Out_(album))
  A jazz record made as a set of studies, each tune in an unusual meter (5/4, 9/8 grouped 2+2+2+3).
  It is the canonical case of one meter per piece, learned by playing along.
- **Aksak counting, short and long** (technique, Balkan and Turkish practice). [Wikipedia: Aksak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksak)
  Odd meters are felt as a chain of short (2) and long (3) beats, often learned as dance steps.
  It isolates grouping from the number on the time signature, and the feet meet it first.
- **Tala kept with claps and waves** (technique, Indian classical music). [Wikipedia: Tala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tala_(music))
  A metric cycle is marked with a fixed sequence of claps, finger counts and waves of the hand.
  It makes the place in the cycle a gesture, so the meter is held in the hands while something else is sung.
- **Takadimi** (counting system, Hoffman, Pelto and White, 1996). [Wikipedia: Takadimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takadimi)
  A set of rhythm syllables in which each position within the beat has its own sound, different for simple and compound time.
  It isolates the position inside a beat as a spoken syllable.

### Syncopation, dots and ties (tier 2)

Syncopation is usually shown by rotating or displacing a fixed pattern against a fixed pulse, on a wheel, a necklace or a book of exercises.

- **Roland TB-303 step programming** (hardware, Roland, 1981). [Wikipedia: Roland TB-303](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_TB-303)
  A bass sequencer where pitch and time are entered separately, and each step is a note, a tie or a rest, with accent and slide as flags.
  It isolates the tie as its own kind of step, entered with its own button.
- **Noise Engineering Zularic Repetitor** (Eurorack module, Noise Engineering). [Zularic Repetitor](https://noiseengineering.us/products/zularic-repetitor)
  A trigger generator holding a mother rhythm and three children that are offset against it by knobs.
  It isolates displacement: a knob slides a known pattern off the beat by a chosen amount.
- **"A different way to visualize rhythm"** (TED-Ed lesson and video, John Varney, 2014). [TED-Ed](https://ed.ted.com/lessons/a-different-way-to-visualize-rhythm-john-varney), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2UphAzryVpY)
  Rhythms are drawn on concentric wheels, and turning a wheel turns one familiar pattern into another.
  It isolates where the accents fall against the main beat, shown as a rotation.
- **Rhythm Necklace** (iOS app, Meara O'Reilly and Sam Tarakajian, 2015). [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rhythm-necklace-geometric-sequencer/id954669874), [Ethan Hein's write-up](https://ethanhein.com/wp/2015/rhythm-necklace/)
  A sequencer of up to four circular "necklaces" whose beads are spread by an algorithm and then flipped and rotated by hand.
  It isolates the evenness and rotation of a pattern, after Godfried Toussaint's geometry of rhythm.
- **Ableton Learning Music, "Backbeats"** (web explainer, Ableton, 2017). [Backbeats](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/make-beats/backbeats.html)
  A page of the beats chapter that moves snares onto beats two and four and then lets the reader move them off.
  It isolates which beats carry the weight, in a grid the reader edits.
- **Rhythm Heaven** (game series, Nintendo, 2006 onward). [Wikipedia: Rhythm Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_Heaven)
  Short minigames that are each one rhythmic idea, many of them about off-beat entries, judged by ear with deliberately misleading pictures.
  It isolates the off-beat as a response to be made with one button.
- **"The secret rhythm behind Radiohead's 'Videotape'"** (video, Vox Earworm, 2017). [youtube.com/watch?v=p_IHotHxIl8](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p_IHotHxIl8)
  An explainer showing that a piano part that sounds on the beat is written an eighth note off it.
  It isolates syncopation as a matter of where the listener believes the beat is.
- **Clave and tresillo** (patterns, Afro-Cuban practice). [Wikipedia: Clave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clave_(rhythm)), [Wikipedia: Tresillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tresillo_(rhythm))
  Short asymmetric patterns, played on two sticks, that the rest of an ensemble is organised around.
  They isolate syncopation as a fixed key pattern, learned by clapping it against a foot on the beat.
- **Ted Reed's "Syncopation"** (drum method book, Ted Reed, 1958). [Wikipedia: Ted Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Reed)
  A book of plain one-line rhythms that drummers read in many set ways, one limb keeping time while another plays the line.
  It isolates the syncopated line from what is done with it.
- **Metronome displacement** (exercise, drums and guitar).
  The click is heard as the "and", or the "e", or the last triplet note, while the player keeps the bar. (No link; a common practice-room exercise.)
  It isolates the player's own pulse by moving the reference off it.

### Swing and groove / microtiming (tier 2)

Here the prior art is mostly a single number (a swing percentage) or a per-step nudge, with a small body of writing and video about what Dilla did to both.

- **Linn LM-1 and MPC swing** (hardware feature, Roger Linn, 1979 onward). [Attack Magazine interview](https://attackmagazine.com/features/interview/roger-linn-swing-groove-magic-mpc-timing/2)
  Linn's drum machines delay every second sixteenth note by a percentage, where 50% is straight and 66% is a triplet.
  It isolates swing as one ratio, and the interview gives his reasons for the values between.
- **Elektron micro timing** (hardware feature, Elektron). [Elektronauts thread](https://www.elektronauts.com/t/digitakt-micro-timing-capabilities/226313)
  Any step on a Digitakt or its siblings can be pushed earlier or later than the grid by small fractions of a step.
  It isolates timing per note, set by holding a step and turning.
- **Polyend Play** (hardware groovebox, Polyend, 2022). [polyend.com/play](https://polyend.com/play/)
  A sample and MIDI groovebox with per-step micro-moves, chance and per-track swing on a grid of pads.
  It puts the nudge on the same surface as the steps, so timing is edited like pitch.
- **Roland TR-909 shuffle and flam** (hardware feature, Roland, 1983). [Wikipedia: Roland TR-909](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_TR-909)
  Two global settings: one delays the off-beat sixteenths and one splits a hit into two close strokes.
  They isolate two kinds of timing looseness as stepped amounts for the whole pattern.
- **Ableton Live Groove Pool** (product feature, Ableton). [Live manual: Using Grooves](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/using-grooves/)
  Timing and velocity templates, some lifted from MPCs or from recordings, that are applied to any clip in adjustable amounts.
  It isolates feel as an object apart from the notes, with dials for timing, randomness and velocity.
- **GrooVAE** (machine-learning model and plugin, Google Magenta, 2019). [magenta.tensorflow.org/groovae](https://magenta.tensorflow.org/groovae)
  A model trained on drummers' performances that takes a quantised pattern and returns it with human timing and dynamics.
  It isolates what is lost by quantising, and gives it back from data instead of from a rule.
- **Groove Pizza's swing slider** (web app feature, NYU MusEDLab). [musedlab.org/groovepizza](https://musedlab.org/groovepizza)
  The circular grid's slices visibly change width as swing is added.
  It shows swing to the eye as uneven slices of the circle.
- **"I made a new track for teaching swing"** (blog post, Ethan Hein). [ethanhein.substack.com](https://ethanhein.substack.com/p/i-made-a-new-track-for-teaching-swing)
  A music-education writer's track built so that the swing amount changes while everything else is held.
  It isolates swing by automating it inside one piece of music.
- **"How J Dilla humanized his MPC3000"** (video, Vox Earworm, 2017). [youtube.com/watch?v=SENzTt3ftiU](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SENzTt3ftiU)
  A ten-minute explainer of how Dilla played his drums unquantised and where each hit falls against the grid.
  It isolates microtiming by drawing the hits against the lines they miss.
- **"Dilla Time"** (book, Dan Charnas, 2022). [Wikipedia: Dilla Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilla_Time)
  A biography that argues Dilla made a third feel beside straight and swung time, with grid diagrams of the conflict between them.
  It isolates the idea of two feels sounding at once.
- **Swing ratio against tempo** (technique and research, jazz ride cymbal). [Wikipedia: Swing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_(jazz_performance_style))
  Jazz drummers play a longer long note at slow tempos and nearly even eighths at fast ones.
  It shows that swing is not one ratio, which a single percentage hides.
- **Metronome on two and four** (exercise, jazz).
  The click is treated as the backbeat only, and the player supplies beats one and three. (No link; a standard jazz practice method.)
  It isolates the player's placement of the off-beat eighth, with nothing to lean on.

### Borrowed divisions (triplets, tuplets) and polyrhythm (tier 3)

Polyrhythm has the richest set of microworlds: instruments that derive rhythms from ratios, circular and bouncing-ball pictures, and spoken phrases that carry the composite.

- **Rhythmicon** (electro-mechanical instrument, Leon Theremin for Henry Cowell, 1930). [Wikipedia: Rhythmicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythmicon)
  A keyboard whose sixteen keys each sound a pitch from the harmonic series, repeated at the rate of its harmonic number.
  It isolates the identity of ratio in pitch and in rhythm: holding keys 2 and 3 gives both a fifth and three against two.
- **Moog Subharmonicon** (hardware synth, Moog, 2020). [moogmusic.com](https://www.moogmusic.com/synthesizers/subharmonicon)
  A synth with four rhythm generators that divide the clock by whole numbers and are summed onto two short sequencers.
  It makes polyrhythm four knobs, each a divisor.
- **ALM Pamela's Pro Workout** (Eurorack clock module, ALM Busy Circuits). [busycircuits.com](https://busycircuits.com/pages/alm034)
  A clock with eight outputs, each a multiple or division of the tempo, including triplet and other odd factors.
  It isolates the subdivision as a number per output.
- **Nestup** (notation language and Max for Live device, Sam Tarakajian and Alex Van Gils, 2020). [nestup.cutelab.nyc](https://nestup.cutelab.nyc/)
  A small text language for dividing a span of time into any number of parts, and those parts again.
  It isolates the tuplet, nested to any depth, written as brackets.
- **TidalCycles and Strudel mini-notation** (live-coding languages, Alex McLean and others). [Tidal mini-notation](https://tidalcycles.org/docs/reference/mini_notation/), [strudel.cc](https://strudel.cc/)
  A pattern language in which a cycle is divided by however many things are written in it.
  It makes the triplet the default case: three names in a bracket are three equal parts.
- **Groove Pizzeria** (web app, Tyler Bisson at NYU MusEDLab). [MusEDLab post](https://wp.nyu.edu/musedlab/?p=233)
  Two Groove Pizzas side by side with different numbers of slices, played together.
  It isolates the relation between two cycles, shown as two circles.
- **Bounce Metronome** (Windows software, Robert Walker). [Polyrhythms page](https://bouncemetronome.com/features/pro/polyrhythms)
  A metronome that shows each part of a polyrhythm as a ball bouncing under gravity.
  It lets the eye anticipate each stream's beat from the ball's fall.
- **"Harmonic Polyrhythms Explained!"** (video, Adam Neely, 2016). [TheTVDB listing](https://thetvdb.com/series/adam-neely/episodes/8705682)
  A polyrhythm is sped up until its two pulses become two pitches an interval apart.
  It isolates the sameness of rhythm ratio and interval. (YouTube link not verified; the listing confirms the title.)
- **"New Horizons in Music: Polyrhythms"** (talk, Adam Neely at Ableton Loop, 2017). [Ableton blog](https://www.ableton.com/en/blog/new-horizons-music-polyrhythms/)
  A lecture that carries the same ratios from rhythm through pitch to colour.
  It isolates ratio as the thing shared across senses.
- **"7:11 Polyrhythms"** (video, Adam Neely, 2019). [youtube.com/watch?v=U9CgR2Y6XO4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U9CgR2Y6XO4)
  How to work out and play seven against eleven.
  It isolates the method (find the common grid, then place both streams on it) with an absurd case.
- **"How the triplet flow took over rap"** (video, Vox Earworm, 2017). (link not verified)
  An explainer on rappers placing three syllables to the beat over a duple track.
  It isolates the borrowed division in speech, drawn as syllables on a grid.
- **"Nice cup of tea" and "pass the golden butter"** (mnemonics, piano and drum teaching). [Wikipedia: Polyrhythm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyrhythm)
  Spoken phrases whose syllables fall where the two hands of 3:2 or 4:3 fall.
  They isolate the composite rhythm, so two streams are learned as one sentence between the hands.
- **Konnakol** (vocal technique, Carnatic music). [Wikipedia: Konnakol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konnakol)
  Rhythm spoken in syllable groups (ta-ki-ta, ta-ka-di-mi) while the hand keeps the tala.
  It isolates the regrouping of a steady stream into threes, fives and sevens against a fixed cycle.

### Hypermeter and metrical dissonance (hemiola, polymeter, phasing) (tier 3)

The clearest examples give each voice its own loop length or let one copy of a pattern slip against another, so the dissonance is seen as two cycles drifting.

- **Monome Kria** (grid sequencer firmware, monome). [Kria docs](https://monome.org/docs/ansible/kria/)
  A sequencer in which each track, and each parameter of a track (trigger, note, octave, duration), can have its own loop.
  It isolates loop length per parameter, set by dragging a range on the grid.
- **OP-Z track length and speed** (hardware feature, Teenage Engineering, 2018). [OP-Z](https://teenage.engineering/products/op-z)
  Tracks of different lengths run together and come back into line only after many bars.
  It isolates polymeter as unequal rows of the same buttons.
- **4ms Rotating Clock Divider** (Eurorack module, 4ms). [4mscompany.com](https://4mscompany.com/rcd.php)
  Eight outputs divide a clock by 1 to 8, and a voltage rotates which output gets which division.
  It isolates the layering of pulses that makes hypermeter, and lets the layers be swapped while playing.
- **Fugue Machine** (iOS app, Alexandernaut, 2015). [alexandernaut.com/fuguemachine](https://alexandernaut.com/fuguemachine/)
  One piano roll read by up to four playheads, each at its own speed, direction and transposition.
  It isolates augmentation and displacement of one line against itself.
- **Tidal's polymetric braces** (language feature, TidalCycles). [Tidal mini-notation](https://tidalcycles.org/docs/reference/mini_notation/)
  Sequences written in curly braces share a step length instead of a cycle length.
  It isolates the difference between polyrhythm and polymeter as a choice of bracket.
- **Steve Reich's Clapping Music app** (iOS game, London Sinfonietta, Queen Mary University of London and Touchpress, 2015). [Nonesuch announcement](https://nonesuch.com/journal/steve-reich-clapping-music-app-released)
  The player taps one of the two parts of Reich's piece while the other shifts by one eighth note every few bars.
  It isolates phase: one pattern against itself at every possible offset, held with a thumb.
- **Whitney Music Box** (web animation, Jim Bumgardner, 2006). [whitneymusicbox.org](https://whitneymusicbox.org/)
  Dots circle at speeds in whole-number ratios and sound a note each time they cross a line.
  It shows phasing of many cycles at once, with the moments of alignment visible as spokes.
- **Pianophase.com** (web visualisation of Reich's Piano Phase, Alexander Chen). (link not verified; the site did not respond when checked)
  Two rotating figures draw the two pianists' patterns as one slowly moves ahead.
  It isolates gradual phase as two drawings pulling apart.
- **Piano Phase and In C** (compositions, Steve Reich 1967 and Terry Riley 1964). [Wikipedia: Piano Phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_Phase), [Wikipedia: In C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_C)
  Pieces whose whole score is one or a few short cells that players move through out of step.
  They are metrical dissonance as an ensemble game with written rules.
- **Hemiola counting** (technique, Baroque dance to "America"). [Wikipedia: Hemiola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemiola)
  Six eighths are counted alternately as two threes and three twos.
  It isolates regrouping with no change of tempo, learned by clapping both groupings.
- **Meshuggah-style polymeter** (technique, metal drumming). [Wikipedia: Meshuggah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meshuggah)
  Guitars and kick drum cycle an odd-length riff while the cymbal and snare keep 4/4, and the riff is cut off at a round number of bars.
  It isolates polymeter between limbs, with hypermeter as the thing that resets it.

### 20th-century rhythmic techniques (tier 4)

Little is built for these on purpose; the nearest things are sequencers that happen to separate pitch loops from rhythm loops, and machines made for music no hand could play.

- **Kria as talea and color** (grid sequencer firmware, monome). [Kria docs](https://monome.org/docs/ansible/kria/)
  With a note loop of one length and a trigger loop of another, the same pitches come round on different rhythms.
  It is isorhythm by construction, set as two ranges on a grid.
- **Moog Subharmonicon** (hardware synth, Moog, 2020). [moogmusic.com](https://www.moogmusic.com/synthesizers/subharmonicon)
  Its four-step sequences are advanced by sums of clock divisions, so pitch order and rhythm cycle independently.
  It isolates a short pitch cycle against a longer rhythmic one.
- **Nancarrow's player piano** (instrument and studies, Conlon Nancarrow). [Wikipedia: Studies for Player Piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studies_for_Player_Piano_(Nancarrow))
  Rolls punched by hand with tempo canons, accelerations and irrational tempo ratios.
  It isolates rhythm as measured distance on paper, free of what a player can count.
- **Nestup** (notation language, Sam Tarakajian and Alex Van Gils, 2020). [nestup.cutelab.nyc](https://nestup.cutelab.nyc/)
  Nested tuplets and arbitrary proportions written as text and played as MIDI.
  It makes the rhythms of the New Complexity writable without notation.
- **Bounce Metronome's irrational and additive meters** (software, Robert Walker). [bouncemetronome.com](https://bouncemetronome.com/features/pro/polyrhythms)
  The same bouncing-ball metronome set to additive bars and to meters with odd denominators.
  It isolates the click for meters that ordinary metronomes cannot count.
- **Non-retrogradable rhythm** (technique, Olivier Messiaen). [Wikipedia: Non-retrogradable rhythm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-retrogradable_rhythm), [Wikipedia: Quatuor pour la fin du temps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quatuor_pour_la_fin_du_temps)
  A rhythm that reads the same forwards and backwards, used with added values and isorhythmic layers in the Quartet for the End of Time.
  It isolates symmetry in time, most easily seen as durations drawn as a row of blocks.
- **Isorhythm** (technique, medieval motet to Messiaen). [Wikipedia: Isorhythm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isorhythm)
  A fixed rhythm (talea) and a fixed melody (color) of different lengths are repeated until they realign.
  It isolates the independence of a pitch cycle and a duration cycle.
- **Metric modulation** (technique, Elliott Carter; drum-kit practice). [Wikipedia: Metric modulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_modulation)
  A subdivision in the old tempo is taken as the beat of the new one.
  It isolates the pivot between two tempos, practised on the kit by keeping the hands' rate and changing the feet's grouping.
- **Additive and divisive rhythm** (concept and exercise). [Wikipedia: Additive rhythm and divisive rhythm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additive_rhythm_and_divisive_rhythm)
  Building a bar by adding small units (Glass's early pieces, Bartók's Bulgarian rhythms) instead of dividing a large one.
  It isolates the unit pulse as the thing counted, clapped as groups that grow by one.

### Drumbeats (tier 3)

The drum machine is itself the microworld: a grid of a few sounds over a bar, and later attempts replace the grid with a map, a circle or a virtual player.

- **Roland TR-808** (hardware drum machine, Roland, 1980). [Wikipedia: Roland TR-808](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_TR-808)
  Sixteen buttons along the front are the sixteenths of a bar, lit where the chosen drum plays.
  It fixed the picture of a beat as rows of steps, entered one drum at a time.
- **Linn LM-1** (hardware drum machine, Roger Linn, 1980). [Wikipedia: Linn LM-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linn_LM-1)
  The first programmable drum machine with sampled drums, played in on pads in real time and then quantised and swung.
  It isolates the beat as something performed and then corrected.
- **Pocket Operator PO-12 rhythm** (hardware, Teenage Engineering, 2015). [Pocket Operators](https://teenage.engineering/products/po)
  A bare circuit board with sixteen buttons, sixteen drum sounds and sixteen punch-in effects.
  It isolates the beat and its fills: effects are held down in time, so variation is played.
- **Mutable Instruments Grids** (Eurorack module, Émilie Gillet, 2012). [Grids documentation](https://pichenettes.github.io/mutable-instruments-documentation/modules/grids/)
  A "topographic" drum sequencer: a map of patterns learned from many drum loops, read by X and Y, with a density knob per drum.
  It isolates the style of a beat as a position, and its busyness as a fill amount.
- **Korg Volca Beats** (hardware drum machine, Korg, 2013). [Korg volca beats](https://www.korg.com/us/products/dj/volca_beats/)
  A small analogue drum box with a stutter knob and steps that can be switched out.
  It isolates the pattern and two ways of bending it by hand.
- **Logic Pro Drummer** (product feature, Apple, 2013). [Logic Pro user guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/logicpro/welcome/mac)
  A virtual drummer steered by a puck on a pad: right for more complex, up for louder, with fills and swing as dials.
  It isolates a drum part's complexity and intensity as two axes under one finger.
- **Groove Pizza** (web app, NYU MusEDLab, 2016). [musedlab.org/groovepizza](https://musedlab.org/groovepizza)
  Kick, snare and hat as three rings of one circle, with presets of well-known beats.
  It shows a drumbeat as three polygons, so backbeat and four-on-the-floor are recognisable shapes.
- **Ableton Learning Music, "Make beats"** (web explainer, Ableton, 2017). [Make beats](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/make-beats/make-beats.html)
  A chapter of small grids that rebuild named beats and let the reader change them.
  It isolates each drum's role, one page at a time.
- **The Infinite Drum Machine** (web experiment, Manny Tan and Kyle McDonald with Google, 2016). [Experiments with Google](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/drum-machine)
  Thousands of everyday sounds laid out by similarity, any four of which can be dropped into a step sequencer.
  It holds the beat fixed and isolates what the drums are made of.
- **drumbit** (web drum machine). [drumbit.app](https://drumbit.app/)
  A plain browser step sequencer with kits, swing and a shareable pattern.
  It is the generic grid, useful as the baseline the odder things depart from.
- **Funklet** (web app, Jack Stratton). (link not verified; the site did not respond when checked)
  Famous funk drum breaks shown as editable grids with ghost notes and the original audio.
  It isolates one real drummer's bar, including the dynamics a plain grid drops.
- **The Amen break** (sample, The Winstons, 1969). [Wikipedia: Amen break](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amen_break)
  Four bars of drums that became the raw material of jungle and breakbeat by being cut and reordered.
  It isolates a beat as a thing to be sliced, with each slice on a pad.
- **PAS 40 International Drum Rudiments** (exercises, Percussive Arts Society). [pas.org rudiments](https://www.pas.org/resources/rudiments)
  The standard list of sticking patterns (rolls, paradiddles, flams, drags).
  They isolate the hand-to-hand vocabulary beneath any beat.
- **"Boots and cats"** (mnemonic, beatboxing).
  The phrase said in rhythm gives kick, hat, snare, hat. (No link; a common teaching phrase.)
  It isolates the basic rock beat as four syllables.

### Ground bass, loops and looping as form (tier 3)

Loopers are the hardware of this topic, and they differ mainly in how many independent loops there are and whether they may differ in length.

- **Frippertronics** (tape technique, Robert Fripp after Terry Riley and Pauline Oliveros, 1970s). [Wikipedia: Frippertronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frippertronics)
  Two tape recorders share one long loop of tape, so what is played returns seconds later and fades over many passes.
  It isolates the loop as slow decay, with the delay set by the distance between the machines.
- **Boss RC-505 Loop Station** (hardware looper, Boss, 2013; mkII 2021). [RC-505mkII](https://www.boss.info/global/products/rc-505mk2/)
  A tabletop looper with five tracks, each with its own fader and record button, made for beatboxers and singers.
  It isolates layers as five lanes that are built and dropped by hand.
- **Ed Sheeran's Chewie II** (custom looper, Trevor Dawkins for Ed Sheeran). [Reverb news](https://reverb.com/news/ed-sheerans-chewie-looper-crashes-equipboard-gear-site)
  A pedalboard driving a software looper with four tracks for guitar, voice and percussion, with screens showing each loop's state.
  It isolates song form built from loops alone: tracks are muted and brought back to make verse and chorus.
- **Electro-Harmonix 45000** (hardware looper, Electro-Harmonix, 2012; discontinued 2024). [ehx.com](https://www.ehx.com/products/ehx-45000)
  A looper laid out like a four-track tape recorder, with faders, a mixdown track and quantise.
  It isolates the loop as a multitrack tape that never ends.
- **Akai MPC as a looper** (hardware technique, Roger Linn demonstration, 2025). [Synthtopia](https://www.synthtopia.com/content/2025/07/28/roger-linn-demonstrates-how-to-use-the-akai-mpc-as-the-ultimate-looper/)
  Linn shows the MPC's sequencer used as a looper of notes instead of audio.
  It is the same idea as a loop kept as notes, so it can be edited after it is played.
- **mlr** (monome grid and norns script, Brian Crabtree). [norns.community/mlr](https://norns.community/mlr)
  Loops are laid across rows of a button grid, and pressing a button jumps the loop to that point.
  It isolates position within a loop as a place under the finger.
- **Loopy Pro** (iOS app, Michael Tyson, 2021). [loopypro.com](https://loopypro.com/)
  A looper of circular clips on a canvas the player lays out, with loops of different lengths and follow actions.
  It shows each loop as a turning circle, so its length and phase are visible.
- **GarageBand Live Loops** (product feature, Apple, 2016). [GarageBand for iPad guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/garageband-ipad/welcome/ipados)
  A grid of loop cells on the iPad, played by tapping cells and whole columns.
  It isolates the choice of which loops sound now, as a touch grid.
- **Endlesss** (collaborative loop app, Tim Exile, 2020; since closed). (link not verified)
  Players took turns adding eight-bar loops ("riffs") to a shared, ever-changing jam.
  It isolated the loop as a turn in a conversation.
- **The Infinite Jukebox** (web experiment, Paul Lamere, 2012; reborn as the Eternal Jukebox). [FlowingData write-up](https://flowingdata.com/2012/11/19/infinite-jukebox-plays-your-favorite-songs-forever/)
  Any song is analysed for beats that sound alike, drawn as a ring with arcs between them, and played forever by jumping along the arcs.
  It turns a whole song into a loop and shows where it could fold onto itself.
- **Ground bass, passacaglia and the lament bass** (technique, Baroque). [Wikipedia: Ground bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_bass), [Wikipedia: Lament bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lament_bass)
  A short bass line repeats unchanged while the parts above vary.
  It is the loop as a compositional rule, practised by improvising divisions over a fixed bass.
- **Pachelbel's Canon as a loop** (piece and exercise). [Wikipedia: Pachelbel's Canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_in_D)
  Eight bass notes repeated twenty-eight times under a three-part canon.
  It isolates how much change a fixed loop can carry, and is the usual first ground for students.

### Form in pop music (tier 3)

Form is mostly shown either as a row of labelled blocks to be arranged or as a picture of where a song repeats itself.

- **Ableton Session View scenes** (product feature, Ableton, 2001). [Live manual: Session View](https://www.ableton.com/en/manual/session-view/)
  Clips sit in a grid whose rows ("scenes") are launched together, each row a section of the song.
  It isolates the section as a row and form as the order the rows are fired in.
- **Ableton Learning Music, "Song structure"** (web explainer, Ableton, 2017). [Play with song structures](https://learningmusic.ableton.com/song-structure/song-structure.html)
  Blocks of four, eight and sixteen bars are switched on and off along a timeline of a whole track.
  It isolates form as the bar-by-bar presence of parts.
- **Hooktheory TheoryTab sections** (product feature, Hooktheory). [TheoryTab](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab), [forum thread on sections](https://forum.hooktheory.com/t/suggestion-add-song-structure-to-hooktheory-database/5182)
  A database of songs analysed as chords and melody, stored section by section (verse, chorus, bridge).
  It isolates the section as the unit of analysis, though the forum shows users asking for whole-song structure.
- **iReal Pro** (app, Technimo, 2008 onward). [irealpro.com](https://www.irealpro.com/)
  Chord charts with rehearsal letters, repeats and codas, played back by a virtual rhythm section.
  It isolates form as a chart's road map, the way jazz players read AABA.
- **Band-in-a-Box** (software, PG Music, 1990). [Wikipedia: Band-in-a-Box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Band-in-a-Box)
  The user types chords into a grid of bars and marks each bar as an "a" or "b" substyle.
  It isolates the section change as a marker that makes the band play differently.
- **SongSim** (web tool, Colin Morris, 2017). [colinmorris.github.io/SongSim](https://colinmorris.github.io/SongSim/)
  A song's lyrics are drawn as a self-similarity matrix, a dot wherever two words match.
  It shows form as squares and diagonals, with choruses appearing as repeated blocks.
- **"Are Pop Lyrics Getting More Repetitive?"** (interactive essay, Colin Morris for The Pudding, 2017). [pudding.cool](https://pudding.cool/2017/05/song-repetition/)
  Repetition in lyrics is measured by how well they compress and charted across decades and artists.
  It isolates repetition as one number per song.
- **The Shape of Song** (web visualisation, Martin Wattenberg, 2001). [bewitched.com/song.html](https://www.bewitched.com/song.html)
  Arc diagrams join every repeated passage in a piece of music.
  It shows the whole form at a glance as nested arches.
- **Rick Beato, "What Makes This Song Great?"** (YouTube series). [youtube.com/@RickBeato](https://www.youtube.com/@RickBeato)
  Episodes go through one song section by section from its multitrack stems.
  It isolates what each section adds or takes away; individual episode links were not verified.
- **Song Exploder and Switched on Pop** (podcasts). [Wikipedia: Song Exploder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_Exploder), [Wikipedia: Switched on Pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switched_on_Pop)
  One song per episode, taken apart by its makers or by a musicologist and a songwriter.
  They isolate a song's parts in the order it was built, by ear alone.
- **Twelve-bar blues and thirty-two-bar form** (forms and exercises). [Wikipedia: Twelve-bar blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-bar_blues), [Wikipedia: Thirty-two-bar form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-two-bar_form)
  Two fixed plans of chords and sections that players memorise and cycle.
  They isolate form as a known count of bars, which is what makes a jam session possible.
- **Jamey Aebersold play-alongs** (records and books, 1967 onward). [Wikipedia: Jamey Aebersold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamey_Aebersold)
  A rhythm section plays a tune's form over and over with no soloist.
  It isolates keeping one's place in the form, since the record will not wait.

### Classical form (tier 4)

Almost nothing here is an instrument; the prior art is listening guides, annotated animations and one eighteenth-century game.

- **Musikalisches Würfelspiel** (dice game, attributed to Mozart and others, 18th century). [Wikipedia: Musikalisches Würfelspiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musikalisches_W%C3%BCrfelspiel)
  Dice choose one of several precomposed bars for each position of a minuet.
  It isolates the slots of a phrase: every bar is interchangeable only with bars of the same function.
- **Variations Audio Timeliner** (software, Indiana University). [Overview](https://variations.sourceforge.net/vat/)
  A tool for marking a recording into nested, labelled bubbles and playing from any of them.
  It isolates form as a hierarchy of spans, drawn by the student over the sound.
- **Dezrann** (web platform, Algomus group, Lille). [dezrann.net](https://www.dezrann.net/)
  Scores and waveforms that can be annotated with labelled blocks for sections, themes and cadences.
  It isolates the analysis as a layer of coloured labels over the music.
- **The Shape of Song** (web visualisation, Martin Wattenberg, 2001). [bewitched.com/song.html](https://www.bewitched.com/song.html)
  Arc diagrams of repeats in pieces such as Für Elise and the Goldberg Variations.
  It makes rondo and binary repeats visible without any labels.
- **Music Animation Machine** (animated scores, Stephen Malinowski, 1985 onward). [musanim.com](https://www.musanim.com/), [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@smalin)
  Scrolling bar-graph scores of classical works, coloured by voice or by harmony.
  It lets a returning theme be seen as a returning shape.
- **San Francisco Symphony "Keeping Score"** (interactive site and television series, 2006 onward). [keepingscore.org](https://www.keepingscore.org/)
  Interactive scores and timelines for single works such as the Eroica.
  It isolates the listener's place in a long movement.
- **"What Is Sonata Form?"** (televised Young People's Concert, Leonard Bernstein, 1964). [Script at leonardbernstein.com](https://leonardbernstein.com/lectures/television-scripts/young-peoples-concerts/what-is-sonata-form)
  Bernstein builds sonata form up from a three-part song, at the piano and with the orchestra.
  It isolates the balance of departure and return, argued from pop songs.
- **Richard Atkinson** (YouTube channel). [youtube.com/@RichardAtkinson](https://www.youtube.com/@RichardAtkinson)
  Colour-annotated scrolling scores that label themes, sections and contrapuntal devices in Mozart, Haydn and Beethoven.
  It isolates what each bar is doing in the form; no single video title was verified.
- **P. D. Q. Bach's sportscast of Beethoven's Fifth** (comedy recording, Peter Schickele, 1967). [Wikipedia: P. D. Q. Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._D._Q._Bach)
  Two commentators call the first movement as a game, announcing the recapitulation as it arrives.
  It isolates formal events as things that happen in real time and can be called.
- **Sonata Theory and the sentence** (analytical methods, Hepokoski and Darcy; Schoenberg and Caplin). [Wikipedia: Sonata theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonata_theory), [Wikipedia: Sentence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentence_(music))
  Form is treated as a set of expected stations, or as a phrase of idea, repeat and continuation.
  The matching exercise is to write an eight-bar sentence or period from a given two-bar idea.

### Texture and melody/phrasing (tier 3)

Texture is shown best by things that give each layer a body (a character, a card, a coloured line) that can be added and removed.

- **Omnichord** (hardware instrument, Suzuki, 1981). [Wikipedia: Omnichord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnichord)
  Chord buttons, a strum plate and a rhythm box in one case.
  It isolates three layers of a pop texture (chords, a strummed melodic layer, a beat), each with its own surface.
- **Auto-accompaniment "styles"** (hardware feature, home keyboards). [Wikipedia: Electronic keyboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_keyboard)
  The left hand names a chord and the keyboard plays it as drums, bass, chords and pads that can each be switched off.
  It isolates the standard layers of a band as parts with their own buttons.
- **Tenori-on** (hardware instrument, Toshio Iwai and Yamaha, 2007). [Wikipedia: Tenori-on](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenori-on)
  A 16 by 16 grid of lit buttons with sixteen layers, each with its own mode of making notes.
  It isolates layers as sheets that are flipped through and stacked.
- **DropMix** (card game, Harmonix and Hasbro, 2017). [Wikipedia: DropMix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DropMix)
  Cards that each hold one stem of a known song (drums, bass, harmony, lead) are laid on a board and mixed in key and tempo.
  It isolates the layers of a pop texture as coloured cards in four slots.
- **Incredibox** (web and mobile app, So Far So Good, 2009). [incredibox.com](https://www.incredibox.com/)
  Sound icons are dragged onto seven beatboxers, each of whom then loops a beat, effect, melody or voice.
  It isolates texture as who is singing, built and thinned by dragging.
- **Blob Opera** (web experiment, David Li with Google Arts and Culture, 2020). [Blob Opera](https://artsandculture.google.com/experiment/blob-opera/AAHWrq360NcGbw)
  Four singing blobs, and dragging one makes the other three harmonise with it.
  It isolates homophony: one led line and three that follow.
- **Fugue Machine** (iOS app, Alexandernaut, 2015). [alexandernaut.com/fuguemachine](https://alexandernaut.com/fuguemachine/)
  One melody read by several playheads at once.
  It isolates polyphony made from a single line, as in a canon.
- **Music Animation Machine** (animated scores, Stephen Malinowski). [musanim.com](https://www.musanim.com/)
  Each voice is a colour and each note a bar, so a fugue's entries are seen arriving.
  It isolates texture for the eye, with no notation to read.
- **Chrome Music Lab: Song Maker** (web experiment, Google, 2018). [Song Maker](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/Song-Maker/)
  A melody grid above a two-row drum grid.
  It isolates the split between a melodic layer and a beat layer in the simplest possible way.
- **Patatap and Typatone** (web toys, Jono Brandel and Lullatone, 2014 and 2015). [patatap.com](https://patatap.com/), [typatone.com](https://typatone.com/)
  Each key on the keyboard is a sound with a shape, or each letter typed is a note.
  They isolate monophony as a stream of single events, each with its own sight.
- **Moises** (app, stem separation, 2019). [moises.ai](https://moises.ai/)
  A recording is split into voice, drums, bass and the rest, each with a fader.
  It isolates the layers of an existing record after the fact.
- **Travis picking and the Alberti bass** (techniques, guitar and piano). [Wikipedia: Travis picking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_picking), [Wikipedia: Alberti bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberti_bass)
  The thumb or left hand keeps a broken-chord pattern while the other fingers carry a tune.
  They isolate melody and accompaniment within one player's hands.
- **Hocket** (technique, medieval to Dirty Projectors). [Wikipedia: Hocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hocket)
  One melody is shared note by note between two or more performers.
  It isolates the difference between a line and the voices that carry it.

### Dynamics and articulation (tier 3)

The instruments here each add one more thing a finger can do after the note has started, and the older ones show how long the idea has been around.

- **Fortepiano** (instrument, Bartolomeo Cristofori, c. 1700). [Wikipedia: Fortepiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortepiano)
  The keyboard whose hammers let the force of a key set the loudness of a note.
  It is the origin of velocity as a played dimension.
- **Clavichord Bebung** (instrument technique, 18th century). [Wikipedia: Bebung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bebung)
  Rocking the finger on a held clavichord key bends the note, because the tangent stays on the string.
  It isolates pressure after the attack, two centuries before aftertouch.
- **Ondes Martenot touche d'intensité** (instrument, Maurice Martenot, 1928). [Wikipedia: Ondes Martenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ondes_Martenot)
  The right hand chooses the pitch and the left presses a glass key that alone lets sound through.
  It separates articulation and loudness from pitch, one hand each.
- **Roland TB-303 accent and slide** (hardware feature, Roland, 1981). [Wikipedia: Roland TB-303](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_TB-303)
  Two flags on a step: one makes it louder and brighter, the other glides into the next.
  It reduces dynamics and articulation to two bits per note, which is the whole sound of acid.
- **MPC 16 Levels** (hardware feature, Akai). [Wikipedia: Akai MPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akai_MPC)
  One sound is spread over the sixteen pads at sixteen fixed velocities.
  It makes dynamics a place under the finger instead of a force.
- **LinnStrument** (hardware controller, Roger Linn, 2014). [rogerlinndesign.com](https://www.rogerlinndesign.com/linnstrument)
  A grid of note pads, each sensing strike, pressure, left to right, front to back and release.
  It isolates per-note expression in three directions on a flat grid laid out like strings.
- **ROLI Seaboard** (hardware controller, ROLI, 2013). [roli.com](https://roli.com/)
  A piano keyboard made as one continuous soft surface that reads strike, press, glide, slide and lift.
  It keeps the piano's layout and adds what happens after the strike.
- **Expressive E Osmose** (hardware synth, Expressive E with Haken Audio, 2022). [expressivee.com](https://www.expressivee.com/2-osmose)
  A keyboard of ordinary-looking keys that move sideways and sense their whole travel.
  It isolates the first millimetres of a key press, so a note can be bowed into or struck.
- **Haken Continuum Fingerboard** (hardware instrument, Lippold Haken, 1999). [hakenaudio.com](https://www.hakenaudio.com/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuum_Fingerboard)
  A fretless neoprene surface read in three dimensions for each finger.
  It makes loudness and timbre continuous under every finger.
- **TouchKeys** (keyboard sensor kit, Andrew McPherson, 2013). [Augmented Instruments Lab](http://instrumentslab.org/project/touchkeys)
  Touch sensors laid on ordinary piano keys that read where the finger is on each key.
  It is the nearest prior art to sliding along a key to play an effect.
- **MIDI Polyphonic Expression** (specification, MIDI Association, 2018). [Wikipedia: MPE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIDI_Polyphonic_Expression)
  A way of giving every note its own channel so each can bend, swell and brighten apart from the others.
  It is the data model all of the instruments above send.
- **Wind and breath controllers** (hardware, Yamaha and Akai, 1970s onward). [Wikipedia: Wind controller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_controller)
  A mouthpiece that turns breath pressure into loudness and brightness.
  It moves dynamics from the fingers to the breath.
- **Moeller method and accent-tap exercises** (technique, drums). [Wikipedia: Moeller method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moeller_method)
  A whipping motion that gives one loud stroke and several soft ones from a single movement.
  It isolates accent as a height of the stick.
- **Ghost notes and palm muting** (techniques, drums, bass and guitar). [Wikipedia: Ghost note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghost_note), [Wikipedia: Palm mute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_mute)
  Notes played almost silently between the main ones, or damped with the edge of the hand.
  They isolate a second, quieter layer of dynamics inside one part.

### Orchestration and timbre as a played dimension (tier 3)

Timbre becomes playable when it is put on its own control (a drawbar, a joystick, a knob called "timbre"), and it is explained best by interactive pages on waves and harmonics.

- **Hammond drawbars** (instrument feature, Laurens Hammond, 1935). [Wikipedia: Hammond organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_organ)
  Nine sliders set the level of nine harmonics, and players move them while they play.
  It isolates the harmonic recipe of a tone as nine things under the left hand.
- **Ondes Martenot diffuseurs** (instrument feature, Maurice Martenot). [Wikipedia: Ondes Martenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ondes_Martenot)
  The player routes the sound to different speakers, one with a gong for a cone and one strung with sympathetic strings.
  It makes timbre a choice of resonating body, switched from a drawer of keys.
- **Mellotron** (instrument, Bradley brothers, 1963). [Wikipedia: Mellotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellotron)
  Each key plays a strip of tape of a real flute, string section or choir.
  It isolates orchestration as a selector switch on a keyboard.
- **Buchla touch plates and the Music Easel** (instrument, Don Buchla, 1973). [buchla.com](https://buchla.com/product/music-easel/), [Wikipedia: Buchla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchla_Electronic_Musical_Instruments)
  A synth played from pressure-sensitive metal plates, with an oscillator whose "timbre" slider folds the wave.
  It names timbre as a control and puts it under finger pressure.
- **Prophet VS and Wavestation vector joystick** (hardware feature, Sequential 1986 and Korg 1990). [Wikipedia: Vector synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vector_synthesis)
  A joystick crossfades between four sound sources at the corners.
  It isolates timbre as a position in a plane, which is the chord joystick's cousin.
- **Mutable Instruments Plaits** (Eurorack module, Émilie Gillet, 2018). [Plaits documentation](https://pichenettes.github.io/mutable-instruments-documentation/modules/plaits/)
  Sixteen synthesis models that all answer to the same three knobs: harmonics, timbre and morph.
  It isolates timbre as three named dimensions that mean something in every model.
- **NSynth Super** (open hardware, Google Magenta and Creative Lab, 2018). [nsynthsuper.withgoogle.com](https://nsynthsuper.withgoogle.com/)
  A touch pad with a different instrument at each corner and machine-learned blends between.
  It isolates timbre as a place between four known instruments.
- **Ableton Learning Synths** (web explainer, Ableton, 2019). [learningsynths.ableton.com](https://learningsynths.ableton.com/)
  A synth in the browser taught one control at a time, each on a pad the reader drags while it plays.
  It isolates each part of a synth's tone (wave, filter, envelope, LFO) in turn.
- **Syntorial** (training software, Joe Hanley, 2013). [syntorial.com](https://www.syntorial.com/)
  The learner hears a patch and must rebuild it by ear on a synth with hidden settings.
  It isolates timbre as ear training.
- **"Sound"** (interactive essay, Bartosz Ciechanowski, 2022). [ciechanow.ski/sound](https://ciechanow.ski/sound/)
  A long essay of draggable simulations from air molecules to waveforms and harmonics.
  It isolates what a waveform's shape does to what is heard.
- **Chrome Music Lab: Harmonics and Spectrogram** (web experiments, Google, 2016). [Harmonics](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/Harmonics/), [Spectrogram](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/Spectrogram/)
  One shows a string's overtones and the other draws the spectrum of instruments or the microphone.
  They isolate timbre as a picture of which overtones are present.
- **The Infinite Drum Machine** (web experiment, Google, 2016). [Experiments with Google](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/drum-machine)
  Thousands of sounds arranged by a machine so that similar ones sit together.

- **Philharmonia Orchestra instrument guides** (web resource and videos). [philharmonia.co.uk](https://philharmonia.co.uk/resources/instruments/)
  Players of each orchestral instrument show its range, techniques and colours.

- **Boléro, The Young Person's Guide and Klangfarbenmelodie** (compositions and technique). [Wikipedia: Boléro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bol%C3%A9ro), [Wikipedia: Young Person's Guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Young_Person%27s_Guide_to_the_Orchestra), [Wikipedia: Klangfarbenmelodie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klangfarbenmelodie)
  Pieces that hold the tune still and change only who plays it, and the technique of passing one line between instruments.

- **Talk box** (effect, 1970s). [Wikipedia: Talk box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk_box)
  An instrument's sound is piped into the mouth, which shapes it as it would shape speech.
  It makes the mouth the timbre control, the same idea as a vocoder played live.

## Post-tonal theory, notation and the ear

### Pitch-class sets: normal order, prime form, set class, interval-class vector (tier 3/4)

The prior art is almost all calculators: type or click a set, read off its normal order, prime form, Forte number and vector.
Very little of it is played; the nearest thing to hardware is the twelve-button scale mask on a quantizer.

- **Intellijel Scales** (eurorack quantizer module, Intellijel). [intellijel.com/shop/eurorack/scales](https://intellijel.com/shop/eurorack/scales/)
  A pitch quantizer whose panel is one octave of twelve lit buttons that are switched on and off.
  It isolates the pitch-class set as a mask over the twelve notes: the fingers choose the set, and any voltage is forced into it in every octave.
- **Set Theory Calculator** (web tool, Jay Tomlin). [jaytomlin.com set theory help](https://www.jaytomlin.com/music/settheory/help.html)
  A long-standing web calculator for normal form, prime form, Forte number, interval vector, transpositions and inversions.
  It isolates the chain from a handful of notes to their set class, with each step of the reduction shown as its own line.
- **Set Theory Calculator for iOS** (app, Jay Tomlin; listed earlier as "Post-Tonal Theory Calculator"). [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/set-theory-calculator/id791334943)
  The same calculator as a phone app, with a set matrix and a written overview of set theory.
  It isolates set-class lookup as a pocket reference, entered by tapping pitch classes.
- **PC Set Calculator** (web tool, hosted at Mount Allison University). [mta.ca PC Set Calculator](https://mta.ca/pc-set/calculator/pc_calculate.html)
  A calculator page that takes a set and returns its forms, vector and related sets.
  It isolates the set-class table itself, which the page lets you walk from one set to its complement and subsets.
- **music21, User's Guide chapter 25: Post-Tonal Tools** (Python library feature, Michael Cuthbert and MIT). [music21 post-tonal tools](https://www.music21.org/music21docs/usersGuide/usersGuide_25_postTonalTools1.html)
  `Chord` objects answer `primeForm`, `normalOrder`, `forteClass` and `intervalVector`, and a score can be labelled with them.
  It isolates set-class identity as a property of any chord in real repertoire, so the analysis is run over a whole piece instead of one set.
- **ChordGeometries** (desktop program, Dmitri Tymoczko). [ChordGeometries download page](https://dmitri.mycpanel.princeton.edu/ChordGeometries.html)
  A free program that draws chords as points in the voice-leading spaces of "A Geometry of Music" as they are played from a MIDI keyboard.
  It isolates the distance between sets: the eye sees a chord move through a space whose shape is set by the equivalences of set theory.
- **Pitch-Class Set Orders and Forms** (interactive demonstration, Wolfram Demonstrations Project). [demonstrations.wolfram.com](https://demonstrations.wolfram.com/PitchClassSetOrdersAndForms/)
  A small interactive that takes a set and shows its normal order and prime form.
  It isolates the two orderings side by side, changed with sliders and checkboxes.
- **Harmonious** (web reference). [harmonious.nfshost.com](https://harmonious.nfshost.com/)
  A cross-linked site of chords and scales treated as pitch-class sets, with a glossary entry for each term.
  It isolates the idea that a chord and a scale are the same kind of object, by giving both the same kind of page.
- **List of set classes** (reference table, Wikipedia). [List of set classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_set_classes)
  The full Forte table with prime forms, vectors and Z-relations.
  It isolates the catalogue: every possible set class on one page, which is what the calculators look up.
- **12tone on set theory** (YouTube channel; specific video not confirmed). (link not verified)
  The channel draws theory explanations by hand in real time.
  No title is given here because none could be confirmed.
- **The clock-face method** (classroom technique).
  Students mark a set on a twelve-hour clock, find the largest gap, and read the normal order clockwise from the far side of it.
  It isolates normal order as something seen, since the most compact span is visible as the shortest arc.

### Collections and equal divisions of the octave (tier 3)

Prior art here is strongest in keyboards whose geometry is an equal division, and in scale atlases that list every collection.
Verified videos were not found in this pass.

- **Jankó keyboard** (piano keyboard layout, Paul von Jankó, 1882). [Jankó keyboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jank%C3%B3_keyboard)
  A piano keyboard of several terraced rows in which each row is a whole-tone scale and neighbouring rows are a semitone apart.
  It isolates the two whole-tone collections: the hand feels each one as a single flat row, and every key has the same fingering.
- **Chromatic button accordion** (instrument, C-system and B-system). [Chromatic button accordion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatic_button_accordion)
  A button field in which each line of buttons rises by minor thirds and the three basic lines are a semitone apart.
  It isolates the three diminished-seventh chords that divide the octave in four, each of which lies under the hand as one straight line.
- **Scale Navigator** (web app and instrument, Nathan Turczan, after Dmitri Tymoczko's scale network). [scalenavigator.com](https://www.scalenavigator.com/)
  A map of scales joined when they differ by one note, including the whole-tone, octatonic and hexatonic collections beside the diatonic and acoustic ones.
  It isolates nearness between collections: the eye sees which scale is one semitone shift away and the hand steps to it.
- **Messiaen's Modes of Limited Transposition** (web experiment, Jackson Hardaker). [jacksonhardaker.github.io/Messiaen](https://jacksonhardaker.github.io/Messiaen/)
  An interactive graphic of the seven modes and their transpositions.
  It isolates limited transposition itself: turning a mode shows it landing on its own notes before twelve steps are up.
- **A Study of Scales** (web reference, Ian Ring). [ianring.com/musictheory/scales](https://ianring.com/musictheory/scales/)
  Every scale in twelve-tone equal temperament numbered as a twelve-bit mask, each with its own page of symmetry, modes, interval content and neighbours.
  It isolates a collection's symmetry: each page draws the scale on a circle and says under which rotations and reflections it maps onto itself.
- **All The Scales** (web reference). [allthescales.org](https://allthescales.org/)
  A site that enumerates the possible scales of the twelve notes and groups them into families of modes.
  It isolates the count: the whole space of collections is laid out as a finite list.
- **Thesaurus of Scales and Melodic Patterns** (book of exercises, Nicolas Slonimsky, 1947). [Thesaurus of Scales and Melodic Patterns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesaurus_of_Scales_and_Melodic_Patterns)
  Hundreds of patterns built by dividing one or more octaves into equal parts and decorating each division.
  It isolates the equal divisions (tritone, major third, minor third, whole tone) as the skeleton of a practice pattern, which is how many jazz players met them.
- **Coltrane changes** (harmonic technique, John Coltrane, 1959). [Coltrane changes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coltrane_changes)
  A chord substitution cycle that moves the key centre by major thirds, as in "Giant Steps".
  It isolates the augmented-triad axis as a progression that is practised in all its rotations.
- **Diminished and whole-tone pattern practice** (piano and guitar exercise).
  Players learn that there are only three diminished scales and two whole-tone scales, and drill one fingering for each.
  It isolates limited transposition in the hand: after the third diminished fingering there is nothing new to learn.

### Twelve-tone music: rows, matrices, row operations, combinatoriality (tier 4)

The usual embodiment is a matrix generator: type a row, get the 12 by 12 square.
Instruments that play a row as a row are rare, and the famous explainer is a video.

- **Eventide Misha** (eurorack instrument and sequencer, Eventide, 2022). [Eventide Misha](https://www.eventideaudio.com/rackmount/misha/) and [Sound On Sound news](https://www.soundonsound.com/news/eventide-misha-now-available)
  A module played by interval buttons (stay, or up or down by one to four scale steps) with a sequencer built on tone rows.
  It isolates the row as an order of notes in which each must be used before any repeats, and the hand plays moves instead of pitches.
- **RCA Mark II Sound Synthesizer** (studio synthesizer, RCA, 1957). [RCA Mark II Sound Synthesizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCA_Mark_II_Sound_Synthesizer)
  A room-sized synthesizer programmed by punched paper tape, used by Milton Babbitt for his serial electronic works.
  It isolates total specification: each note's pitch, duration and loudness is punched as a number, which suited music composed as ordered series.
- **musictheory.net Matrix Calculator** (web tool, musictheory.net). [musictheory.net/calculators/matrix](https://www.musictheory.net/calculators/matrix)
  A page that takes twelve notes and fills in the matrix of prime, inversion, retrograde and retrograde-inversion forms.
  It isolates the four row operations as the four directions of reading one square.
- **Composer Tools matrix calculator** (web tool). (link not verified; the composertools.com domain now redirects to a parking page)
  A matrix generator that was widely linked from theory courses.
  It isolated the same square, with the row forms labelled along the edges.
- **music21 `serial` module** (Python library feature). [music21.serial reference](https://www.music21.org/music21docs/moduleReference/moduleSerial.html)
  Tone-row objects with transformations, matrices and a catalogue of historical rows from Schoenberg, Berg and Webern.
  It isolates the row as data: real rows from the repertoire can be called up by name and transformed.
- **Tropes** (hexachord system, Josef Matthias Hauer, 1921). [Trope (music)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trope_(music))
  Hauer's 44 pairs of complementary hexachords, a twelve-tone method that sorts unordered halves instead of ordering a row.
  It isolates the hexachord pair, the same object combinatoriality is about.
- **Combinatoriality** and **All-interval twelve-tone row** (reference articles, Wikipedia). [Combinatoriality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatoriality) and [All-interval twelve-tone row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-interval_twelve-tone_row)
  Articles with worked rows that show hexachords completing the aggregate and rows that contain every interval once.
  Each isolates one property a row can have beyond containing twelve notes.
- **Vi Hart, "Twelve Tones"** (YouTube video, Vi Hart, 2013; video link not verified). [Scientific American write-up](https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/roots-of-unity/twelve-tone-vi-hart/) and [NPR write-up](https://www.npr.org/sections/deceptivecadence/2013/07/11/200831752/schoenberg-doesnt-suck-serialism-demystified)
  A half-hour drawn video essay that builds twelve-tone rows from scratch and sets a nursery rhyme with them.
  It isolates the row as a rule against repetition, and shows the operations by drawing and flipping the row on paper.
- **Samuel Andreyev and David Bruce Composer on serial music** (YouTube channels; specific videos not confirmed). (link not verified)
  Both channels publish score-following analyses of twentieth-century works.
  No titles are given here because none could be confirmed.
- **Write a row, fill a matrix, compose a miniature** (classroom exercise).
  The standard assignment: choose an order of the twelve notes, complete the matrix by hand, and write a short piece from a few row forms.
  It isolates the bookkeeping of row forms, which is done with pencil and a grid.

### Tuning and temperament, microtonality (not an OMT chapter)

This is the richest subtopic for instruments: a long line of keyboards with more than twelve keys to the octave, and a recent crop of browser tools that retune anything.

- **Archicembalo** (harpsichord, Nicola Vicentino, 1555). [Archicembalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archicembalo)
  A harpsichord with two manuals and 36 keys to the octave, built to play in extended meantone tunings.
  It isolates the split key: a sharp and its neighbouring flat are different notes with different keys.
- **Fokker organ** (31-tone pipe organ, Adriaan Fokker, 1950). [Huygens-Fokker Foundation](https://www.huygens-fokker.org/instruments/fokkerorgan.html)
  A pipe organ in 31 equal divisions of the octave with a generalised keyboard, now in Amsterdam.
  It isolates 31-tone equal temperament as a place to play, on keys arranged so every key has the same shape under the hand.
- **Harry Partch's instruments** (built instruments, Harry Partch, 1930s to 1970s). [harrypartch.com/instruments](https://www.harrypartch.com/instruments)
  Adapted and invented instruments, among them the Chromelodeon and the Diamond Marimba, tuned to a 43-note just scale.
  The Diamond Marimba isolates the tonality diamond: its bars are the lattice of ratios, laid out in the shape of the theory.
- **Lumatone** (isomorphic keyboard, Lumatone Inc.). [lumatone.io](https://www.lumatone.io/)
  A keyboard of 280 hexagonal keys, each lit in any colour and mapped to any note of any tuning.
  It isolates the mapping from layout to tuning: the same hand shape is the same interval everywhere, in whatever division is loaded.
- **Tonal Plexus** (microtonal keyboard, H-Pi Instruments). [hpi.zentral.zone/tonalplexus](https://hpi.zentral.zone/tonalplexus)
  A keyboard with over two hundred small keys to the octave.
  It isolates fine pitch as position: neighbouring keys are a few cents apart, so a comma is a distance the finger travels.
- **The Kite Guitar** (guitar fretting, Kite Giedraitis). [kiteguitar.com](https://www.kiteguitar.com/)
  A guitar fretted for 41 equal divisions of the octave by using every other step, tuned in thirds.
  It isolates near-just thirds and sevenths on a fretboard a guitarist can still reach around.
- **Adjustable microtonal guitar** (guitar, Tolgahan Çoğulu, 2008). [Tolgahan Çoğulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolgahan_%C3%87o%C4%9Fulu)
  A classical guitar whose frets slide along channels under each string, later rebuilt with LEGO pieces.
  It isolates the fret as a movable thing, so the temperament is set string by string with the fingers.
- **Bohlen-Pierce scale and its instruments** (scale and built instruments). [Bohlen-Pierce scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohlen%E2%80%93Pierce_scale)
  A scale that divides the twelfth (3:1) into thirteen steps, for which clarinets and other instruments have been built.
  It isolates the octave itself by removing it: the interval that repeats is a twelfth.
- **Hayward Tuning Vine** (software interface, Robin Hayward). (link not verified)
  A playable lattice of just-intonation pitches drawn as coloured balls, one colour for each prime.
  It isolates the prime factors of a ratio as directions on the screen.
- **Monochord** (ancient instrument). [Monochord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monochord)
  One string over a movable bridge, with a ruler beneath.
  It isolates the ratio: an interval is a length the hand measures.
- **Scale Workshop** (web app, Sevish; later versions by Lumi Pakkanen and others). [sevish.com/scaleworkshop](https://sevish.com/scaleworkshop/) and [scaleworkshop.plainsound.org](https://scaleworkshop.plainsound.org/)
  A browser tool to type or generate a scale in cents or ratios, play it from the computer keyboard, and export it for synths.
  It isolates the scale as a list of numbers that can be heard the moment it is typed.
- **Leimma and Apotome** (web apps, Khyam Allami and Counterpoint, 2021). [isartum.net/leimma](https://isartum.net/leimma) and [isartum.net/apotome](https://isartum.net/apotome)
  Leimma builds and plays a tuning system in the browser, and Apotome makes generative music in it.
  They isolate the tuning as the first decision, made on a circle of pitches before any note is played, with maqam and other non-Western systems as presets.
- **Wilsonic** (iOS app and plug-in, Marcus Hobbs). [wilsonic.co](https://www.wilsonic.co/)
  A playable explorer of Erv Wilson's scale designs.
  It isolates the scale-generating structures (moments of symmetry, combination product sets) as diagrams that are played.
- **Scala** (desktop program and file format, Manuel Op de Coul). [huygens-fokker.org/scala](https://www.huygens-fokker.org/scala/)
  A program for building, analysing and comparing tunings, with an archive of several thousand scales.
  It isolates the tuning as a text file, which became the format most synths read.
- **MTS-ESP** (plug-in suite, ODDSound). [oddsound.com MTS-ESP](https://oddsound.com/mtsespsuite.php)
  A master plug-in that retunes every supporting synth in a session at once, and can change tuning while notes sound.
  It isolates tuning as a global, automatable setting, separate from the instruments.
- **Xenharmonic Wiki** (community wiki). [en.xen.wiki](https://en.xen.wiki/w/Main_Page) (the site answers 403 to automated checks; open it in a browser)
  A wiki with a page for nearly every equal division, temperament and comma.
  It isolates each tuning as an article, cross-linked by the intervals it approximates.
- **Terpstra Keyboard web app** (web experiment). (link not verified; the site's certificate failed)
  A hexagonal on-screen keyboard for any equal division, after Siemen Terpstra's design.
  It isolates the generalised layout in a browser.
- **minutephysics, "Why It's Impossible to Tune a Piano"** (YouTube video, minutephysics). [youtube.com/watch?v=1Hqm0dYKUx4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1Hqm0dYKUx4)
  A short drawn explanation of why pure intervals do not close into octaves.
  It isolates the comma: stacked ratios are multiplied out on screen until they miss.
- **3Blue1Brown, "Music And Measure Theory"** (YouTube video, 3Blue1Brown). [youtube.com/watch?v=cyW5z-M2yzw](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cyW5z-M2yzw)
  A video that asks which frequency ratios sound consonant and answers with rational approximation.
  It isolates how close a ratio must be to a simple fraction, drawn on the number line.
- **Adam Neely, Sevish and Zheanna Erose on microtonality** (YouTube channels; specific videos not confirmed). (link not verified)
  All three publish microtonal music and explanations; Sevish's music resources page is at [sevish.com/music-resources](https://sevish.com/music-resources/).
  No video titles are given here because none could be confirmed.
- **Tuning a guitar by harmonics** (guitar technique). [Guitar harmonics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar_harmonics)
  Matching the fifth-fret harmonic of one string to the seventh-fret harmonic of the next and listening for beats.
  It isolates the pure fourth, and players soon learn that tuning this way leaves the guitar out with itself, which is temperament met by hand.
- **Ringing chords in barbershop** (vocal technique). [Barbershop music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbershop_music)
  Quartets tune dominant sevenths to just ratios until an overtone rings above the chord.
  It isolates just intonation as a sensation the singers chase by ear.

### Staff notation: notes, clefs, ledger lines, grand staff, key signatures, beaming, rests (tier 4)

Prior art divides into engraving tools, which know every rule, and note-naming drills, which teach one.
The odd corner is hardware for writing notation.

- **Rastrum** (five-nibbed pen, historical). [Rastrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastrum)
  A pen with five points that draws a whole staff in one stroke.
  It isolates the staff itself as a single gesture of the hand.
- **ODLA** (hardware keyboard for notation, Kemonia River). [odlamusic.com](https://odlamusic.com/)
  A keyboard whose main feature is a raised five-line staff of keys, for entering notes into notation software by touch.
  It isolates staff position: the finger presses the line or space, which also makes notation reachable for blind users.
- **Keaton Music Typewriter** (typewriter, Robert Keaton, patented 1936). (link not verified)
  A typewriter with a circular keyboard that prints notes and symbols onto staff paper.
  It isolated the placing of a symbol at a height on the staff, set with a lever.
- **MuseScore** (notation program, open source). [musescore.org](https://musescore.org/en)
  A free score editor with automatic beaming, spacing and playback.
  It isolates engraving rules as defaults: beams, stems and rests come out right without being asked for.
- **Noteflight** and **Flat** (browser notation editors). [noteflight.com](https://www.noteflight.com/) and [flat.io](https://flat.io/)
  Score editors that run in a web page and are shared like documents.
  They isolate notation as something written together, which is why schools use them.
- **StaffPad** (handwriting notation app, StaffPad Ltd, 2015). [staffpad.net](https://www.staffpad.net/)
  A tablet app that reads notes written on a staff with a stylus and turns them into engraved, playable score.
  It isolates the pen stroke: the hand writes as on paper and the page tidies itself.
- **LilyPond** and **Verovio** (text-to-engraving systems). [lilypond.org](https://lilypond.org/) and [verovio.org](https://www.verovio.org/)
  LilyPond compiles a text file into an engraved score, and Verovio renders encoded scores in a browser.
  They isolate notation as the output of rules, with the layout decided by the program.
- **Dorico** (notation program, Steinberg). [steinberg.net/dorico](https://www.steinberg.net/dorico/)
  A score editor that stores notes as durations in time and works out beaming, ties and rests from the metre.
  It isolates the split between what a rhythm is and how the metre requires it to be written.
- **Tenuto and the musictheory.net exercises** (app and web drills, musictheory.net). [Tenuto](https://www.musictheory.net/products/tenuto) and [note identification exercise](https://www.musictheory.net/exercises/note)
  Timed drills for naming notes, key signatures and intervals on the staff, each with settings for clef and range.
  Each drill isolates one reading skill, answered by tapping a name or a piano key.
- **Note Rush** (app, Note Rush). [noterushapp.com](https://www.noterushapp.com/)
  A note-reading game that listens through the microphone while the learner plays each shown note on a real instrument.
  It isolates the link from a staff position to a key under the hand, with no screen keyboard in between.
- **Music Notation Training** (web drill). [notationtraining.com](https://www.notationtraining.com/)
  A browser flashcard trainer for notes on the staff, with clef and range settings.
  It isolates note naming, including ledger lines, as a speed drill.
- **Tantacrul's notation software reviews** (YouTube videos, Tantacrul). [Sibelius](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dKx1wnXClcI) and [MuseScore](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4hZxo96x48A)
  "Music Software & Bad Interface Design: Avid's Sibelius" and "Music Software & Interface Design: MuseScore", long critiques of how notation programs make the user enter notes.
  They isolate note entry as an interface problem, shown click by click.
- **Line and space mnemonics and landmark notes** (teaching technique).
  "Every Good Boy Does Fine" and "FACE" for the treble staff, or the landmark method of fixing a few notes and reading by interval from them.
  They isolate the first step of reading, turning a position into a letter.

### Alternative and graphic notation (tier 4)

There are two families: reformed staves that fix the traditional staff's uneven spacing, and time-on-one-axis pictures (rolls, falling notes, animated scores).
Tablature and shape notes are older answers to the same complaints.

- **Player piano roll** (paper roll, from the 1890s). [Piano roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano_roll)
  A paper roll in which a hole's position is its pitch and its length is its duration.
  It isolates the pitch-against-time grid, the picture every sequencer still uses.
- **Boomwhackers** (tuned plastic tubes, 1995). [Boomwhacker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boomwhacker)
  Tubes coloured by pitch, played from scores that use the same colours.
  They isolate colour as the name of a note, so reading is matching.
- **Klavarskribo** (notation system, Cornelis Pot, 1931). [klavarskribo.eu](https://klavarskribo.eu/en/)
  A vertical staff whose lines are the black keys of the piano, read downward, with time shown by distance.
  It isolates the keyboard as the staff: a note is drawn where the finger goes.
- **Hummingbird** (notation system, Blake West and Mike Sall). [hummingbirdnotation.com](https://www.hummingbirdnotation.com/)
  A redrawn staff notation in which each pitch has its own symbol shape and durations are drawn as lengths.
  It isolates redundancy: pitch is told by both position and shape.
- **Dodeka** (notation system and keyboard, Dodeka Music). [dodekamusic.com](https://dodekamusic.com/)
  A four-line chromatic staff with twelve equal positions per octave, no accidentals, and note length drawn as bar length.
  It isolates the chromatic scale as evenly spaced, so an interval always looks the same.
- **The Music Notation Project** (web catalogue of alternative notations). [musicnotation.org](https://musicnotation.org/)
  A site that collects and compares chromatic-staff notation systems against a set of criteria.
  It isolates the design space: dozens of staves that differ in one choice each.
- **Figurenotes** (notation system, Kaarlo Uusitalo and Markku Kaikkonen, Finland). [figurenotes.org](https://figurenotes.org/)
  Notes written as coloured shapes that match stickers on the instrument, with colour for the note and shape for the octave.
  It isolates matching: no symbol has to be decoded before it can be played.
- **Shape notes and the Sacred Harp** (notation and singing tradition, from 1801). [fasola.org](https://fasola.org/) and [Shape note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shape_note)
  Staff notation with a differently shaped notehead for each solfège syllable.
  It isolates scale degree: the shape says what the note is in the key, whatever the key.
- **Numbered notation (jianpu)** (notation system). [Numbered musical notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numbered_musical_notation)
  Scale degrees written as the digits 1 to 7, with dots for octaves and underlines for shorter values.
  It isolates the degree as a number, the same idea as Perfecto's degree keys.
- **Nashville Number System** (chart notation). [Nashville Number System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Number_System)
  Chord charts written as scale-degree numbers so a band can change key at once.
  It isolates the progression from the key.
- **Braille music** (notation system, Louis Braille). [Braille music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braille_music)
  Music written in the six-dot cell as a single line of signs for pitch, value and octave.
  It isolates notation with no vertical axis at all, read by the finger.
- **Songsterr** (interactive tablature player). [songsterr.com](https://www.songsterr.com/)
  Guitar tablature that scrolls and plays back in the browser.
  It isolates the where of a note (string and fret) over the what.
- **Rocksmith** (game, Ubisoft, 2011). [Rocksmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocksmith)
  A game played on a real guitar, with notes that fly toward the player along a pictured fretboard.
  It isolates timing and place on the neck as a moving target.
- **Synthesia** (program, Synthesia LLC). [synthesiagame.com](https://synthesiagame.com/)
  Coloured bars that fall onto a pictured piano keyboard and wait for the right key.
  It isolates which key and when, and is the source of the falling-notes video style.
- **Hooktheory TheoryTab** (web database and editor). [hooktheory.com/theorytab](https://www.hooktheory.com/theorytab)
  Songs shown as a piano roll in which melody notes are coloured by scale degree over Roman-numeral chord blocks.
  It isolates function: the same colour is the same degree in every song and key.
- **Chrome Music Lab: Song Maker and Kandinsky** (web experiments, Google Creative Lab). [Song Maker](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/Song-Maker/) and [Kandinsky](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/Kandinsky/)
  Song Maker is a coloured grid that plays, and Kandinsky turns drawn lines and shapes into sounds.
  Song Maker isolates the grid score, and Kandinsky the graphic score in which a drawing is the piece.
- **Music Animation Machine** (animated scores, Stephen Malinowski). [musanim.com](https://www.musanim.com/)
  Bar-graph and shape scores that scroll in time with a recording, with each voice in its own colour.
  It isolates voice and contour for a listener who does not read staff notation.
- **smalin, "Bach, Toccata and Fugue in D minor, organ"** and **musanim, "Stravinsky, Rite of Spring (complete), animated graphical score"** (YouTube videos, Stephen Malinowski). [Bach](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ipzR9bhei_o) and [Stravinsky](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5IXMpUhuBMs)
  Two of the best-known Music Animation Machine videos.
  They isolate texture: the fugue's entries and Stravinsky's layers are seen as colours.
- **"Ligeti - Artikulation"** (YouTube video, uploaded by Donald Craig). [youtube.com/watch?v=71hNl_skTZQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=71hNl_skTZQ)
  Rainer Wehinger's 1970 listening score for Ligeti's tape piece, scrolled in time with the music.
  It isolates the graphic score made after the fact, with a shape and colour for each kind of sound.
- **Tantacrul, "Notation Must Die: The Battle For How We Read Music"** (YouTube video, Tantacrul). [youtube.com/watch?v=Eq3bUFgEcb4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Eq3bUFgEcb4)
  A long survey of proposed replacements for staff notation and why they did not replace it.
  It isolates the comparison, and is the best single tour of this subtopic.
- **Cornelius Cardew, "Treatise"** (graphic score, 1963 to 1967). (link not verified)
  193 pages of lines, circles and symbols with no instructions on how to play them.
  It isolates interpretation: the performers decide what a mark means.

### Conducting patterns (tier 4)

Prior art is gesture controllers that let a baton or hand drive tempo and dynamics, and museum pieces that let visitors conduct a recorded orchestra.
Very little teaches the beat patterns themselves.

- **Radio Baton, or Radiodrum** (controller, Max Mathews and Bob Boie, 1980s). [Radiodrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiodrum)
  Two batons tracked in three dimensions over a sensing surface, used with Mathews's Conductor program to beat time for a stored score.
  It isolates the conductor's job as Mathews saw it: the score supplies the notes, and the hand supplies when and how loud.
- **Buchla Lightning** (controller, Don Buchla, 1991). [Buchla Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchla_Lightning)
  Two infrared wands whose position and motion in the air are turned into MIDI.
  It isolates gesture in free space, with a conducting mode among its presets.
- **Mi.Mu gloves** (gesture gloves, Imogen Heap and team). [mimugloves.com](https://mimugloves.com/) and [WIRED 2012 demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6btFObRRD9k)
  Sensor gloves that map hand posture and movement to sound and effects.
  They isolate the expressive hand, the left hand of a conductor more than the beat.
- **Personal Orchestra and the Virtual Conductor** (museum installation, Jan Borchers and others, House of Music Vienna, 2000). [Personal Orchestra at RWTH Aachen](https://hci.rwth-aachen.de/po) and [House of Music Vienna](https://www.hdm.at/en/)
  Visitors conduct a filmed Vienna Philharmonic with an infrared baton, and the video and audio follow their tempo.
  It isolates tempo and dynamics as the two things a baton controls, with a real orchestra's sound.
- **Conductor's Jacket** (wearable sensor research, Teresa Marrin Nakra, MIT Media Lab, late 1990s). (link not verified)
  A jacket of muscle, breathing and motion sensors worn by working conductors to record what their bodies do.
  It isolates the physiology of conducting as measured signals.
- **Semi-Conductor** (web experiment, Google Creative Lab, 2018). [experiments.withgoogle.com/semi-conductor](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/semi-conductor)
  A browser piece that watches the user's arms through the webcam and plays an orchestra of recorded fragments faster, slower, louder or softer.
  It isolates arm speed and height as tempo and loudness, with no hardware.
- **Wii Music, conductor mode** (game, Nintendo, 2008). [Wii Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wii_Music)
  A mode in which the remote is waved as a baton in front of an on-screen orchestra.
  It isolates tempo following as a game score.
- **Leonard Bernstein conducting with his face** (YouTube video of a Haydn encore with the Vienna Philharmonic; exact title not confirmed). (link not verified)
  Bernstein leads a movement with eyebrows and expression alone, hands at his sides.
  It isolates what is left of conducting when the beat pattern is taken away.
- **Dalcroze eurhythmics** (teaching method, Émile Jaques-Dalcroze). [Dalcroze eurhythmics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalcroze_eurhythmics)
  A method that teaches metre and phrase through whole-body movement.
  It isolates the beat as something stepped and swung before it is counted.
- **Beat patterns in the air** (conducting exercise). [Conducting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conducting)
  The standard patterns for two, three, four and six, practised with a metronome or recording.
  They isolate metre as a shape: the downbeat is down, the last beat is up.

### Sight-singing (tier 4)

Prior art is mostly systems for naming scale degrees so they can be sung: hands, syllables and shapes.
Software adds a microphone and marks the pitch.

- **Guidonian hand** (mnemonic, medieval, attributed to Guido of Arezzo). [Guidonian hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guidonian_hand)
  A map of the gamut onto the joints of the left hand, which a teacher points at to lead singers.
  It isolates the hexachord and its mutations as places on the body.
- **Curwen hand signs and Tonic sol-fa** (teaching system, Sarah Glover and John Curwen, 19th century). [Tonic sol-fa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonic_sol-fa)
  Movable-do syllables, each with a hand shape held at a height that matches the degree.
  It isolates the character of each scale degree as a gesture, shown before it is sung.
- **Kodály method** (teaching method, Zoltán Kodály and colleagues). [Kodály method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kod%C3%A1ly_method)
  A sequenced curriculum of movable-do solfège, hand signs, rhythm syllables and folk song.
  It isolates the order in which degrees are learned, starting from the falling minor third.
- **Shape-note singing** (tradition and notation). [fasola.org](https://fasola.org/)
  Singers sing through each tune on the syllables first, reading them off the note shapes, then on the words.
  It isolates reading degrees at sight as a social practice, with the syllable printed in the notehead.
- **Sight Reading Factory** (web app, GraceNotes LLC). [sightreadingfactory.com](https://www.sightreadingfactory.com/)
  An endless generator of new sight-reading and sight-singing examples at a chosen level, with recording for assignments.
  It isolates the first reading: no example is ever seen twice.
- **EarMaster sight-singing exercises** (program feature, EarMaster ApS). [earmaster.com](https://www.earmaster.com/)
  Exercises that show a melody, listen through the microphone and mark each sung note for pitch and rhythm.
  It isolates accuracy of the sung degree, with the pitch curve drawn against the notes.
- **Vocal Pitch Monitor** (app). [vocalpitchmonitor.com](https://www.vocalpitchmonitor.com/)
  A live graph of the pitch being sung against note lines.
  It isolates the voice's pitch as a line the singer can watch.
- **SingTrue** (iOS app, Easy Ear Training). (link not verified)
  A set of games that teach matching pitch and controlling the voice.
  It isolates pitch matching for people who think they cannot sing in tune.
- **Blob Opera** (web experiment, David Li with Google Arts and Culture, 2020). [Blob Opera](https://artsandculture.google.com/experiment/blob-opera/AAHWrq360NcGbw)
  Four singing blobs dragged up and down for pitch and forward and back for vowel, with the other three harmonising.
  It isolates the sung line as a drag gesture, with harmony supplied.
- **Pink Trombone** (web experiment, Neil Thapen, 2017). [dood.al/pinktrombone](https://dood.al/pinktrombone/)
  A cross-section of a vocal tract that speaks as the tongue and lips are dragged about.
  It isolates how the voice makes a vowel, the instrument under all sight-singing.
- **"Bobby McFerrin Demonstrates the Power of the Pentatonic Scale"** (YouTube video, World Science Festival, 2009). [youtube.com/watch?v=ne6tB2KiZuk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ne6tB2KiZuk)
  McFerrin jumps between spots on a stage and the audience sings the right note for each, including spots he has not taught.
  It isolates the scale as positions in space that a crowd can sight-sing from a body.
- **Singing over a drone** (vocal exercise).
  Singing scale degrees slowly against a held tonic and listening for each interval to settle.
  It isolates the tuning and feel of each degree against the tonic.

### Dictation and ear training (tier 4)

This is the most crowded field: dozens of trainers that play something and ask what it was.
The useful difference is between interval drills with no key and functional drills that establish a key first.

- **Theremin and Ondes Martenot** (instruments, Leon Theremin, 1920, and Maurice Martenot, 1928). [Ondes Martenot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ondes_Martenot)
  Instruments with continuous pitch, the theremin with nothing to touch and the ondes with a ring on a wire beside a dummy keyboard.
  They isolate the ear as the only guide to pitch, since no key or fret tells the player where a note is.
- **Functional Ear Trainer** (app, Alain Benbassat's method). [miles.be](https://www.miles.be/)
  A cadence sets a key, one note sounds, and the learner names its scale degree.
  It isolates the degree heard in a key, which is the opposite of naming bare intervals.
- **Tonedear** (web trainer). [tonedear.com](https://tonedear.com/) and [functional scale degrees](https://tonedear.com/ear-training/functional-solfege-scale-degrees)
  Free browser drills for intervals, chords, scales, progressions and functional degrees.
  Each page isolates one thing to listen for, answered by one click.
- **teoria** (web tutorials and exercises, José Rodríguez Alvira). [teoria.com exercises](https://www.teoria.com/en/exercises/)
  Ear and theory exercises with fine control, down to melodic and harmonic dictation.
  It isolates dictation in small steps, from two notes up to a phrase.
- **musictheory.net ear exercises** (web drills). [interval ear training](https://www.musictheory.net/exercises/ear-interval)
  Customisable drills for hearing intervals, scales and chords.
  They isolate one category at a time, with the set of possible answers chosen by the learner.
- **EarMaster** and **Auralia** (programs, EarMaster ApS and Rising Software). [earmaster.com](https://www.earmaster.com/) and [risingsoftware.com/auralia](https://www.risingsoftware.com/auralia)
  Full aural curricula used in schools, with dictation, progressions, rhythm tapping and sung answers.
  They isolate each aural skill as a graded course with records kept for a teacher.
- **Chord Crush** (web game, Hooktheory). [hooktheory.com/chord-crush](https://www.hooktheory.com/chord-crush)
  A trainer that plays clips of real songs and asks for the chords by Roman numeral.
  It isolates hearing a progression in recorded music instead of in piano tones.
- **ToneGym** (web games). [tonegym.co](https://www.tonegym.co/)
  A gym of daily ear games with levels and leaderboards.
  It isolates the habit, by making each skill a short scored game.
- **Meludia** (web method). [meludia.com](https://meludia.com/fr/)
  Listening exercises that start from sensations such as up or down, stable or unstable, dense or sparse, before any names.
  It isolates the perception underneath the theory term.
- **Theta Music Trainer** (web games). [trainer.thetamusic.com](https://trainer.thetamusic.com/)
  Small games for pitch, intervals, chords, rhythm and also for mixing and tone colour.
  It isolates a skill to a game, some of them (EQ, panning) outside what theory courses train.
- **Syntorial** (synthesis ear trainer, Audible Genius). [syntorial.com](https://www.syntorial.com/)
  A course that plays a synth patch and has the learner rebuild it by ear on a real synth panel.
  It isolates timbre as something to take dictation of, knob by knob.
- **Complete Ear Trainer** and **Perfect Ear** (mobile apps). [completeeartrainer.com](https://completeeartrainer.com/) and [perfectear.app](https://www.perfectear.app/)
  Phone ear trainers arranged as levels of interval, chord and scale drills.
  They isolate the daily drill in a pocket.
- **The Music Lab** (citizen-science listening tests, Samuel Mehr's lab). [themusiclab.org](https://www.themusiclab.org/)
  Online listening games, including a tone-deafness test, that also gather research data.
  It isolates what an untrained ear can do, measured on a large public sample.
- **Chrome Music Lab: Spectrogram** (web experiment, Google Creative Lab). [Spectrogram](https://musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/Spectrogram/)
  A live picture of the frequencies in a sound from the microphone or a set of samples.
  It isolates overtones, so the ear can check what it thinks it hears against a picture.
- **Rick Beato, "Why Adults Can't Develop Perfect Pitch"** (YouTube video, Rick Beato). [youtube.com/watch?v=816VLQNdPMM](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=816VLQNdPMM)
  A video on absolute pitch and the age at which it can be acquired.
  It isolates the line between absolute and relative pitch, which is what every trainer above works on.
- **Interval song mnemonics** (ear technique). [Interval recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interval_recognition)
  Remembering each interval by the opening of a known tune, such as "Here Comes the Bride" for a perfect fourth.
  It isolates the bare interval, and is criticised for exactly that, since the tune brings its own key.
- **Chet** (iOS ear-training app). (link not verified)
  An ear trainer in which heard melodies are played back on an on-screen instrument.
  It isolates call and response.

### Transcription (tier 4)

Prior art is slow-down players for doing it by ear, and machine listeners that try to do it for you.
The practice of transcribing a solo is the older and still central technique.

- **Transcribe!** (program, Seventh String Software). [seventhstring.com](https://www.seventhstring.com/)
  A player that slows audio without changing pitch, loops a selection, and shows a spectrum over a piano keyboard for the moment under the cursor.
  It isolates the held instant: one chord frozen and its likely notes drawn on a keyboard.
- **Amazing Slow Downer** (program, Roni Music). [ronimusic.com](https://www.ronimusic.com/)
  A player that changes speed and pitch independently and loops.
  It isolates tempo as a dial, which is the whole of what many transcribers need.
- **Capo** (Mac and iOS app, SuperMegaUltraGroovy). [Capo](https://supermegaultragroovy.com/products/capo/)
  A practice app that slows a song, guesses its chords and beats, and lets tablature be drawn onto a spectrogram.
  It isolates the spectrogram as the page being transcribed from.
- **Soundslice** (web player and editor, Soundslice LLC). [soundslice.com](https://www.soundslice.com/)
  Notation and tablature synchronised to a real recording or video, with looping and slowing.
  It isolates the join between a finished transcription and its source, bar by bar.
- **Melodyne, Direct Note Access** (program feature, Celemony). [celemony.com Melodyne](https://www.celemony.com/en/melodyne/what-is-melodyne)
  An editor that shows the separate notes inside recorded audio, including chords, as blobs that can be moved.
  It isolates the note inside the recording, which can be read off or changed.
- **Moises** (app, Moises Systems). [moises.ai](https://moises.ai/)
  Stem separation that pulls vocals, bass, drums and other parts out of a mixed track, with chord and key detection.
  It isolates one instrument from the mix, the first step of transcribing a part.
- **AnthemScore** (program, Lunaverus). [lunaverus.com](https://lunaverus.com/)
  A program that turns audio into sheet music with a neural network and lets the guess be corrected against a spectrogram.
  It isolates the machine's first draft and the human's correction of it.
- **Basic Pitch** (web demo and open-source model, Spotify, 2022). [basicpitch.spotify.com](https://basicpitch.spotify.com/)
  A browser tool that turns a recording of one instrument into MIDI, with pitch bends.
  It isolates audio to notes as a drag-and-drop step.
- **ScoreCloud** (program, DoReMIR). [scorecloud.com](https://scorecloud.com/)
  Notation software that writes down what is played or sung into it.
  It isolates dictation of oneself, with performance in and score out.
- **Chordify** (web app). [chordify.net](https://chordify.net/) (the site answers 403 to automated checks; open it in a browser)
  Chord charts worked out automatically from any song and scrolled in time with it.
  It isolates the chord layer of a recording.
- **dancohen, "Animated Sheet Music: 'Giant Steps' by John Coltrane"** (YouTube video, Dan Cohen). [youtube.com/watch?v=2kotK9FNEYU](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2kotK9FNEYU)
  A transcription of Coltrane's solo that draws itself in time with the recording.
  It isolates the finished transcription as a performance to watch, and began a genre of such videos.
- **Charlie Parker Omnibook** (book of transcriptions, 1978). [Charlie Parker Omnibook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Parker_Omnibook)
  Sixty Parker solos written out, published in several transpositions.
  It isolates the transcribed solo as study material for players who did not transcribe it.
- **Parsons code** (melody notation for lookup, Denys Parsons, 1975). [Parsons code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parsons_code)
  A melody written only as up, down or repeat for each note.
  It isolates contour, the least a transcription can say and still identify a tune.
- **Transcribe a solo** (practice technique).
  Learning a recorded solo by ear, singing it, playing it and only then writing it down.
  It isolates the path from ear to hand, which jazz teaching treats as the main way a style is learned.

### Stylistic periods and music history as an explorable thing (tier 4)

Prior art is maps and time machines for recorded popular music.
Explorable timelines of the classical style periods that OMT means were not found in this pass, and this subtopic is the thinnest.

- **Every Noise at Once** (web map, Glenn McDonald, from 2013). [everynoise.com](https://everynoise.com/)
  A scatter of several thousand genre names, each of which plays a sample when clicked and opens its own map of artists.
  It isolates genre as a position in a space: the eye reads the axes from dense to spiky and from organic to mechanical.
- **Radiooooo** (web and app "musical time machine", from 2013). [radiooooo.com](https://app.radiooooo.com/)
  A world map and a row of decades: choose a country and a decade and hear music from there and then.
  It isolates place and period as the two dials of a radio.
- **Musicmap** (web genealogy, Kwinten Crauwels, 2016). [musicmap.info](https://musicmap.info/)
  A zoomable chart of more than 230 popular genres by date, with the lines of influence between them.
  It isolates descent: each genre is placed on a time axis with its parents.
- **Ishkur's Guide to Electronic Music** (web guide, Ishkur, 2000, rebuilt 2019). [music.ishkur.com](https://music.ishkur.com/)
  A scrolling time chart of electronic genres with opinionated notes and audio for each.
  It isolates one family tree in depth, with examples to hear at every node.
- **Map of Metal** (web map). [mapofmetal.com](https://mapofmetal.com/)
  An illustrated map of metal subgenres with a playlist for each region.
  It isolates genre as geography.
- **The Pudding, music history of the Billboard charts** (web piece, The Pudding, 2017). [pudding.cool/2017/03/music-history](https://pudding.cool/2017/03/music-history/)
  An animated run through the top of the Billboard chart over the decades, with the leading song playing as it changes.
  It isolates the passing of time in popular style as something heard continuously.
- **Citizen DJ** (web tool, Brian Foo, Library of Congress, 2020). [citizen-dj.labs.loc.gov](https://citizen-dj.labs.loc.gov/)
  A browser sampler built on free-to-use historical recordings from the Library's collections.
  It isolates the archive as raw material, explored by cutting it up.
- **Pentatonix, "Evolution of Music"** (YouTube video, Pentatonix, 2013). [youtube.com/watch?v=lExW80sXsHs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lExW80sXsHs)
  A four-minute a cappella medley that runs from chant to current pop in order.
  It isolates period style by keeping the performers fixed while the era changes.
- **Postmodern Jukebox** (band and video series, Scott Bradlee). [postmodernjukebox.com](https://postmodernjukebox.com/)
  Current pop songs rearranged in the styles of earlier decades.
  It isolates style from song: the tune is held still and the period is changed.
- **Musikalisches Würfelspiel** (musical dice games, 18th century). [Musikalisches Würfelspiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musikalisches_W%C3%BCrfelspiel)
  Tables of pre-written bars from which dice choose a minuet or waltz.
  It isolates a period's style as a set of interchangeable parts, which is why any throw sounds Classical.
- **Pastiche composition** (classroom technique).
  Writing a short piece in the manner of a given composer or period.
  It isolates style as a set of constraints the student has to find and obey.
